150	Life on other planets
151	Question
152	Is there any kind of life on other planets? (not necessarily intelligent life)
153	Life may seem to be something ordinary on our planet. In reality, however, a great number of cosmological parameters must lie within specific numerical limits for life to be possible. This is exactly what holds for the Earth. If even one of all these parameters had a slightly different value, there would be no life on our planet.
154	By 2009, 322 cosmological parameters had been found that lie within very narrowly defined numerical limits so as to favour life on Earth, and new ones are constantly being discovered. The probability of all of them occurring together by chance has been calculated at 1 in 10<sup>304</sup>! If we consider that the number of all the planets in the universe is estimated to be only of the order of 10<sup>22</sup>, we realise that, normally, there should not be a single planet in the universe with life on it. The existence of life on Earth is therefore a miracle.
155	On the basis of these data we can conclude that it is mathematically and physically impossible for any form of life to exist on another planet.
156	You can read more in our article on the <a href="material/2008_06_05-19.htm">anthropic principle</a>.
157	Similarity between ape and human DNA
158	How can it be explained that human DNA is 96%–98% similar to the DNA of various <br> species of ape, if the theory of evolution is not true?
159	First of all, the figure of 96%–98% is inaccurate. There are two main reasons for this:
160	because the DNA of the chimpanzee, for example, is 12% larger than human DNA, so the similarity should be expressed in terms of the genes being compared each time, not in terms of the whole genome.
161	because the percentage of genetic similarity varies from study to study, depending on the part of the genome examined, the amount of DNA used and the computational techniques applied.
162	So the results of the various studies actually give a range of similarity between 87% and 96%.
163	We should also know that a difference in DNA of even 1% corresponds to about 30 million “chemical letters” (nitrogenous bases). And if a change (mutation) in a single “chemical letter” can bring about the dramatic changes we see in genetic diseases, how great a difference can 30 million chemical letters make from one organism to another, so that when we compare man with the chimpanzee we are speaking of a different species! (simply with some similarities).
164	Moreover, genetic similarity does not seem to lead to clear evolutionary conclusions, since there are species that are very similar in form but have little genetic similarity to each other, and that are genetically more similar to other species which they resemble less in form.
165	Finally, similarity in the DNA code does not necessarily mean similarity in the information it carries.
166	You can read more details in our <a href="material/2009_01_29.htm#Genetic_Similarity">article on this</a>.

170	Life from outer space – panspermia
171	Could life on Earth have come from micro-organisms that originated <br> in space, reached the Earth on meteorites and then evolved?
172	Regarding this theory (panspermia), the finding its supporters mostly refer to is the <a href="files/Katagomaste_apo_to_Diastima_in.gr.pdf">Murchison meteorite</a>, which fell in Australia in 1969 and was found to contain certain amino acids (the building blocks of proteins) and certain nucleobases (the building blocks of DNA and RNA), which even contain the rare isotope carbon-13, thought to be of non-terrestrial origin.
173	The theory of panspermia, however, does not solve the problem of <a href="material/2008_10_23.htm">abiogenesis</a>; it simply moves it to another environment. This counts rather against the theory: if it is impossible to explain the spontaneous appearance of life on a planet as <a href="material/2008_06_05-19.htm">suitable</a> as the Earth, how much harder would it be for this to have happened in the hostile space environment of another planet? And of course, the reason this alternative is being looked for is that it is becoming ever clearer that there has not been enough time on Earth for abiogenesis, and then evolution, to take place.
174	So if life really did appear on some other planet, since there are no known planets with suitable conditions, that planet would have to be very far away. Some estimate that the nearest such planet is somewhere in the Andromeda galaxy, 44 light years away. Studies have shown that for pieces of rock from our neighbour Mars to reach the Earth, they often have to wander through space for even millions of years. How much longer, then, would they need to reach the Earth from so distant a planet! But let us suppose that there is a suitable planet around the nearest star (Proxima Centauri, 4.2 light years away), and that a piece breaks away from it at an escape velocity like that of the Earth (11 km/s). Then, if it is not deflected and does not collide with anything else, it needs 115,000 years to reach the Earth. If it starts from the Andromeda galaxy, it has to travel for more than 1 million years!
175	This means that the organic molecules or micro-organisms travelling on the meteorite would have remained exposed to the conditions of space (no air, extreme temperatures, high doses of radiation) for a very long time, and so it would be impossible for them to survive.
176	In addition, when a celestial body enters the Earth's atmosphere, high temperatures develop (1600 <sup>o</sup>C) that destroy meteorites either completely or for the most part. And that is without speaking of the conditions under which a piece of rock breaks away from a planet to become a meteorite, which could happen, for example, in a very violent collision. Could micro-organisms survive such an ejection and such enormous acceleration? And even if they survived all these conditions and reached the Earth, what guarantees that this “seeding” would succeed, and that the micro-organisms could adapt, survive further and evolve?
177	In any case, even if spores or bacteria are one day found on other planets, for example on Mars, this alone proves nothing. For if we accept that living matter can be carried from Mars to the Earth, the reverse may just as well have happened, from pieces of the Earth's surface that broke away and reached Mars, or even more so from spacecraft from Earth that land on the surface of Mars or the Moon.
178	You can read more about the problems panspermia faces as a theory in the article <a href="http://creation.com/did-life-come-from-outer-space" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Did life come from outer space?</a> on the <a href="http://creation.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">creation.com</a> website.
179	The sun standing still for Joshua
180	Can science explain the event recorded in the book of <br> Joshua, that the sun stood still for a whole day?
181	This event is recorded in the book of Joshua, chapter 10, verses 12–14, and describes how God stopped the sun and the moon for a full day so that the people of Israel could finish the battle against the Amorites. It was therefore a day that lasted 48 hours in all.
182	From a scientific point of view, such an event cannot happen. Moreover, any slowing of the Earth's rotation would cause catastrophic floods and weather on a vast scale, as the oceans and the atmosphere would keep moving at their original speed. It was therefore a miracle that God performed, similar to the moving back of the shadow on the sundial recorded in 2 Kings 20:8–11.
183	For the historicity of this event, as for the Flood, there is evidence in the accounts of ancient peoples of a longer day or night, depending on where each people lived (in the eastern or the western hemisphere). For example:
184	There is an ancient Greek myth about Phaethon, son of the god Apollo, who secretly took from his father the chariot with the winged horses with which Apollo guided the sun on its daily course across the sky. Not knowing how to drive the chariot well, young Phaethon caused chaos and a day longer than usual.
185	The demigod hero Maui of Māori mythology in New Zealand delayed the sunrise, so that the night lasted longer than usual.
186	The chronicles of the Culhuacan empire (in the region of Mexico) record a night that was slow to end.
187	You can read more in the article <a href="http://creation.com/joshuas-long-day" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Joshua’s long day</a> on the <a href="http://creation.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">creation.com</a> website.
188	The Bible and science disagree on the age of the Earth
189	According to various estimates based on the Bible, Creation took place 6 to 10 thousand years ago, while according to scientific data the Earth is about 4.5 billion years old and the universe 13.7 billion years old. What explains this contradiction?
190	The estimates that, on the basis of the Bible, give Creation an age of 6 to 10 thousand years usually take the 6 days in the first chapter of Genesis as 6 periods of twenty-four hours. But a careful study of that passage, and of other parts of the Bible, gives several reasons why these 6 days could not have been twenty-four-hour days. You can find more details in <a href="material/2008_05_22.htm">our article on this</a>.
191	So by the word “day” a longer period is meant, which we cannot define precisely. We could therefore consider that the total length of the 6 days of Creation corresponds to the ages that science estimates today for the Earth and the universe, and so there is no contradiction.
192	Of course, we should always keep some reservation about these ages, since scientists themselves say that they are only estimates (based on certain observations and methods of measurement) and not fully proven facts.

194	Could the three hours of darkness during the Lord's crucifixion have been caused by an eclipse of the sun?
195	No, the darkness during the Lord's crucifixion cannot have been caused by an eclipse of the sun. It was a supernatural event, which we describe in more detail in an <a href="files/CrucifixionDarkness.pdf">article on this</a>.
196	Proving God's existence through science
197	Question – comment
198	Trying to turn someone to Christianity, and more generally <br> trying to prove that God exists, through logical arguments, makes no sense.
199	The aim of the “Christianity &amp; Science” broadcast and of this website is not to try to prove the existence of God, but to point out that even for a completely rational thinker, the path of logical argument can make them seriously question whether the view that there is no God holds up logically.
200	Matters of faith and the miraculous works of God cannot be examined by a process of proof. What can be put to a scientific test, and pass it, is their historicity: that what the Bible records are events that really took place in human history.
201	Proof of God's existence is a personal matter for each person, and comes not as the result of a process of reasoning but as the result of personal experience.
