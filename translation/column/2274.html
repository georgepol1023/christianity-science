The Flood (Part 4)

“In the six hundredth year of Noah’s life, in the second month, the seventeenth day of the month, the same day were all the fountains of the great deep broken up, and the windows of heaven were opened. And the rain was upon the earth forty days and forty nights.” (Genesis 7:11–12)

To close our series of articles on the Flood, we will answer two more frequently asked questions:

1) Where did such a quantity of water come from, able to cover the whole Earth so that “all the high hills, that were under the whole heaven, were covered” (Genesis 7:19), and where did those waters end up? Could it have been a local flood in the region of Mesopotamia?

Answer:

Our planet has enormous quantities of water underground. Scripture says with astonishing accuracy that “all the fountains of the great deep [were] broken up” – that is, water began to gush up from deep fractures in the ground – while at the same time “the windows of heaven were opened” – that is, torrential rain poured down.

The quantities of water that still exist underground were estimated only in the last decade. A 2015 publication in the journal Nature Geoscience, for example, reports that “scientists have estimated that the planet holds a total of 23 million cubic kilometres of groundwater, down to a depth of two kilometres. If this water were on the surface of the Earth, it would cover all the land with a layer of water about 180 metres deep, or raise the level of the seas by 52 metres”1.

It is important to stress that for events in the distant past that were not observed, science can only put forward hypotheses and build models of how things may have happened, without being able to prove that what a particular model or theory describes really did happen. On the subject of the Flood, there are models that soundly argue that extensive geological changes on the Earth’s surface following the Flood led to the formation of the highest mountains that exist today. The highest mountains in Noah’s time were evidently lower than today’s, to the extent that they could be covered by water.

The same geological changes created natural reservoirs for the water, which gradually withdrew, revealing the dry land and the shape of the continents as they are today. So if anyone wants to see where the waters of the Flood are today, he need only go to the seashore.

That the Flood was a worldwide and not a local event is shown by the answer to the 2nd question, which follows.

2) Is there evidence that an event of such a scale as the Flood once took place?

Answer:

All over the planet, wherever rock sections are exposed, parallel bands of rock (strata layers) can be seen. The only satisfactory explanation for their formation is the deposition of soil materials swept along by huge quantities of water, which then settled according to their weight.

The formation of fossils of sea creatures found at high altitudes is due to some sudden geological event (the Flood), followed by the uplift of the land.

In more than 270 cultures around the world there are flood stories with the same basic plot, which testifies to their common origin among the descendants of Noah who were scattered abroad.

According to scientists, oil and natural gas reserves were formed from the accumulation of large quantities of dead animals and plants that were buried, and whose organic matter was turned into fossil fuels under high pressures and temperatures. This accumulation bears witness to their being swept along by huge quantities of water and to the geological events that followed.

For geological formations such as the Grand Canyon and other canyons like it, the most satisfactory explanation is that large sections of the ground were carried away by huge quantities of water.
