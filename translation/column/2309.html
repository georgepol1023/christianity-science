Sleep, laziness and good habits

Sleep and its importance for the body have greatly occupied the scientific community in recent years. We know that the average person will spend a third of his life asleep, which comes to about 25 years. So important questions inevitably arise about the role sleep plays in a person’s physical and mental health, and about what the Word of God tells us about bad sleeping habits.

Why sleep matters

Sleep is a state of altered consciousness and limited interaction with our surroundings, in which the body’s senses and the voluntary movement of the muscles are suspended. Sleep is necessary for memory and for passing important memories into long-term memory, for the healing of wounds, for the immune system and for the working of the hormones in the body.

It is especially vital for children’s proper development and general health. Studies have shown that children who sleep well have better attention, behaviour, ability to learn, memory and overall mental and physical health. Too little sleep can lead to high blood pressure and obesity. On the other hand, too much sleep is linked with depression, some chronic illnesses and a weakening of the body’s defences.

Laziness

Yet despite the benefits of good sleep, a person can easily fall into sloth and laziness. Solomon tells us (Proverbs 6:9–11):

“How long wilt thou sleep, O sluggard? when wilt thou arise out of thy sleep? Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep: so shall thy poverty come as one that travelleth, and thy want as an armed man.”

Just before this, he urges us to learn from the ant, which takes care in summer to gather what it will need in winter, though it has no overseer to command it.

So as we too are now in the summer season, it is very important to build good and blessed habits of sleep.

Good sleeping habits

  • It is very important to get up early in the morning and give time to communion with the Lord. Jesus Christ used to rise in the morning while it was still dawn and go out to pray to His Father (Mark 1:35). This summer, try to begin your day with the voice of the Spirit before all the other voices of daily life begin to steal your attention.
  • It is also good to go to bed early and not to stay up late for no reason. Melatonin, which contributes greatly to good-quality sleep, is secreted by the pituitary gland and follows the cycle of day and night. This means that 8 hours of sleep from 23:00 to 07:00 are much more beneficial to health than from 02:00 to 10:00 in the morning.
  • Using screens shortly before we lie down is also destructive to the quality of sleep. It has been shown that the light of the screen suppresses the secretion of melatonin and also keeps a person from falling into deep, restful sleep. The result is that the body is constantly tired even though it has slept enough. How lovely it is, then, before I go to sleep, to put down the screen and take the Word of God in my hands, and fall asleep thinking about the wonders and the works of the Lord.

Conclusion

Stress, daily pressure, widespread light pollution, excessive use of screens and the modern way of life in general are destroying people’s physical and mental health by disturbing one of the body’s most important functions: sleep.

But one of the Lord’s most beautiful promises to His children is:

“I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety.” (Psalm 4:8)

So this summer, keep a routine, honour the Word of God and enjoy your time of prayer. And if you suffer from insomnia and cannot sleep, know this: “for so he giveth his beloved sleep.” (Psalm 127:2)
