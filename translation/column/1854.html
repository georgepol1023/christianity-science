The peace of God and the peace of men

“Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men.” (Luke 2:14)

As we close another year that God’s longsuffering has given us, we would like to share with our readers a message of joy and hope.

We looked around us, but the only such message is the message of the Gospel of Jesus Christ: “And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come.” (Matthew 24:14).

These days great and small, rich and poor, educated and uneducated will wish one another peace. Political and religious leaders, and scientists too, will wish for world peace and declare that they are committed to achieving it. And yet the world has never been more dangerous and insecure.

As these lines are written, unprecedented, uncontrolled fires are burning California. Recently (11 September 2018) the Secretary-General of the UN stressed: “Climate change is moving faster than we are, and we must end the paralysis… We have the tools to make our action effective, but we lack the leadership to do what we must. Time is running out much faster than we thought,” the UN Secretary-General stresses. “We have just 12 years to change the destructive course…” (1)

In just 40 years the planet has lost a significant part of its biodiversity, that is, of its species and ecosystems. For example, from 1970 to 2012 the world’s populations of fish, birds, mammals, amphibians and reptiles fell by 58%. “The collapse of the world’s wildlife populations is a warning sign that nature is dying. But instead of putting the planet on life support, we are putting a sticking plaster on it.”

It is now perfectly clear that the road humanity is wildly following is not the right one. Man must recognise that the first broken relationship is the one between man and God. This is the first relationship that must be restored.

The ungodly person is the one who does not acknowledge God’s sovereign rights and his own guilt before Him. “But the wicked are like the troubled sea, when it cannot rest, whose waters cast up mire and dirt. There is no peace, saith my God, to the wicked.” (Isaiah 57:20–21).

God’s plan is to restore the longed-for peace between man and God, from which flows peace in relationships between people, states and nations.

The Person who would bring peace was prophesied in the Word of God, which cannot lie: “For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace…” (Isaiah 9:6). This Person is none other than the Lord Jesus Christ, the only begotten Son and Word of God. He Himself said to His disciples: “Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you” (John 14:27). The world does not give peace but a “balance of terror”, as it is aptly called, adorned with suspicion and deceit.

The apostle Paul writes: “Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ…” (Romans 5:1). In the person who accepts the sacrifice of Christ, the Holy Spirit comes to dwell, and His fruit is “love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance” (Galatians 5:22–23).

The message of Christmas, the message of peace and of God’s love for man, will be heard once again this year. Blessed shall we be if the signs of our times awaken us to receive into our hearts the Lord Jesus Christ, the Prince of Peace.

The call is personal and urgent: “Save yourselves from this untoward generation” (Acts 2:40).
