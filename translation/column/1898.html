Addiction to and dependence on “smartphones” (Part 1)

“All things are lawful unto me, but all things are not expedient: all things are lawful for me, but I will not be brought under the power of any.” (1 Corinthians 6:12)

The history of mobile phones (officially born in 1973) is very recent, but their development has been dizzying: we are already entering the age of fifth-generation (5G) phones. The parallel development of the internet, and the connection of smartphones to it, offer unimaginable data speeds. Already 100% of the country is covered, and at least 90% of the population use mobile phones and the internet.

But the mass use of mobile phones has increased concern about possible negative effects of the electromagnetic fields they emit when in use. For the first time in history, a large part of the population holds a wireless radiation antenna next to their head for a good part of the day.

In the light of this, certain questions urgently need answering:

  1. Is there really addiction to and dependence on mobile phones?
  2. Are there effects on health (physical or mental) from excessive use of mobile phones?
  3. What are the mechanisms of addiction, and how can we change them so as to prevent or treat dependence?
  4. THE COMPLETE SOLUTION (!)

There is no doubt that the very rapid spread of mobile phones is partly due to the fact that they are always there to “free your hands”. You can make calls, send messages, enter the kingdom of the internet and social media, find out about the weather, what your friends are doing, what is happening in the world and much more. All this creates an illusion of complete freedom and, in the end, of personal omnipotence. Let us consider what effect it would have, especially on a child who grows up with a smartphone in his hand, if we suddenly took away this extension of his hand…

One study showed, without exaggeration, that half of all teenagers are addicted to their phones. But the problem does not stop there; it extends to adults too, without their realising it…

Smartphone dependence is a worldwide epidemic, and in America they have begun to take the necessary steps, setting up digital detox centres for teenagers from the age of 13. The greatest fear from their uncontrolled use, the experts say, is the loss of the natural instincts for movement, exploration and social interaction. We cannot ignore the fact that face-to-face contact involves many senses, letting us see, hear, touch and smell the other person. This is lost as we replace the presence of the person we are talking to with a screen.

In young people this can have great effects on the development of the brain’s neural connections. Once again we marvel at the Word of God, which cannot lie, which foresees that as the Day of the Lord draws near, some will make a habit of having not physical but virtual fellowship in the church. The Word of God urges us not to forsake the assembling of ourselves together, and to consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works (Hebrews 10:24–25)!

The American Academy of Pediatrics has set the times children may spend in front of a screen: one hour a day, 2 hours at the weekend at most. Clinical psychologists in Greece too are crying out: parents, pay proper attention to what your children are doing. Parents need training. That they cannot set limits is their own issue. The teenage years are too late to set limits. You set them early and you keep them. Limits make children happier and healthier.

“Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.” (Proverbs 22:6)
