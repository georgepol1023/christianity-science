The natural sciences in the search for truth (Part 1)

“I have seen an end of all perfection: but thy commandment is exceeding broad.” (Psalm 119:96)

We thank the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, because He has made sure that the categorical admission of scientists of world standing has reached us too: that “rational thought can never reach the final truth”.

Shortly before our Lord Jesus Christ comes to catch up His church, while knowledge has increased and people run to and fro on the earth (Daniel 12:4), this finding is an invitation to “repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ” (Acts 20:21). God “will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth. For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus; who gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time” (1 Timothy 2:4–6).
The Word of God should have been kept in love, preached in love and defended in love.

But the tyrannical rule of the Roman Catholic church in the Middle Ages, and its violent opposition to anything that did not agree with its views or interests, brought faith in the Word of God into disrepute. The height of its horrific methods was the Inquisition, before which a countless multitude of dissenters were brought and martyred.

Kepler’s (1571–1630) formulation of the heliocentric system, in which the Earth moves around the Sun and not the other way round, called into question many ideas that had until then been taken for granted, taught in the universities and schools and protected by the established Church.

The famous Galileo (1564–1642) supported Kepler’s heliocentric system, and he is referred to as the “father of modern astronomy”. Because he dared to oppose the accepted teaching, he made many enemies and was regarded as a heretic. His conflict with the Roman Catholic Church is often cited as an example of the clash between authority and freedom of thought, and especially science, in Western society.

Newton’s monumental work, the Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy, summed up the achievements of the previous period and led science in new directions. It is the most important milestone of scientific determinism. According to this, if we know the initial conditions of a system and the principles that govern its motion, then we can predict how it will develop in the future. This proposition clearly has metaphysical implications, since it implies that the truth about the development of the world lies within the facts and principles of material reality itself, defined by relations of cause and effect – banishing every transcendent reality (the presence of God).

So “Truth” is known and can be determined, and man can conquer it…
And so the idea of the universal (perfect) man is introduced, represented by Leonardo da Vinci.
All this is summed up in a picture associated with Denis Diderot (a representative of the Enlightenment), in which “Truth” stands at the top surrounded by light, with her veils being removed by Philosophy and Reason (1 January 1764).

The new man who emerges no longer needs knowledge by revelation… He does not need God! Even if God exists, reason and philosophy are enough for him to know the “Truth”.

How wrong they were… and how much the all-knowing God loves us…

“For after that in the wisdom of God the world by wisdom knew not God, it pleased God by the foolishness of preaching to save them that believe. For the Jews require a sign, and the Greeks seek after wisdom: but we preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews a stumblingblock, and unto the Greeks foolishness; but unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God. Because the foolishness of God is wiser than men; and the weakness of God is stronger than men.” (1 Corinthians 1:21–25)

In the rest of this feature we will see how science itself overturned these beliefs.
