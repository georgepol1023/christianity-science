Young people and religion: a fading relationship

In one of the most fitting verses of the Bible for young people, Psalm 119 tells us: “Wherewithal shall a young man cleanse his way? by taking heed thereto according to thy word.” But what is the relationship of young people today with the Word of God, with religion and with the existence of God, both in our own country and around the world?

We can draw very useful information from an interesting study that was published in part in the Greek press (1). In this representative survey, carried out in October 2021 with a sample of 451 young people aged 17–34, questions were answered on “youth and religion” in Greek society.

The main conclusion from the analysis of the data is that there is a clear trend towards secularisation among young people (57% described themselves as Orthodox Christians and 24% as atheists), and a significant strengthening of attitudes and views among them in favour of a secular state (45% were in favour of religious education in school being optional, and 41% in favour of abolishing the blessing with holy water at the start of the school year). According to those asked, the voice of the Church is considered fairly backward (60%), and was judged negative towards homosexuals (77%), the place of women in society (60%) and refugees and migrants (58%).

Another survey, carried out in our country by the Pew Research Center in 2015–2016, showed the great confusion that exists about the fundamental principles of Christianity and the Gospel. While 90% of those asked described themselves as “Orthodox Christians”, nevertheless:

only 16% go to church weekly, 61% go rarely (a few times a year), and 22% never go to church.

12% of those who say they are Orthodox consult horoscopes, tarot cards and fortune-tellers.

59% believe in fate, and 20% believe in reincarnation.

36% of the “faithful” do not even believe in Paradise, while on the existence of God 26% are “fairly” sure and 7% are “not at all” sure.

Finally, another extensive survey carried out in Christian churches in North America by the Barna Group (2) showed that less than 1% of so-called Christians aged 18 to 23 have a consistent biblical worldview. One of the most important reasons is intellectual scepticism. Young people are not equipped with the right tools to face the strong wave of atheism and doubt, and so they make shipwreck of their faith.

So the question arises: how will young people remain in the faith? What is the role of the church and the family in raising young people who will be steadfast in the faith and fervent in spirit – for whom faith will not be an intellectual leap into the void, but a daily lived experience?

A young person, like every person, is looking for two things: love and truth. The Word of God tells us: “And they continued stedfastly in the apostles’ doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.” (Acts 2:42). The Lord’s instruction has not changed… We have changed. Instead of entertaining young people with sketches, bands and videos, the church needs to teach them the Scriptures with consistency, truth and diligence.

Young people need to find a safe refuge in a holy fellowship of love, and to experience the power of the Holy Spirit; to live the risen Christ, and to work for the glory of His Name.

A basic aim of the church is to teach the Scriptures to young people, so that through them “the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works” (2 Timothy 3:17).

That is the kind of youth Christ is preparing: men and women full of the Holy Spirit and of faith!
