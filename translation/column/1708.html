The effect of music on people and its role in the Christian life (Part 2)

Continuing our feature, we will try to set out from Scripture the spiritual effects of music, so that we can understand its role in the Christian life.

In the First Book of Samuel we read that when the Spirit of the Lord departed from the disobedient King Saul, an evil spirit troubled him, and the only way he could find peace was for the young David to play the harp for him: “And it came to pass, when the evil spirit from God was upon Saul, that David took an harp, and played with his hand: so Saul was refreshed, and was well, and the evil spirit departed from him” (1 Samuel 16:18, 23). The right music, then, played by the right person, resulted in the evil spirit being driven away.

So we conclude that music exposes a person to spiritual influences, because it can either help the heart open to sense the presence of God or, the other way round, open the way to interaction with evil spirits. The message of a song may indeed be expressed mainly through its words, but it is always accompanied by the music that suits it. So if someone hears only the melody of a song, he can grasp the general message and the mood it is trying to convey. For example, a lullaby cannot be set to fast, rhythmic music, nor a war march to a limp, soft melody. Moreover, the style and character of a melody are recognised worldwide, whatever the culture.

As for the role of music in the Christian life, the Word of God says that through hymns:

  • The name of the Lord is glorified and His wonderful works are proclaimed (for example, Psalm 96 and others), and there is also the opportunity for witness and confession: “I will praise thee, O Lord, among the people: I will sing unto thee among the nations.” (Psalm 57:9).
  • A person receives strength to do the will of God: “So will I sing praise unto thy name for ever, that I may daily perform my vows.” (for example, Psalm 61:8). Conversely, worldly, unspiritual songs weaken the Christian.
  • Christians admonish and teach one another: “teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord” (Colossians 3:16)
  • It becomes easier for a person to be filled with the Holy Spirit: “And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit; speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord” (Ephesians 5:18–19)

Since these things set out the role of music in the Christian life, we would like to point out that not every kind of music can be used for these purposes simply because it may have “Christian” words. The words of a hymn or a Christian song should not be frivolous or shallow but spiritual: based on the Word of God and containing messages of teaching and admonition.

Finally, we would like to make clear that the use of psalm-singing, and of music in general, in Christianity is not aimed at creating a cheerful atmosphere; it has real results, because it opens a person’s heart and makes it easier for him to draw near to God. After all, great victories of the people of God have been won as they glorified and praised the Lord:
“And at midnight Paul and Silas prayed, and sang praises unto God: and the prisoners heard them. And suddenly there was a great earthquake, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken: and immediately all the doors were opened, and every one’s bands were loosed.” (Acts 16:25–26)
