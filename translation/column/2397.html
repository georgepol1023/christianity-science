Why the Bible stands above all other sacred books (Part 2)

In the previous article we saw three basic points on which the Bible stands above all other sacred books, as it makes the very serious claim to be the Word of God, given by inspiration of God, for it is written: “All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable…” (2 Timothy 3:16). It is the oldest religious book, it is the bestseller of all time, and it is also completely and fully set within the historical context in which its events take place.

In this 2nd part we will set out two more basic tests of reliability and truth in which the Bible far surpasses all other sacred books.

  1. The Bible is a book in harmony with basic scientific principles.

Although it was written by 40 different authors (many of whom were “unlearned and ignorant men”) over a period of 16 centuries (during which scientific knowledge went through various phases and changes), all the statements in the Bible are in complete harmony with one another and scientifically consistent.

At a time when the understanding of the natural sciences was at a fairly primitive level, there are hundreds of statements in the Bible that are entirely consistent with modern scientific knowledge. The account of the creation of the world is radically different from the corresponding myths of the ancient Greeks or the ancient Indians. It describes the initial conditions and the sequence of events with scientific soundness, without losing the simplicity of the text or its theological purpose.

The writers could not have known that the Earth hangs upon nothing, that the universe is expanding, that the air of the atmosphere has weight, and that the number of the stars is of a similar order of magnitude to the number of grains of sand on the seashore, if the Holy Spirit had not revealed it to them.

  1. The Bible: a unique book of prophecy

Muhammad claims the title of the prophet through whom Allah brought humanity the final and complete revelation of his will. Yet he himself neither spoke nor wrote any prophecy, nor did he foretell any event that lay in his future.

The Bible, by contrast, is full of very specific prophecies that were fulfilled, often hundreds or even thousands of years later, with complete accuracy. The prophet Isaiah speaks of prophecy as the ultimate test for telling apart the gods that are no gods from the Lord Almighty:

“Let them bring them forth, and shew us what shall happen: let them shew the former things, what they be, that we may consider them, and know the latter end of them; or declare us things for to come. Shew the things that are to come hereafter, that we may know that ye are gods: yea, do good, or do evil, that we may be dismayed, and behold it together.” (Isaiah 41:22–23)

The prophecies of the Old Testament that refer to the person of the Messiah – directly or indirectly – are reckoned to number several hundred. They concern:

  • the time and place of His birth,
  • His divine origin,
  • His life,
  • the character and content of His work,
  • the miracles He would perform,
  • His death and
  • His resurrection.

All these predictions were completely fulfilled in the person of Jesus of Nazareth (see, for example, Genesis 49:10, Isaiah 9:7, 7:14, Micah 5:2, Isaiah 53:1, 4, 8, Daniel 9:1, 24, Psalm 2:7–9, 22:16–18).

Indeed, according to a statistical study (Canon Liddon), there are 332 different prophecies about the Messiah in the Old Testament which found their literal fulfilment in the historical person of Jesus. The probability of all of this happening by chance is calculated as 1 in a number that begins with 84 and is followed by 97 zeros!

And what can we say about the hundreds of prophecies concerning the course and fate of cities, nations, peoples, tribes and whole empires? And how can we fail to mention the prophetic words of our Lord Himself about His own crucifixion and resurrection, the destruction of Jerusalem, the course of the Church and the last days?

The honest student of the Bible, then, cannot help admitting that the Bible is the only sacred book that passes every test of reliability and is worthy of the honour of being called “the Word of God”. That is why we are called to study the Bible every day in a spirit of obedience, so that it may guide us into all truth, as it is written: “…ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free” (John 8:32)
