The social conformity experiment and where the Christian stands! (Part 1)

In the period after the Second World War many scientists tried to study the causes that led ordinary, everyday citizens to behave in such an extreme way and to submit to the influence of the crowd or of authority.

Among others, in 1950 the social psychologist Solomon Asch began a series of experiments on how people behave under social pressure. The aim of the experiment was for the researcher to find out whether the subject being studied would give in to the opinion of the majority, even when it was in direct contradiction to what his own eyes told him.

Each participant was placed in a group of 5–7 “confederates” (that is, people who knew the real purpose of the experiment but introduced themselves as ordinary participants).

The participants were shown a card with one line, followed by another card with 3 lines, (1), (2) and (3). They were asked to say which of the three lines matched the reference line in length (see picture).

The “real” participant answered last or second to last, after first hearing the confederates all give the same wrong answer.

To his surprise the researcher found that 37% of the volunteers gave in to the opinion of the majority, even though the answer was clearly wrong. In a personal interview afterwards, it emerged that the participants gave in for 2 different reasons:

Some honestly believed that the rest of the group was right, and so they adopted its opinion.

Others knew that the other participants were wrong but did not want to take a different position, fearing social exclusion and ridicule from the others.

So this social experiment and its conclusions give us great spiritual lessons, and shed light on truths written for thousands of years now in the Word of God, which cannot lie.

In a world that accepts the lie and rejects the truth, that calls black white and white black, where sin is christened freedom and holiness backwardness, it is easy for a child of God to be influenced, or to be afraid to rise to the occasion.

But the apostle Paul tells us:

“…and be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.” (Romans 12:2).

In these last times the Lord calls us to be “…blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom ye shine as lights in the world” (Philippians 2:15): to stand for the truth and to lift up the Word of God, whatever the cost.

There will always be social pressure to conform: not to stand out from everyone else; at school, to dress like the others; at university, to embrace atheistic philosophical messages; and to the question “Where will you go when you die?”, to answer too… “wherever the crowd goes”. But God “hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation”.

So stand like Daniel in Babylon, who would not defile himself with the idolatrous king’s food, although his companions gave in; like Joseph in Egypt, who refused the invitation of Potiphar’s wife to the passing pleasure of sin.

And finally, do not be afraid to bear witness to God. Do not be afraid to stand out, raising the banner of Christ. At the moment when everyone says “there is no God”, stand up and say that you have come to know Him, and that He is risen! For “whosoever shall confess me before men,” said Christ, “him will I confess also before my Father which is in heaven”!
