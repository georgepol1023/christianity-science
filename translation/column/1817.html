The macrocosm and the microcosm: the same Maker

In recent years, as the Word of God prophesied, we have witnessed an astonishing explosion of knowledge: “But thou, O Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end: many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased” (Daniel 12:4).

Armed with state-of-the-art equipment, man is exploring the macrocosm – the universe around him – and also the microcosm, the structure of the matter that surrounds him.
Great telescopes, some of them in orbit around the Earth, explore the depths of the universe at astonishing distances, unimaginable to human experience. According to the views of materialism, the universe is eternal and self-existent. But modern science now recognises that the universe, however vast it really is, is not infinite, and that there was a beginning to its creation. It is estimated that there are 100 billion galaxies, and each galaxy has about 100 billion solar systems… Let us take an (imaginary) journey through it.

As we move away from the surface of the Earth, at a million kilometres (109 m) we meet the orbit of the Moon, while at 100 billion kilometres (1014 m) our solar system can barely be made out. For greater distances the light year is used as a measure: the distance a vehicle would travel in one year if it went at the speed of light (300,000 kilometres a second), equal to ten trillion kilometres, or 1016 m. From that distance the sun of our solar system can no longer be seen. Enormous distances – and this is only the beginning! At 100,000 light years (1021 m) our own galaxy can just be made out, while at one million light years (1022 m) the spiral of the cluster of galaxies to which our galaxy belongs begins to appear. At 10 million light years (1023 m) even that is lost in the ocean of the universe!

But what about the microcosm that surrounds us? Let us take a leaf from a tree in our hands. If we approach it with a microscope, at 100 microns (10-4 m) we can make out its cells. At one micron (10-6 m) the nucleus can be clearly seen, and at 1,000 ångströms (10-7 m) the chromosomes of the cell, where all the genetic information is stored. At one ångström (10-10 m) we see the electron cloud of the atom, which our fellow countryman Democritus believed could not be split any further. But modern science has gone much deeper. At one picometre (10-12 m) we see the enormous empty space between the nucleus and the orbits of the electrons. At one fermi (10-15 m) we reach the surface of the protons and neutrons, while deeper still, at 100 attometres (10-16 m), we “see” the individual quarks.

We are surrounded by a great wonder, Creation, which cries out for the Creator. The dizzying sizes that surround us, and the incredible complexity of the macrocosm and the microcosm, leave without excuse anyone who dares to claim that all this came about and is sustained by itself. We need only mention that the famous biophysicist Harold Morowitz (1927–2016) calculated the probability of a single bacterium arising by itself as 1 in 10100,000,000,000! That is, completely impossible (1).

Truly, “The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork.” (Psalm 19:1)
Yes, our Earth is very small in the universe, but it is its important planet. Science today tells us that the whole universe came about and is sustained so that life can exist on Earth (see “The anthropic principle”, Christianity, August 2008). Here lives man, the crown of creation and the object of God’s love. On this planet the cross was set up and the blood of the Lord Jesus Christ was shed – the only begotten Son of God, through whom the whole universe was created and is sustained – so that we might be redeemed from sin.
The Bible tells us: “For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him: and he is before all things, and by him all things consist” (Colossians 1:16–17), and Hebrews 1:3: “upholding all things by the word of his power”.

Through faith in the sacrifice and Resurrection of Christ, man enters eternal life. “For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life” (John 3:16).

Soon Christ will come again to earth with power and great glory. Let us get ready to welcome Him.
