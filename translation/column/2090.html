Humanity in search of joy

Christmas is traditionally seen as a festive season associated with joy. People have the chance to meet their loved ones, various events are organised, gifts are exchanged, homes and streets are decorated, special foods are eaten, and in general there is an effort to create a festive atmosphere.

The reality, however, is that the way modern life is structured, and the events we see happening around the world, create – despite every effort – an atmosphere opposite to the one hoped for, not only at Christmas but throughout the year. Besides the coronavirus pandemic, scientists say there is also a worldwide “pandemic” of mental disorders, above all depression, an illness that in various forms affects about 350 million people every year and which, according to the World Health Organization, will be the leading cause of disability worldwide by 2030. The consequences of spreading depression are many and its cost is great, at the personal, family and social level. Studies on the subject are constant, as are the specialists’ efforts to deal with it through various sessions and treatments.

This October, in fact, the well-known scientific journal Nature Medicine announced a new treatment for depression using a brain implant placed in the right area of the brain through minimally invasive surgery. The device detects patterns of brain activity linked with depression and produces suitable electrical stimulation that creates feelings of alertness and positivity, so combating the symptoms of depression.

It is indeed an innovative and very promising method. But we should note that all these efforts and treatments aim simply to deal with or ease the symptoms and not the causes of depression, either because the causes are still undetermined or because they come from factors and situations that cannot be changed. On the other hand, however valuable a scientific achievement this implant really is, it should make us stop and think that we are now entering an age in which science will be able to intervene in the brain even to shape mood and emotions. What possibilities will open up in future with the development of such technologies, and to what degree will the human psyche be able to be shaped and steered? Once again, the answers to these crucial questions are found in the eternal Word of God, which cannot lie. The book of Revelation prophesies a time when some kind of intervention in the human body, through receiving the mark of the antichrist, will take away the possibility of free will and of repentance (Revelation chapter 14).

On the other hand, God’s call to man to know true joy is constant. This happens when the basic problem is dealt with, which is the cause of sorrow and misery, and which is none other than sin. The solution was given 2,000 years ago on the cross of Golgotha. All anyone has to do is come to the Father through Jesus Christ, confessing his sins, and then something wonderful will happen: the blood of Jesus Christ will cleanse him from all sin, his relationship with God will be restored, and he will have a living relationship and communion with Him.

Dear reader, you don’t need to look for a special occasion or wait for Christmas to feel some joy that will itself be temporary. Hurry to the joy of eternal life with Christ, and to the security of the presence of the One who will take charge of your whole life, in the best way, to the very end.

“Thus saith the LORD, Stand ye in the ways, and see, and ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein, and ye shall find rest for your souls.” (Jeremiah 6:16)
