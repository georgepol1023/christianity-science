The church and the acceptance of same-sex couples

This February the General Synod of the Church of England decided to give its blessing to the marriages and civil partnerships of same-sex couples. This means that its clergy are not allowed to conduct a same-sex marriage, but they can, once a civil marriage or civil partnership has taken place, hold a service of “blessing” for the couple, so making it accepted in the body of the church. It is a historic decision, taken by a vote of the church’s governing body after long consultations and heated debate.

“Now the Spirit speaketh expressly, that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils; speaking lies in hypocrisy; having their conscience seared with a hot iron” (1 Timothy 4:1–2)

The main argument put forward by the supporters of this position is love. Since God Himself is love, the church, out of love, should not reject same-sex couples but should open its arms and accept them into its fold as children of God. But this is a misinterpretation of the biblical meaning of love. God is indeed love, and it is precisely out of love that He set limits and laid down laws for marriage, as for everything in His creation:

“Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female” (Matthew 19:4)

Anything, then, that distorts what He created is opposed to His love!

A further argument is the view that the passages of the Bible that condemn homosexuality refer to cases of abuse and violence or of casual relationships, whereas in modern times same-sex relationships show love, faithfulness and mutuality. This argument is clearly refuted by a careful study of the relevant passages, for example Romans 1:24–27, where it is plain that whatever departs from “the natural use” is “against nature”, “that which is unseemly” and “vile affections”.

It must be made clear that the biblical meaning of love is not the same as whatever satisfies a desire or a passion. Love protects the person loved from corruption and reveals the danger of loss and destruction. It is not sentimentality that does not want to upset the sufferer, but deep concern for the revealing of the truth that sets free. That is precisely why the Bible shows that genuine love also hates (see Proverbs 6:16–19). For example, whoever loves children hates abortion, whoever loves life hates murder, and whoever loves the truth hates lies.

In exactly this spirit the church of Christ is called to preach: “Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.” (Matthew 3:2), offering love and acceptance to the sinner – not so that he may stay the same, but so that he may be transformed into a new creature in Christ Jesus. This is exactly the way the Lord drew near to sinners:

“Why eateth your Master with publicans and sinners? But when Jesus heard that, he said unto them, They that be whole need not a physician, but they that are sick. […] for I am not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.” (Matthew 9:11–13)

Those of us who are responsible for shepherding and overseeing the Christian flock must, in the fear of God, keep “in doctrine … uncorruptness” (Titus 2:7), knowing that the church of Christ is “the pillar and ground of the truth” (1 Timothy 3:15), so that it may be “the salt of the earth: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be salted? it is thenceforth good for nothing, but to be cast out, and to be trodden under foot of men.” (Matthew 5:13)
