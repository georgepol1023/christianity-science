Does atheism have answers? (Part 7) Life after death

Continuing our series of articles on how well atheism stands up as a worldview, we will look, by way of conclusion, at the subject of life after death. For a worldview to be complete, it must give a clear answer about what happens when a person reaches the threshold of death: is it the end of his existence, or is there some continuation after death?

The atheistic view of the world proposes that the whole physical world works by mechanistic processes, blindly directed by the laws of nature. So there is no soul, and nothing spiritual. All of man’s so-called “spiritual” functions are due solely to his brain. After all, according to the theory of evolution, as the human brain evolved over the ages, at some point it passed a “threshold” of complexity, and then consciousness, self-awareness, emotions and so on sprang from this biological machine. So since we are purely biological machines, subject to decay and with a limited lifespan, at death all the “spiritual” functions should stop, and there should no longer be any possibility of consciousness, perception, emotions, memory and so on, exactly as happens with the bodily functions.

In 2001 Sam Parnia (a physician) and Peter Fenwick (a neuropsychiatrist at the Institute of Psychiatry in London) published a study based on the accounts of a group of 63 heart attack victims who were classed as clinically dead but were later revived. The aim of the research was to study the so-called near-death experiences that have been reported from time to time in various parts of the world by so many people that the phenomenon is now considered impossible to ignore. The researchers set specific criteria for judging such an experience acceptable and not a hallucination or the product of imagination. They found that about 10% of those asked reported that, during the time their brains were not working, they had organised thought processes, with the capacity for memory and emotions (the possibility that this sense was an impression created by the brain because of lack of oxygen or side effects of drugs was ruled out). In particular, they were able to reproduce accurately images, sounds and conversations that were taking place around them while none of their body’s sense organs was working. Many accounts also agree with one another about meeting and speaking with a higher person whom they describe as God. The research continued, and until a few years ago the participants had passed 2,000, while the proportion of near-death experiences reported grows as the research expands, and has now reached 40%.

On the basis of these and other studies in other areas of human existence that we have mentioned in earlier articles, many scientists around the world have now begun to accept that man does not only have a brain (which is material) but also a non-material reality, what we call mind and spirit, which, it seems, goes on existing even after the body and the sense organs stop working, keeping the person’s consciousness. In the 16th chapter of the Gospel of Luke, a very interesting conversation after death takes place between the rich man and Abraham:

“And in hell he lift up his eyes, being in torments, and seeth Abraham afar off, and Lazarus in his bosom. And he cried and said, Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water, and cool my tongue; for I am tormented in this flame. But Abraham said, Son, remember that thou in thy lifetime receivedst thy good things, and likewise Lazarus evil things: but now he is comforted, and thou art tormented.” (verses 23–25)

So on yet another question that shows up the inconsistency of the atheistic worldview, the Bible, which is the Word of God that cannot lie, states clearly, in this and many other places, that after death a person still has consciousness, senses, organised thought, memory, emotions, awareness of his condition and the ability to communicate.

The person who has the Word of God as the compass of his life is rightly informed from the start about life after death, knowing that as Jesus Christ “rose from the dead”, so he too will take part in His eternal kingdom with a new resurrection body.

“But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.” (1 Corinthians 15:20) “but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.” (1 John 3:2)
