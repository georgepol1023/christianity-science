The reliability of the Bible – Biblical archaeology

“…to write unto thee in order… that thou mightest know the certainty of those things, wherein thou hast been instructed” (Luke 1:3–4)

Dear readers, we greet you in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. As we enter a new year, we thank God for His love and longsuffering. Events are moving fast and testify that the hour when the Lord will come to receive His church is very near: “I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also.” (John 14:3).

Man today lives in insecurity, fear and a sin that has now reached its full measure… And yet there is a people, the church of Jesus Christ, who enjoy the peace of God, because they believe His Word, which is full of wonderful promises. They do not make frantic moves, but grow in holiness, take care of the family, work and give to those who have not, and wait patiently for the Lord to come and receive them (1 Thessalonians chapter 4). They are a strong people: “…in quietness and in confidence shall be your strength” (Isaiah 30:15). They are the salt of the earth and the light of the world (Matthew 5:13–14). They are an informed people, who study the things of God in the church, so as to become like Christ: “and the disciples were called Christians first in Antioch” (Acts 11:26).

With these thoughts, then, we want to begin this year too by recommending the Bible, the Word of God which cannot lie, which should be missing from no home and no heart.

The Bible was written over a period of about 15 centuries by about 40 different writers. It consists of the Old Testament and the New Testament, and it makes a very serious claim: “It is the Word of God.”

Man’s highest means of communication, as opposed to the animals’, is speech – the word. In the same way the Bible, which is the Word of God, speaks straight into the heart, the spirit, the mind and the conscience of the sincere person.

This claim proves itself when a sincere person takes the Bible into his hands. But we want to look at the reliability of the Bible not only as a result of its effect on a person’s life – which is the primary evidence of its reliability (1 Thessalonians 2:13) – but also through archaeology and the study of ancient texts.

The writers of the Bible are about 40 different people who lived in different times and in different social and political settings. Yet the result is a unified book: reading the Bible gives the sense not of changing books but of reading one and the same book throughout.

A basic question is how we know that the text of the Old Testament we read today is the same as the original Hebrew text. As for the text, first there is the Septuagint translation into Greek, made in the 3rd century BC, and the Masoretic text, whose latest manuscripts date from the 9th century AD, so that their reliability was questioned. But in 1947 the Qumran manuscripts (the Dead Sea Scrolls) came to light. They date from the 2nd and 1st centuries BC, and they confirm the reliability of the Masoretic text.

An important factor ensuring that the texts of the Old Testament we have today are faithful to the originals is that the copying of the manuscripts was done by specialist copyists, called Talmudists, who kept truly very strict rules when copying the sacred texts, so as to avoid any error.

The differences between the Bible, especially the Old Testament, and the mythological stories of ancient peoples are also obvious. The texts of the Bible give a clear setting of events in place and time, complete consistency with historical and scientific facts, and impartiality in describing people. The people of the Bible are not idealised; often their weaknesses are shown too. The clear purpose of all the texts is to exalt one Person alone: Jesus Christ.

“…Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom; teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord.” (Colossians 3:16)
