The get-rich-quick deception

In our time the idea is strongly promoted that a person can get rich quickly, easily and without effort. Through social media and various “influencers”, a picture of success is presented that promises instant profit and an easy life. Behind this picture, however, lies a great delusion: the deception of easy wealth.

The Bible clearly warns us that “…the love of money is the root of all evil” (1 Timothy 6:10). Money is not the problem; the love of it is. And once the love of money takes root in a person’s heart, it is only a matter of time before all seventeen works of the flesh spring up from it as well.

At the same time, the wisdom of Proverbs reminds us that real fruit comes through hard work: “In all labour there is profit” (Proverbs 14:23). Easy wealth promises much without effort, but that road does not lead to blessing; it leads to loss and misery.

The Bible gives us striking examples of people who were carried away by the love of money and came to a tragic end.

  • Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve disciples, betrayed Christ for thirty pieces of silver. Money blinded him spiritually and led him to destruction.
  • Also, in the parable of the rich fool (Luke 12:16–21), a man gathered great riches and believed he had secured his future. But God calls him a “fool”, for that very night his soul is required of him. His wealth did not save him, because his heart was far from God.

In our modern age this same trap appears in new forms.

Many influencers promote ways of making “easy money”, risk-free investments and quick success. This creates the illusion that happiness lies in wealth and that it can be gained without effort and without sacrifice – without reading and study, simply by pressing a few keys and having the right contacts.

Gambling addiction, too, is another expression of this delusion. A person hopes for a quick win, but is often led into losses, debts and despair. What begins as a “game” ends up as chains that take him away from peace and from God. And it is written: “for of whom a man is overcome, of the same is he brought in bondage” (2 Peter 2:19).

Christ, however, calls us to a different road: not the easy one, but the true one – the road of honest work, of trust in God and of freedom from the love of money. A person who lives with moderation and faith does not make his life depend on money, but finds true joy in his relationship with God.

So let us watch over our hearts. Let us not be carried away by promises of easy profit, but seek the life that God blesses. For, as the Bible teaches us: “But seek ye first the kingdom of God… and all these things shall be added unto you.” (Matthew 6:33).

This is the road that leads to true blessing.
