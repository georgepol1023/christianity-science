The Flood (Part 3)

“And of every living thing of all flesh, two of every sort shalt thou bring into the ark, to keep them alive with thee; they shall be male and female. Of fowls after their kind, and of cattle after their kind, of every creeping thing of the earth after his kind, two of every sort shall come unto thee, to keep them alive.” (Genesis 6:19–20)

Continuing our feature on the Flood, we will answer a common question: how did all the kinds of animals fit into Noah’s ark?

First of all, it is obvious that the creatures that live in water did not need to go into the ark. Next, there was no need for all the varieties (breeds) we find today within a kind to go into the ark. To understand this, let us think that today there are more than 130 breeds of dog (sheepdogs, Dobermans, poodles, bulldogs and so on). Noah did not take a pair of every breed, but simply a pair of the dog kind. The breeds came about later, through the genetic variety that was expressed through cross-breeding and according to the regions where that kind was scattered geographically after the Flood. In any case, the ark did not hold people of all the races we see today, but only 8 people of the same family line. And yet from them has come all the variety of people that exists today.

So according to the “worst-case” calculations, at most 35,000 animals needed to go into the ark. Since there are very large animals (elephants, for example) and very small ones (mice, for example), we can take the sheep as the average size of an animal on the ark. It is as if 35,000 sheep had to go into the ark. If we assume that a large railway freight wagon can hold 240 sheep, then 150 such wagons would be needed – which do not fill even a third of the ark’s capacity, as we gave it in the previous article (about 40,000 cubic metres). It is also possible that for the large kinds of animals Noah took not adults but young ones, so that they took up less space, ate less food and were quieter.

So there was ample room for the animals to be settled and to live in comfort, and plenty of room for food stores. Perhaps a whole deck of the ark was empty. After all, it is well known that for a vessel to float it must have plenty of empty space.

In today’s ark, which is the church of Christ, there is room for you too, dear reader. Noah, as a preacher of righteousness, warned people to be saved from God’s coming judgment, which would come through an event that had never happened on Earth before. The message is the same today, from the lips of our Lord Jesus Christ:

“For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, and knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be.” (Matthew 24:38–39)

In the next part, God willing, we will complete the subject of the Flood by answering the question of where all the water that covered the Earth came from and where it went, and we will also look at whether evidence has been found that an event of such a scale once took place on our planet.
