Scientific references in the Bible (Part 2)

“All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable” (2 Timothy 3:16)

With this article we complete our feature on the places in the Bible that touch on subjects within the scope of science, and which are set out in the relevant verses with remarkable consistency with modern scientific knowledge.

  • The human body contains the same elements as the soil

“And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground” (Genesis 2:7)

The elements of the human body are oxygen, carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, calcium, phosphorus, sulphur, potassium, chlorine, sodium and other minerals. All these elements are also found in the soil. Our body contains enough carbon to fill two sacks of charcoal, enough iron to make two large nails, and enough sulphur to make many boxes of matches.

  • The life of living things depends on the blood

“For the life of the flesh is in the blood […] for the life of all flesh is the blood thereof” (Leviticus 17:11, 14)

Today we know that this is indeed true, because the blood both carries nutrients to all the cells of the body and carries out the exchange of gases in the lungs as we breathe: it takes up the oxygen we breathe in and carries it all over the body, and gives up the carbon dioxide for us to breathe out. These two functions, nourishment and breathing, are the basic functions of life.

Not knowing any of this, doctors in the Middle Ages practised bloodletting (removing blood) from patients to drive out the disease which, as they understood it, “flowed in their blood”. The result, of course, was that the patient was in the end in danger of dying from loss of blood.

  • Physical health is linked with the state of the mind

“A sound heart is the life of the flesh: but envy the rottenness of the bones.” (Proverbs 14:30)

“A merry heart doeth good like a medicine: but a broken spirit drieth the bones.” (Proverbs 17:22)

Modern medicine and psychology confirm that the state of the mind has a decisive effect on physical health on at least three levels: on the endocrine system and its production of hormones, on the immune system, and on the cardiovascular system, where a poor psychological state, stress and other factors can increase the likelihood of heart attacks and strokes.

  • The water cycle

“All the rivers run into the sea; yet the sea is not full; unto the place from whence the rivers come, thither they return again.” (Ecclesiastes 1:7)

About 1,000 years before Christ, Solomon puts into words an early understanding of the water cycle (300 years earlier than a similar first statement by Hesiod), saying that although all the rivers pour into the sea, it does not overflow, because the water “returns” (through evaporation) to where it came from. This cyclical journey of water, vital for the planet, began to be understood in later times, in the 16th–17th centuries, but only at the beginning of the 19th century were ideas about the water cycle universally accepted by science.

All the examples we have mentioned in this article and the previous one, and others we could give, lead to the conclusion that although the Bible is not a science book, whenever it says something that falls within the scope of science it is not wrong, but consistent with modern knowledge and discoveries. The importance of this conclusion is that it is one more pillar supporting the inspiration of God in the Bible; and when a person receives the Bible into his heart as the word of God, the way opens for him to receive salvation:

“the holy scriptures, which are able to make thee wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus” (2 Timothy 3:15)
