The natural sciences in the search for truth (Part 2)

“Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.” (John 5:24)

In our previous feature we saw how humanity was led to the “Enlightenment” and to the view that by reason and philosophy it could know absolute truth. There was a certainty that truth is known and can be determined, and that man can conquer it.
The tools of human thought – mathematics, physics and philosophy – declared that the truth about the origin and development of the world lies within the facts and principles of material reality itself. Every thought of God was banished. The Principia Mathematica of Russell and Whitehead was the most famous attempt to find that perfect system by which everything else could be tested as true or false.

And suddenly everything changed. The existing models could not explain the behaviour of atomic and subatomic particles, and so quantum mechanics was formulated, a basic axiom of which is the “uncertainty principle” stated by Heisenberg. Put simply: “We cannot know precisely both the position and the velocity of a particle at the same time.” Man’s pride and certainty gave way to embarrassment and uncertainty, at least as far as physics was concerned.
Mathematics, however, seemed unshakeable: the perfect tool for conquering truth. But the critique of Ludwig Wittgenstein (1889–1951), philosopher of mathematics, shook the foundations of extreme scientific determinism. These had already begun to crumble after the publication of the incompleteness theorem (1931) by the young Kurt Gödel (1906–1978). Gödel worked on the provability of mathematical principles and showed that logic will always rest on unprovable principles, since it is impossible to show that all principles can be proved. In short, Gödel proved, in a strictly mathematical way, that logic is unable to reach the whole of the truth. The universe is a closed system, and with tools, however humanly perfect, we can never know the final Truth.
The information must come from outside, by revelation. The Truth is a Person, the Lord Jesus Christ (John 14:6), and the Father reveals Him to the humble. The proud person who tries selfishly – by his own sword, as we might say – to reach the truth shuts himself out.
The Lord’s disciples had not studied the philosophy of logic, or higher mathematics and physics. But they had before them the Great Teacher, the Truth Himself, and they had a humble spirit. To the Lord’s question, “But whom say ye that I am?”, Simon Peter the fisherman did not hesitate: “Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God.” “And Jesus answered and said unto him, Blessed art thou, Simon Barjona: for flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven. And I say also unto thee, That thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it…”
(Matthew 16:13–18).

The Word of God is the absolute truth on which a person can and must build his life. In it there are no “probablys”, no vagueness, no logical dead ends. The Word of God will never disappoint you. It will inform you with complete reliability about everything, “For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him…” (Colossians 1:16).

But here we must stress that the Word of God, which cannot lie, also tells us with complete certainty about hell and eternal loss. Some may smile ironically. But let us take heed, for the Word of God speaks to us of the everlasting fire prepared for the devil and his angels.

God “will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth. For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus; who gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time…” (1 Timothy 2:4–6)
