From conception to birth

The wonder of life and the providence of God

In our broadcast on Radio Christianity (104.3 FM) on 27 January 2022, we began a new series on the wonder of life and the unborn child.

From ovulation and the physical union of husband and wife we go on to fertilisation and the formation of the zygote. Then follow implantation and growth, differentiation and the formation of the placenta. The embryo grows so fast in the first weeks of pregnancy that, if the rate stayed the same for all 9 months, it would weigh 1.5 tonnes at birth!

Throughout the whole process, mechanisms of incredible beauty and precision are at work: mechanisms built into the DNA of the first cell and carried out with a precision that the best-equipped factory would envy.

Here we will not look at the wonderful biology (we have dealt with it at length in earlier articles), but will dwell on just 2 points that particularly impress us and lead to the realisation that behind all this is the good hand of God the Father.

  1. From the moment the sperm unites with the egg (conception), a new life appears.

Great efforts have been made to place the beginning of new life at a later stage. The Stoics believed that the soul enters the body when the newborn takes its first breath, while Aristotle believed in the theory of gradual ensoulment (a vegetative soul which then becomes an animal soul and finally a human one). Modern scientists, for their part, have tried to identify the beginning with some biological feature, such as the first heartbeat or the start of brain activity.

But the Word of God is clear and leaves no room for doubt. From the moment of fertilisation and the appearance of the zygote, we also have a new being.

In the prophet Hosea it says: “As for Ephraim, their glory shall fly away like a bird, from the birth, and from the womb, and from the conception.” It takes us back to conception as the beginning of existence, not to birth or to life in the womb (Hosea 9:11).

Also, in the angel’s visit to Joseph, the original Greek text says: “Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost. And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS.” (Matthew 1:20–21). It speaks of the child Mary carries in her womb as “that which is begotten in her” (as the Greek word literally means), and separates it completely from the birth still to come.

Moreover, the Word of God speaks of the twins Rebekah carried in her womb as children, and not as physical entities, embryos or a collection of cells, as various modern organisations would have it: “And the children struggled together within her; and she said, If it be so, why am I thus? And she went to enquire of the LORD.” (Genesis 25:22)

  1. The whole process is marked not by chance but by remarkable providence.

During physical union, about 300 million sperm from the man compete to fertilise a single egg of the woman. But only one of them, under the right conditions, will be able to get through the membrane of the egg and lead to conception.

Let us imagine it for a moment: 300 million different combinations that could lead to 300 million different children – different in their outward features, in their sex and in their physical abilities. The fact that in the end I am me is not so simple. Behind it is the wonderful providence of God.

To the prophet Jeremiah the Lord said: “Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations.” (Jeremiah 1:5). The Lord knew you before conception. “Thine eyes did see my substance, yet being unperfect”, says the psalmist (Psalm 139:16).

So know today that you are not the chance product of some natural processes. You are not simply a collection of cell structures that happened to be favoured and born. You are a wonderful creation of God the Father, who has watched over and still watches over every moment of your life. He loved you before you even existed…

“The LORD hath appeared of old unto me, saying, Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee.” (Jeremiah 31:3)
