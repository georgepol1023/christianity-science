The climate is changing

“The earth mourneth and fadeth away, the world languisheth and fadeth away, the haughty people of the earth do languish. The earth also is defiled under the inhabitants thereof; because they have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, broken the everlasting covenant.” (Isaiah 24:4–5).

As these lines are written, the USA is preparing to face hurricane Maria, while the biblical-scale destruction from hurricanes Harvey and Irma, which have only just passed, is still fresh. At the same time two devastating earthquakes, of 8.4 and 7.1 on the Richter scale, have shaken Mexico. The damage from the fires in Portugal, France, America and Greece is incalculable, and 2017 looks set to be the fourth year in a row with record rises in global temperature.


What is going on, the ordinary person wonders. The scientists assure us: the climate is changing. We have come within a breath of the point beyond which developments will be rapid and irreversible.
Below we set out for our readers ten thoroughly studied measures that confirm that the climate is changing very fast.


1. The average air temperature over land has risen.

Result: drought and heatwaves that cause fires, poor harvests and shrinking water reserves.

2. The average air temperature over the oceans has risen

Result: more and stronger hurricanes.

3. The ice covering the Arctic is shrinking very fast.

Result: less of the sun’s radiation is reflected back (albedo), and more is absorbed by the water that replaces the ice.

4. The glaciers are melting.

Result: populations that depend on water from the steady melting of glaciers will suffer from water shortages.

5. Sea levels are rising.

Let us remember that the world’s ten largest cities are on the coast. Farmland will shrink and ecosystems will be disturbed.

6. The humidity of the air has risen

Result: air-conditioning units work harder, adding to the rise in temperature.

7. The oceans are warmer

The oceans store and release heat on a large scale. They are essential to keeping the climate stable, and the rise in their heat is both evidence of and a mechanism for destabilising the world’s climate.

8. The surface temperature of the seas has risen.

This leads to more and stronger cyclones and hurricanes.

9. The areas of the northern hemisphere covered by snow are shrinking.

This (among other things) speeds up the rise in global temperature.

10. The temperature of the lowest layer of the atmosphere has risen.

Satellite measurements show that the temperature of the troposphere (as this layer of the atmosphere is called) has risen as a result of the greenhouse effect. Mainly carbon dioxide, which comes from burning fossil fuels, traps the sun’s energy and raises the temperature.
