Climate change is nearing the point of no return

“It is a matter of time before climate change shows its destructive nature in a way that will be visible to all. The question is whether we can take corrective action, or whether we have already reached a point of no return” (1).

It is autumn 2018. The wounds in the body of society are still fresh from the fire in Attica that cost the lives of 99 of our fellow human beings. Phenomena such as Mediterranean cyclones, which most of us did not even know existed, will be frequent (unwelcome) visitors, the experts tell us. The passage of cyclone Xenophon through our country left great destruction behind it. And around the world, too, the climate is becoming ever faster and more unstable: devastating fires in California, Portugal, Spain and France; heatwaves in northern Europe; melting ice; rising sea levels; shrinking farmland; desertification; new diseases – these make up our daily scene.

Finally, the statement of the IPCC co-chair Debra Roberts that “the next few years are probably the most important in our history” gives the measure of the challenge humanity faces. An immediate reorientation of the world economy is needed, “at a pace that has no documented historic precedent” (2).

At this point we should tell our readers that the IPCC is a scientific panel of the UN, founded in 1988, with 195 member states. Its purpose is to assess the effects climate change will have on human life and to propose measures to prevent them.

Recently (October 2018) the IPCC, in an urgent announcement after analysing 6,000 studies, noted that the problems caused by climate change are already being felt and will in any case get worse. Humanity has 12 years to cut emissions by 45% compared with 2005 levels. What is needed is systematic promotion of renewable energy, a very rapid move away from coal as a source of energy, and the planting of new forests over vast areas.

“Limiting warming to 1.5°C is possible within the laws of chemistry and physics, but doing so would require unprecedented changes” in energy, land use, industry and transport, said Jim Skea, co-chair of the IPCC’s Working Group III. (2)

“With 2.6 degrees of warming, 50 million people in Egypt, India, Indonesia, the Philippines, China, Vietnam, Bangladesh and the USA will live in areas that may become uninhabitable because of constant flooding. National borders will lose their meaning. You can put up a wall to stop 10,000, 20,000, a million people, but not 50 million,” said Aromar Revi, director of the Indian Institute for Human Settlements and one of the authors of the report (2).

Wherever modern man turns, he sees dead ends and deadly dangers. And yet there is a wonderful Salvation. It is He who said: “Let not your heart be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me. In my Father’s house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also. And whither I go ye know, and the way ye know. Thomas saith unto him, Lord, we know not whither thou goest; and how can we know the way? Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.” (John 14:1–6)

Suddenly the Lord Jesus Christ will come to receive His church (1 Thessalonians 4:13–5:11). God loves man very much, and not only does He not want man to be lost; He has prepared for him a glorious eternal future, through His only begotten Son, our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.

Let us call on Him in faith to come into our lives while there is still time; soon it will be too late…

“And with many other words did he testify and exhort, saying, Save yourselves from this untoward generation.” (Acts 2:40)
