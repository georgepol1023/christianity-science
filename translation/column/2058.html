What is life?

It is a question bound up with our very existence. Biology tries to trace the characteristics of life. Astrobiology studies the conditions that must exist on a planet for life to develop, and astronomers scan the endless sky in vain for signs of extraterrestrial life. But in the end, what is life?

The farmer sows the precious seed in the ground, and after a few quiet months he is amazed to see life springing up of its own accord from the soil. The young couple enjoy the fruit of their love, and a new life begins to form in the mother’s womb. It is the “miracle of life”, as many scientists have called it. Yet in the face of all this, all science can do is describe it with awe.

One of the most basic principles of biology is that “every living cell comes from another living cell”. For the last two centuries biologists have tried in vain to discover the origin of life. The theory of abiogenesis was put forward, according to which, in the oceans of our primordial Earth and under the influence of forces from lightning and volcanic activity, amino acids began to form entirely “by chance”. The amino acids built proteins, which in turn built single-celled and finally multicellular organisms. Out of matter, over millions of years, life emerged. But the theory fell, because it broke basic laws of biology, physics and information science (see our website for more information).

In the end, all science has been able to do in the face of this wonder is to describe it, not to define it. Every living organism must have the following 6 characteristics:

  • Homeostasis: regulating its internal environment so as to keep a steady state
  • Organisation: cooperation between the various cells, from tissues up to the level of organ systems
  • Metabolism: the transformations of energy as chemical substances and energy are converted.
  • Growth: increasing in size with age up to a final size, depending on the organism.
  • Adaptation and response to stimuli: gradual variation in response to changes in the environment and internal changes.
  • Reproduction: the ability to produce new, independent organisms.

But this brings us back to the original question. In the end, what is life?

Modern man’s inability to define life is not due to scientific inadequacy, but to the fact that life is not a biological property but a Person. About 2,000 years ago the Lord Jesus Christ said to His 11 disciples: “I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.” John 14:6

In the 1st chapter of John, too, we have an astonishing statement: “In him was life; and the life was the light of men” (John 1:4). In Christ there is life. He forms the bones of the baby in the mother’s womb (Psalm 139:15–16). He sends forth His Spirit and renews the face of the earth (Psalm 104:30). “…he giveth to all life, and breath, and all things” (Acts 17:25).

So in the end, the answer to the age-old question of all scientists and philosophers is Jesus Christ. And to your own problem and dead end, too, the answer remains the same. Only Jesus Christ can meet your every need, answer your every question and wipe away your every tear.

A few days ago the Christian world celebrated Easter and the Resurrection of our Lord. It is not simply a historical event but a lived experience that each of us can enjoy. For He died to give you a far higher and richer life: eternal life!

“Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: and whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?” (John 11:25–26)

If you believe it, you will taste it!
