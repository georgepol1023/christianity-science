Summer 2021: the end-times thermometer is rising…

This past summer there was remarkable activity in space matters, keeping those who follow humanity’s developments and progress in space “conquests” very interested.

The fault that struck the historic Hubble space telescope in mid-June was successfully dealt with. Hubble has been in orbit since 1990 and has provided an enormous wealth of information about distant planets, black holes, nebulae, galaxies and much more…

A little earlier, on 7 June, NASA’s uncrewed Juno mission approached Jupiter’s icy moon Ganymede at the shortest distance ever achieved (1,000 km), and the next day it flew past Jupiter, passing from one pole to the other in a few hours and sending back a wealth of information and never-before-seen images of the system of the giant planet of our solar system. At the same time, efforts in so-called “space tourism” are growing: for a few hundred thousand dollars, it offers the experience of a short flight to the edge of space. Despite the novelty of the venture, the competitive race has already begun, with two well-known multimillionaires trying in July to win the headlines with test space flights in which they took part personally.

Lowering our gaze to the Earth, though, in looking back over the summer, apart from the coronavirus pandemic, the extreme weather events plaguing the planet leave a particular mark. July was a month of devastating and deadly floods for Germany and northern Europe. At the same time, a heatwave struck the United States and Canada, with temperatures above 50o C. Finally, in Siberia about one million hectares of forest were destroyed by fire, while in California this year the 2020 record of 4 million hectares of forest and farmland burned was exceeded. A recent study by the United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction (UNISDR) recorded a 74.5% increase in the number of major natural disasters on the planet in 2000–2019 compared with the previous 20 years, and according to the experts things are expected to get worse in future.
