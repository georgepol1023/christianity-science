Evolution or creation? (Part 2)

“And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that it was good.” (Genesis 1:25).

A great truth stated already in the 1st chapter of the Bible: all kinds of animals (and plants) were created different from one another from the beginning, and there never was, nor can there be, a passing from one kind to another, as the theory of evolution claims. The difference between kinds, and their stability, is secured by their genetic material (DNA), an extremely complex macromolecule found in the nucleus of every cell of every organism. In 1953 Watson and Crick presented the double helix model, which shows that DNA is like a “spiral staircase”, whose “steps” are made of pairs of four nitrogen-containing bases (adenine, thymine, guanine, cytosine). With these four “chemical letters” a remarkable encoding of all the information about each organism is achieved in its DNA molecule.

For building cells and tissues, for producing proteins, and for every function of every organism, the information needed is stored in, and retrieved from, the DNA molecule. Also, every new cell that is made contains an exact copy of the genetic material, and there are special cell mechanisms that prevent “copying errors”, so favouring the stability of the kind rather than its alteration and transformation into something else. It is also worth noting that anything that contains information has a complexity that is not chaotic but specified. Random processes can produce only random complexity, never specified complexity. Only a mind could do that. So a molecule such as DNA cannot have come from random mechanical processes. Another aspect of complexity is irreducible complexity.

Today we know that in living organisms there are organs and systems that work through the coordinated action of many specialised parts. This means they cannot have come from small gradual changes in some hypothetical earlier evolutionary forms. In other words, their complexity is irreducible: if even a small part of the organ or mechanism had been missing in some earlier evolutionary form, or had been slightly different from what we see today, the organ or mechanism would not have worked at all, and would have been rejected by natural selection. Typical examples are the eye, the blood-clotting mechanism and others. Another blow to the theory of evolution comes from palaeontology, the science that studies the form and life of organisms in earlier geological periods.

The basic source of information for palaeontology is fossils: the remains or traces of animals or plants found buried in rock. According to the theory of evolution, the various geological layers of rock should be full of fossils showing the various intermediate, transitional forms of organisms as they evolved from one kind to another. So, starting from the beginning of life on Earth, the fossil record should contain first simple single-celled organisms, then slightly more complex multicellular organisms with simple organs, with complexity gradually increasing until fully formed animals and plants appear. But the fossils show that while at first there were only simple single-celled organisms on Earth, quite suddenly there is an abrupt appearance (an “explosion”) of complex organisms.

From the Cambrian period onwards (thought to be 500–600 million years ago), all the known animal and plant types (phyla) that exist today appear abruptly, one after another. That is, in the fossil record, without any simpler intermediate evolutionary forms, complete organisms suddenly appear, fully developed and with complex systems (for example circulatory and nervous systems, complex skeletons, specialised organs for walking, swimming, catching food and so on) – which points to a great and sudden act of creation! From Darwin’s time (the 19th century) until today, when palaeontology has developed spectacularly and has far more fossils at its disposal, the problem of the lack of intermediate transitional forms in the fossil record (the so-called “missing links”) remains unsolved.

But the real “missing link” is not between ape and man but between God and man, and it is Jesus Christ, whose name anyone who calls on it can use to be reconnected with the heavenly Father: “behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation” (2 Corinthians 6:2).
