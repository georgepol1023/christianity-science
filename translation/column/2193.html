Does love make a family? (Part 2)

“Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it” (Psalm 127:1)

As we saw in our previous feature, in our time there is an attempt to redefine the family, as it has been known since the beginning of human history, by making “the existence of love” the only criterion and abolishing every other condition (sex, marital status). So any kind of union of people today claims the right to be called a family, since “love makes a family”, according to a popular slogan of our day.

The question is often asked whether it matters if two people who want to start a family are married. Here we should stress that marriage is not simply a ceremony or a document making a relationship official, but a commitment with a real role, because it creates an environment of stability and security that cannot be achieved in any other way. Research shows that children who grow up with parents who live together unmarried, or in some other kind of union, are more likely to show deviant behaviour and lower achievement, or even to be abused.

Could there be a family with two mums or two dads? It is important to mention that many sociologists hold that there is no such thing as vague “parenting”; there are specifically “fatherhood” and “motherhood”, because the ways in which a man and a woman interact with a child are very different (ways of communicating, playing, disciplining and so on). Men cannot give mothering, nor women fathering. A child needs a man as father and a woman as mother, from whom he receives distinct and complementary benefits.

So we see that, however vital and fundamental love is for a family to work and to thrive, it is not what defines a family and gives it its existence. After all, if parents with children for some reason stop loving each other, do they stop being a family?

“Charity [love] … doth not behave itself unseemly … rejoiceth not in iniquity, but rejoiceth in the truth” (1 Corinthians 13:4–6)

The “love” that many appeal to in order to define the family sadly lacks genuineness if it does not accept the truth that is objectively revealed; and love is absent where there is “that which is unseemly” (see Romans 1:27).

Dear readers, the time is the last time, and we are not surprised by the attempts to distort the truth, since this is prophesied to happen before the coming of the Lord. By altering the meaning of marriage and the family, the enemy of man’s soul clearly wants to hide and belittle the magnificent event that is soon to take place in heaven: the marriage of the Lamb (Revelation chapter 19). Indeed, according to the apostle Paul, marriage between two people was given by God as a foretaste of this eternal union of Christ with the church:

“This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church.” (Ephesians 5:32)

So let us fear God: on the one hand, so as not to try to alter an institution He established as a picture of a heavenly mystery; and on the other, so as to repent and be cleansed from sin, since the blood of Christ still flows and His arms are still open for eternal salvation:

“He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved; but he that believeth not shall be damned.” (Mark 16:16)
