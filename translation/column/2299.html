School bullying (Part 1)

“This know also, that in the last days perilous times shall come.” (2 Timothy 3:1)

Lately, almost every day, we hear news of more and more cases of what is called school bullying. It is deliberate, unprovoked, systematic and repeated aggressive behaviour or violence that causes physical and emotional pain.

Although it is not a new phenomenon, its recent rise, together with the ever younger school ages at which it appears, should at the very least be a cause of serious concern, which we will address in this column in the light of the Word of God.

The basic scenario is that on one side there are one or more people who feel “strong”. Finding fertile ground in the mildness of a shy character, in some physical defect, or in someone doing unexpectedly better than them, they systematically subject the victim to verbal or physical violence and to acts that degrade his personality.

Looking more deeply into the various aspects of the subject, it is not hard to see that the children who are victims of bullying suffer not only physical harassment but also enormous psychological pressure and attacks on their sense of their own worth. This often leads to feelings of guilt that may even develop into depression, whose symptoms will follow them into later life. Things are even worse in cases where they are forced to keep silent, afraid that the people they would look to for support will probably confirm their “defects” by criticising them for being different from the majority or for being unable to defend themselves.

On the other side, as for the children who bully, child psychologists speak of such behaviour as an attempt to fill some psychological emptiness, usually coming from their family environment, which they try to fill through acts that give them a feeling of superiority and dominance over others (the “weak”). This intimidating behaviour, though, often develops into delinquency and follows these people into adult life and into other settings (for example, the workplace).

A third group involved in school bullying are the children who see bullying happen but remain silent onlookers, either for fear that if they react they will be the next victims, or out of indifference, because it does not concern them directly.

To all this we must add that the ease of recording offered by the devices we use every day, such as smartphones, together with constant internet connection, has made it almost an established habit for such “entertaining” incidents to be posted on social media, multiplying the victim’s pain and the consequences of such public humiliation.

God willing, in our next article we will see what the Word of God, given by inspiration of God, proposes in the face of all this, for healing and for protection against such situations. Genuine Christianity is not theoretical knowledge but lived experience. That is exactly why, through such a personal relationship with God through Jesus Christ, a person can gain a right understanding of things through the knowledge of the Word of God, and at the same time receive the power of the Holy Spirit: on the one hand, as a potential victim, not to be overcome by bullying directed at him; and on the other, as a potential bully, to be transformed into a new person, “created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.” (Ephesians 2:10)
