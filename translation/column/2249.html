Navigating the ocean by the starry sky

In the old days sailors found their way across the ocean by watching the sky. As the seafarers of the past crossed the Atlantic between Europe and North America, they had to know at every moment their position on the map and their heading, so as to reach the port they wanted safely. They did this with the right geometrical instruments, and by knowing the main constellations and how they relate to the points of the compass.

Our life, too, is like a voyage. It has a definite beginning, an uncertain and stormy course, and a clear and longed-for destination. To cross this “dark ocean” we need, in the same way, the perfect guidance of Christ, and also the help of the workers of the church and of our other brothers and sisters.

So we can draw some very useful lessons from this comparison and apply them to our spiritual life.

1. “I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star.” (Revelation 22:16)

The Pole Star is part of the tail of the Little Bear, and it can easily be found in the night sky of the northern hemisphere by way of the constellation of the Great Bear. It stands out from all the stars because it lies right on the line of the imaginary axis around which our Earth turns. So it always shows us north, and also our latitude, through the angle it makes with the horizon.

In the same way, Christ is our one fixed point of reference, by which we can find our spiritual position and course. He is the unshakeable rock on which anyone can safely build his life.

2. “…and they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever.” (Daniel 12:3)

A navigator can find his exact position on the ocean by “bringing down” two or three stars. In practice he measures the angle a known star makes with the horizon and, knowing the exact time and date, looks up tables and handbooks from which he draws a straight line on the map. He does the same with another star, and the point where the two lines cross on the map is his position. The more stars he uses, the better his fix will be.

In our spiritual life, God has set the workers of the church as stars in our lives, to guide us through the one handbook, which is the Word of God. As we try to find our way, they will teach us the course we must follow so that we can travel safely to the eternal harbour.

3. “These are spots in your feasts of charity, …feeding themselves, …trees whose fruit withereth, without fruit, … wandering stars” (Jude 12–13)

Unlike the fixed stars, the planets cannot serve as a fixed point of reference, because their position in the sky is constantly changing. (The Greek word for planet means “wanderer”.)

In the same way, on our spiritual journey some people will try – people whom God has not set in place and who do not work in the service of the local body of the church – to steer us according to their own opinion and for their own glory, with the sole aim of running us onto spiritual “rocks and reefs”. They seem to be stars, but in reality they are wanderers. They look like sheep but are wolves, and the Lord’s advice is: “Ye shall know them by their fruits.”

So we thank God, because on this spiritual journey we are not alone. We have our Christ, who guides us; the Word of the Lord as a compass to keep us on course; and the workers of the Gospel, who counsel us.

“Remember them which have the rule over you, who have spoken unto you the word of God: whose faith follow, considering the end of their conversation.” (Hebrews 13:7)
