Scientists with a deep faith in God (Part 2)

From the mathematician and physicist Blaise Pascal to the chemist and microbiologist Louis Pasteur.

Contrary to the prevailing view of our time, which holds that serious scientists must be atheists, in this series we study and present the lives of great scientists who shaped the development of scientific knowledge and at the same time had a very deep faith in the Creator God of the Bible.

Blaise Pascal (1623–1662)

Blaise Pascal was a French mathematician, physicist, inventor, philosopher and writer, whose contribution reaches into both the scientific and the religious world.

Pascal studied the motion of fluids and the development of hydraulic systems, which revolutionised engineering. His contribution to probability theory was also enormous, culminating in “Pascal’s triangle”, with its applications in algebra. He was an inventor as well: at only 19 he designed and built the first mechanical calculator.

Pascal’s life, however, took a dramatic turn when, at the age of 31, he experienced a deep religious conversion that reshaped his worldview and his approach to his scientific work. This conversion took place during what Pascal described as the “Night of Fire”.

That night of 23 November 1654, while he was at his brother’s house, he wrote a note that included the sentence: “I did all I could to find God through reason, and I failed. Then I felt an unexpected grace in my heart.” He describes how he suddenly felt an intense inner fire, a sense of spiritual transformation: peace, joy, certainty that God exists, and an awareness of His love. This experience was decisive for the rest of his life.

After that night, Pascal devoted himself to God. Pascal’s most famous religious work, the Pensées (Thoughts), is a collection of reflections on human nature, on the misery of man without God, and on the rational justification of faith. In this unfinished masterpiece Pascal presents his famous “wager” argument, which held that “it is a rational decision to believe in God, because the possible gains of faith far outweigh the losses.”

For Pascal, science was a way of approaching the mysteries of God, but it was faith that allowed a person to understand them fully.

Louis Pasteur (1822–1895)

Louis Pasteur (1822–1895) was a French chemist and microbiologist, famous for discoveries that laid the foundations of microbiology.

His main scientific contribution lies in the following three achievements:

  • He developed pasteurisation, a method of sterilising food and drink.
  • He proved that germs cause disease, overturning the theory of spontaneous generation.
  • He invented vaccines for rabies and leptospirosis, saving thousands of lives.

In short, he linked science with health, laying the foundations of modern microbiology and epidemiology.

Throughout his life, Pasteur’s faith deeply influenced his worldview, as he saw his scientific work as revealing the divine order in nature.

Brought up in a devout, believing family, Pasteur kept his faith all his life, often expressing his trust in the providence of God and in the harmony of creation.

Some well-known sayings of Pasteur about his deep faith in God have gone down in history:

  • “The order and beauty of nature are an irrefutable argument for the Creator”, showing that observing the world led his thoughts to God.
  • “God is the source of all truth”, declaring his complete confidence that truth, both scientific and moral, comes from God.
  • “I pray before I begin my experiments, because His guidance is necessary”, bearing witness that his faith was active and personal, not a formality.

We could speak at length about many other great scientific figures of the past whose faith in God and deep scientific knowledge went side by side, peacefully and complementing each other, throughout their lives.

But above all they understood that the grace of God was upon them, enabling them to understand what others could not: “And he hath filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship;” (Exodus 35:31)
