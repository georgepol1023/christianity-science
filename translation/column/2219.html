The creation of the world (Part 1)

The creation of the world has always occupied people of every age. From the ancient ideas about the origin of the cosmos to the modern scientific knowledge of cosmology, many hypotheses have been put forward.

Some claimed that in the beginning was chaos. Others told us of gods fighting one another, and parts of the heaven and the earth arising from their struggles. Others held that the universe is an eternal entity with no beginning and no end, so that it is not subject to the principle of causality, and also so that the theory of evolution has room to work. Modern cosmologists maintain that the universe came into being from an extremely dense and hot state about 13.8 billion years ago.

So what is the truth in the end? And above all, what does the Word of God tell us?

In the 1st chapter of the book of Genesis, in fewer than 30 verses, the Holy Spirit describes the whole act of creation to us in a way that is both understandable and scientifically correct. But to understand what is written more fully, we should bear in mind the following three points:

  1. “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.” (Genesis 1:1).

This first verse clearly states that there was a beginning to the creation of the universe, and that God is responsible for that beginning. We could say that this verse takes us from the first moment of creation to the point in time when all the stars and galaxies have been formed, our solar system has taken shape, and the Earth is in its early phase.

  1. Initial conditions and point of observation.

“And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.” (Genesis 1:2).

When we carry out any physical experiment, we must first define its initial conditions and also the point of observation. If we change these two things, we often record completely different measurements, although in reality we are carrying out the same experiment.

In the same way, here God makes sure to tell us the initial conditions at the beginning of the account, when the Earth was:

  • void (with no life on it),
  • without form (with no marked features, and no great depressions or uplifts of its crust) and
  • dark (as light could not get through the primordial atmosphere, which was full of dense greenhouse gases).

It also points out that we will watch the sequence of events from the surface of the Earth’s waters, looking up at the surrounding atmosphere (that is, where the Spirit of God is moving), and not from some undefined point in space.

  1. The order of events in time.

The account’s attention is turned mainly to the person of the Creator and to the order of events in time, rather than to the absolute length of the period of creation.

We have many reasons to believe that each day of creation was not an earthly 24-hour day but rather a period of time. Among other things, there are serious indications in this direction from the following points:

  • In many places Scripture speaks of the relativity of time (Psalm 90:4 and 2 Peter 3:8)
  • The events that take place on the 6th day are very hard to fit into an earthly 24 hours (Genesis chapters 1–2)
  • The 7th day has not yet ended and has lasted more than 6,000 years (Genesis 2:3 and Hebrews 4:3–10)

Having laid down the basic points that will help us understand the account better, in our next feature we will describe each day of creation in detail, and at the end we will marvel at the greatness of God and at the wisdom of what is written!

“O LORD, how manifold are thy works! in wisdom hast thou made them all: the earth is full of thy riches.” (Psalm 104:24)
