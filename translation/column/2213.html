Science and scientism

We often hear the belief strongly defended that the only way to discover the truth about anything is to use scientific methods. This view is called scientism, and it is very important to know how it differs from science, which covers all the methods of observing and studying natural phenomena in order to draw conclusions and understand the mechanisms behind them.

Scientism is essentially a philosophical position according to which truth can be reached only through the empirical data of science, so everything that happens around us must be explained in mechanistic ways. As a result, anything metaphysical must be rejected as a matter of principle, since there is no other reality apart from the material one we perceive.

Many scientists oppose the absoluteness of this position, because it dogmatically rejects as non-existent anything that cannot be studied by scientific methods, and so in effect undermines its own objectivity. For example, how far can feelings like love, friendship, philotimo (the Greek sense of honour and generosity) and altruism, and the actions that come from them, be explained scientifically? Does that mean they do not exist and have no value in human life? What is more, according to scientism everything moral must be set aside on the altar of science, and so the way is open for practices such as abortion, eugenics and others.

In the 17th and 18th centuries, with the movement of the Enlightenment, the view became established that absolute truth can be determined and known exclusively through rational thought and scientific methods. Nothing should be taken as an axiom, and every truth that exists should be provable. Later, the Principia Mathematica (1910–1913) of Russell and Whitehead was the most famous attempt to find a set of fundamental assumptions from which all the mathematical truths that govern the world could be derived.

And suddenly everything changed. Atomic and subatomic particles were discovered, whose behaviour could not be explained by the existing models of classical physics. So quantum mechanics was formulated, one of whose basic principles is Heisenberg’s “uncertainty principle”, according to which it is impossible to determine both the position and the momentum of a particle at the same time.

The second blow to the foundations of extreme scientific determinism came a little later with the publication of the “incompleteness theorem” (1931) by Kurt Gödel, who worked on the provability of mathematical principles and showed that logic inevitably rests on certain unprovable principles, since it is impossible to show that all principles can be proved. In short, Gödel proved, in a strictly mathematical way, that logic is unable to reach the whole of the truth. The universe is a closed system in which, with exclusively deterministic methods, however well developed, we can never know absolute truth.

So if science itself admits that it is unable to make absolute truth known, how long will we refuse to look for it in the One who created the universe? Indeed, according to another fundamental principle of physics (the principle of causality), this Creator must transcend the material world and be a Person who is all-wise and almighty. So the very principles of the modern natural sciences tell us that we are wrong to try to decide whether God exists on the basis of scientific methods!

So if you thirst to know the One who made you and the eternal plans He has for you personally, there is a way: kneel down in your room and call on the name of Jesus Christ. He is the One who, by His sacrifice on the cross and His resurrection, opened the way to the Father and to eternal life:

“I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.” (John 14:6)
