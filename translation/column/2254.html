The good student – and disciple!

As another school and university year opens before us, we want first of all to wish all students a good and blessed school or university year, with progress and success.

At the same time, all of us are called to be disciples of Jesus Christ. (In Greek the same word, mathitis, means both a student and a disciple.) In this article we will see that many of the qualities a school student needs in order to succeed, and in the end to get into the course of his choice, are the same as those a disciple of Christ needs in order to grow every day and in the end to enter abundantly into the Kingdom of Heaven.

Diligence:

One of the most basic qualities for success is diligence: steadiness in reading, studying and schoolwork, whatever the outside circumstances. Very often, as teachers, we say that success has less to do with intelligence than with diligence and hard work.

In the same way, in spiritual things, the apostle Peter tells us: “Wherefore the rather, brethren, give diligence to make your calling and election sure” (2 Peter 1:10). Salvation is a divine gift, yet one that we must work out with holy fear (Philippians 2:12). The Christian life is a diligent, daily and loving relationship with the person of our Lord Jesus Christ. With diligence, every day, I bend over the Word of God to hear what He wants to teach me, and I kneel in prayer to speak to Him.

Focus and self-denial:

A student who aims to get into a top course and to excel must also deny himself some passing pleasures and some of his rest to reach his goal. There will be many moments when he has to choose between having a good time and enjoying himself on the one hand, and studying and keeping to himself on the other; and if he is not devoted and focused on his goal, he will not reach the finish successfully.

In the same way, the apostle Paul gives us his own testimony: “…this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before, I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.” (Philippians 3:13–14). He had a specific goal and had devoted his whole life to it. Nothing could draw him away from that focus, and no distraction was enough to disturb his course. “…let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us, looking unto Jesus…” (Hebrews 12:1–2).

A good teacher:

The Lord Jesus Christ told us: “The disciple is not above his master: but every one that is perfect shall be as his master.” (Luke 6:40). And this is true in both the natural and the spiritual world.

Choosing a good teacher is crucial to a student’s success. A good teacher inspires, leads, guides and encourages. He stands beside the student so that he can unfold all his gifts. He is a model for him to imitate and a guide for him to follow. But his example and his own ability also set an upper limit on the student’s progress. The student cannot surpass the teacher.

We thank God because in the church our teacher is the Holy Spirit, and He has set ministries and gifts “…for the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ: till we all come in the unity of the faith, and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fulness of Christ” (Ephesians 4:12–13)

So from our hearts, as the “Christianity & Science” team, we wish all young people a good school and university year, but above all:

“Ye therefore, beloved… grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. To him be glory both now and for ever. Amen.”

(2 Peter 3:17–18)
