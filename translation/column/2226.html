The creation of the world (Part 2)

In the first chapter of the book of Genesis we have the account of the creation of the world out of nothing, as it is written: “Through faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear.” (Hebrews 11:3)

In the 1st part of this article we dwelt mainly on the first two verses, where God has already created the whole of the heaven and the Earth, and where we are given the initial conditions and the point from which the act of creation is observed.

In the 2nd part we will look, simply and clearly, at the events that take place on each day, and marvel at how fully they agree with modern scientific findings, even though they were written 3,500 years ago.

Day 1: “Let there be light”

The dense atmosphere of greenhouse gases covering the Earth does not let the light of the sun pass through and light up the Earth’s surface. During the 1st day the opaque atmosphere becomes translucent, and day is divided from night. So we have the alternation of darkness and light, without the lights in the firmament being visible yet.

Day 2: “Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters”

An early water cycle is created, with evaporation and condensation, as the various layers of the atmosphere that are necessary for the climate and for sustaining life are formed.

Day 3: “Let the waters … be gathered together” and “Let the earth bring forth grass”

By the Word of the Lord, great geomorphological changes begin through the movement of the lithospheric plates; dry land appears, the masses of water drain away accordingly, and the ocean basins are formed.

Everything is now ready to receive the phenomenon of life, which only the life-giving Word of the Creator can give. So vegetation springs up – grass with its seed, and fruit trees appear, all after their kind. Not an evolutionary natural process, but a divine and wonderful creation!

Day 4: “Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven”

Photosynthesis, taking in CO2 and producing oxygen, speeds up the further clearing of the atmosphere, turning it from translucent to fully transparent. As a result, all the heavenly bodies and the lights in the sky, which had already been created from the 1st verse of the account, become visible to the naked eye.

Day 5: “Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl”

The act of creation now continues in the sea, from which the fish, all the creatures of the sea, and also the birds of the air miraculously spring, with the command to be fruitful and multiply through reproduction.

Day 6: “Let the earth bring forth the living creature” and “Let us make man in our image, after our likeness”

Now the creating Word turns to the land, and the living creatures of the land appear: reptiles, mammals and all the cattle.

At the close of the 6th day the Holy Trinity works together to create man from the dust of the ground. And whereas for everything else the Lord sent forth His Word, man the Lord formed with His own hands, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life.

So as we follow this whole sequence of creation and its wonderful design, all our heart can do is cry out:

“O LORD, how manifold are thy works! in wisdom hast thou made them all: the earth is full of thy riches.” (Psalm 104:24)
