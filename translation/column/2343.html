The search for intelligence and the meaning of information (Part 1)

How do we tell whether something was created by intelligence or is the product of chance? Where is the dividing line that tells us whether an intelligent creator or blind coincidence lies behind a formation?

Our everyday experience, through simple observations, can lead us straight to a sure conclusion.

When we see twigs scattered across a yard, we take it for granted that their random arrangement is due to the wind that was blowing earlier. But when we see the same twigs arranged to form a nest, we are certain that some little creature built it.

When we notice the ripples on a sandy beach, we understand that the repeated movement of the waves created the pattern. But when we see four letters carved into the sand – “HELP” – we are certain that there is a shipwrecked person somewhere near.

The conclusion, then, is that natural processes can create complexity, but that complexity is chaotic. At best, perhaps, a pattern may be formed. In both cases, though, the formations produced do not arise to serve any purpose.

By contrast, wherever there is complexity that is specified, or wherever we see a formation, however simple, that serves some purpose or contains information, it can only be the product of some intelligence. Indeed, by studying the formation in each case, we are able to sketch the characteristics and intentions of whoever created it.

One branch of science where this can be clearly seen is archaeology. Even the simplest cave drawings, where a few strokes depict the shapes of people and animals, are recognised not as random formations but as pictures made by human hands, from which many conclusions can be drawn about the living conditions of the people of that time, their daily habits, their social structure and so on. The presence of intelligence is even more obvious in more complex archaeological finds, whether buildings or, especially, inscriptions. Even if these cannot be understood at first, the specified complexity of their shapes and markings leaves no doubt that the find contains a message with specific information; so we are speaking not of a random formation but of the work of an intelligent person.

On the other side, the search for extraterrestrial intelligence through various SETI programmes (Search for Extra-Terrestrial Intelligence) has failed to detect radio signals or any other kind of evidence showing that life exists on another planet or star system. The SETI programmes are based on trying to receive electromagnetic radiation that carries traces of signals transmitted by civilisations on other planets. From time to time, and especially since the 1950s, signals have been detected that were thought to be significant, but in the end it was found that they were either some natural pattern or a chance event that was never repeated. In other words, no electromagnetic signal has ever been found to have the features that would confirm it was not a random natural phenomenon but the product of an intelligent being.

For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made” (Romans 1:19–20)
