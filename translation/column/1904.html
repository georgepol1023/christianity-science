Addiction to and dependence on “smartphones” (Part 2)

“Their sorrows shall be multiplied that hasten after another god…” (Psalm 16:4)

26 June has been established internationally as the World Day against Drugs and every kind of dependence. Despite the warnings, the good wishes and the exhortations, the list is sadly growing very fast.

In 2018, 55 new psychoactive substances were detected in the EU, bringing to 730 the total number of substances monitored by the European agency. But besides substances, dependence is also caused by alcohol, gambling and, in recent years, with very rapidly growing reach, smartphones and the internet! Indeed, the rise in dependence on phones and the internet is being seen together with a fall in the frequency of other forms of dependence…

It took 100 years for the harmful effects of smoking to be established, and 50 years for the carcinogenic effect of asbestos to be documented. Within just 5 years of the international scientific community beginning to investigate the harm from the electromagnetic radiation (EMR) of smartphones, we sadly had the first bad results. When a phone is used, 80% of the radiation emitted reaches 5 cm into the brain when the device is held against the ear. The harmful effect is certainly stronger in children, and it is classified as possibly carcinogenic for the young. EMR from smartphones interferes with the body’s natural electrical system and disturbs sleep and the immune and endocrine systems. Finally, long exposure to EMR has been associated with chronic pain, depression, chronic fatigue syndrome and even Alzheimer’s disease.

It is not the first time that experts have warned that we are addicted to phones and social media, and that we must do something for our own good. Children who use mobile phones expose their brains to radiation and put their health at risk. This risk to children’s health is not much lower than that from tobacco or alcohol.

Dr Gerard Hyland, a biophysicist at the University of Warwick and twice nominated for the Nobel Prize in Medicine, said: “As a parent I would be extremely cautious about letting my children use a mobile phone, even for a very short time. My advice would be to avoid mobile phones.”

Everyone recommends setting some hours of the day (only a few at first) when you will not use your phone.

Unexpected helpers here are the “great” Steve Jobs (Apple), Bill Gates (Microsoft) and many others, who let their children have a smartphone only at 14. What is more, no phone ever appeared at the evening meal, and in the last hours of the day they encouraged their children to listen to music or to talk with their parents about subjects of general interest. They knew better…

But why is it so hard to follow the experts’ advice? What, in the end, is the mechanism of dependence, and what is the best solution to this complex and so serious problem? God willing, we will speak about this in the next issue.
