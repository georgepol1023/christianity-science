Evolution or creation? (Part 4)

“If ye continue in my word, then are ye my disciples indeed; and ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free.” (John 8:31–32)

A very common argument for the theory of evolution is the existence of so-called “vestigial organs”. These are organs found in various animals and in man that give the impression of being remnants of organs that fell behind in development, and do not seem to perform any function in the body. Supporters of the theory of evolution believe they are “leftovers” of organs that were functional in ancestral evolutionary forms.

A typical example is the coccyx, a bone at the base of the human spine which gives the impression that there was once a tail there that withered away in the course of evolution and disappeared, leaving behind a remnant. In reality, however, the coccyx is an attachment point for the muscles that allow upright posture, walking and other supporting functions. It is also often said that the appendix seems to be a useless projection of the intestine. In reality it is part of the lymphatic system and helps remove waste from the body’s fluids.

More generally, it keeps being found that in various organisms many organs that until recently were thought “vestigial” do in fact perform at least one useful function for the body. In man especially, the number of supposed “vestigial organs” fell from 180 (listed by 1890) to 0 in 1999!

A related subject is that of “homologous organs”: organs which in different kinds of organisms show great similarities in structure and form, even though they may perform different functions. A typical example of homologous organs is the forelimbs of various animals, which have the same basic series of bones as one another and as the human arm: humerus, radius and ulna, carpal bones and, in some cases, metacarpal bones and finger bones.

According to supporters of the theory of evolution, such similarities in structure and form show that there was a common ancestor. But this position is entirely arbitrary, and could be considered no stronger than the view that homologous organs are signs not of evolution but of creation. For it is reasonable that a common creating Mind would use the same basic design to build similar structures in organisms. For example, the fact that a car and a lorry have “homologous organs” (wheels, steering wheel, engine and so on) is due to the same basic design being used for all vehicles, not to some kind of evolution. What is more, biology today reports that there are organs and structures described as “homologous” that are formed during development from different groups of embryonic cells in different kinds of organisms. For example, the forelimbs of the lizard and of man are formed from different parts of the embryo’s trunk.

Finally, another argument for the theory of evolution rests on genetic similarity, the most popular example being that human DNA is 96% to 98% or more similar to the DNA of various kinds of ape. First we should say that the percentage of genetic similarity found varies from study to study between 87% and 96%. Even so, genetic similarity does not seem to lead to clear evolutionary conclusions. For example, the chimpanzee is thought to have the greatest genetic similarity to man, yet the gibbon, with less genetic similarity, is more like man in form as regards walking on two legs. And the gorilla, also less genetically similar to man, has particular genes that are more like human ones than the chimpanzee’s are. Moreover, since human DNA is made up of about 3 billion “chemical letters”, even if the similarity with an ape’s DNA reached 99%, the 1% difference would amount to about 30 million “chemical letters”. But it is impossible for such extensive differences in genetic material to have come about by evolution through mutations, as we showed in our previous feature.
