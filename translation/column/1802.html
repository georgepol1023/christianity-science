The historical evidence for the Resurrection of Jesus Christ (Part 2)

Continuing our journey through the Resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ, we note:

4. The tomb is empty!

  • This fact was never disputed, not even by Christ’s enemies. It was taken for granted, and easy for people of that time to check. Some attempts were made to give a different explanation of what happened. And all these attempts did nothing more than confirm, on the one hand, that the tomb really was empty, and on the other, that the evidence that it was empty was far too strong for anyone to dare claim the opposite.
  • The fact that the grave clothes – tightly wound and stuck together with sticky aromatic substances – were empty, and that the napkin that had covered Christ’s head was wrapped together separately a little way off, shows that the body had come out of the grave clothes by itself, and calmly.

5. Attempts to explain the empty tomb

a. Swoon: Christ did not die on the cross but lost consciousness. He was buried alive and came round after many hours.

Answer: We saw above that Jesus was undoubtedly dead. But even if He had not been, after the ordeal He went through and the wounds He had, without medical help and bound in grave clothes with strongly toxic spices, He would more likely have died than recovered. And even if He had recovered, it would have been impossible for a weakened man covered in wounds to free himself from the grave clothes, roll away the huge stone, overcome the Roman guard, walk several kilometres and present himself to the disciples.

b. Jesus’ body was stolen: Someone stole the body from the tomb during the night.

Answer: Jesus’ enemies had no motive, and His frightened disciples had no power, to do such a thing. Anyone trying to steal the body would have had either to overcome the Roman guard first, or hope that all the guards would fall asleep (which is impossible), and then roll away the stone in complete silence and steal the body in haste – not take the trouble to remove the grave clothes stuck to it, let alone fold them. The story of the stolen body even led the Roman soldiers into the contradiction of spreading the word that they had fallen asleep (though they knew this was punishable by death), while still knowing that those who stole the body were, supposedly, the disciples.

c. Hallucinations: The disciples seeing Christ risen was a mass hallucination.

Answer: Hallucinations happen to particular kinds of people, when they are in a particular psychological state. Hallucinations are also very individual and extremely subjective. The New Testament says that more than five hundred people of different temperaments and in different psychological states came into contact with the risen Christ – conditions that do not favour hallucinations – and all their testimonies agree. Nor was there fertile ground for hallucinations, because no one expected to see the Lord risen. He Himself, after all, had difficulty convincing them of His Resurrection.
