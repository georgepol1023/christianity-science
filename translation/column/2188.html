Does love make a family? (Part 1)

“Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female, and said, For this cause shall a man leave father and mother, and shall cleave to his wife: and they twain shall be one flesh?” (Matthew 19:4–5)

These words from the lips of Jesus Christ, which show how God made the first marriage, are perhaps more relevant than ever, since we live at a time when there is a push to redefine marriage and the family, two institutions that are fundamental to the life of every person individually and to society as a whole.

The first word on an institution can only belong to the one who established it; and in the case of marriage and the family, that is God Himself, who from the beginning determined their form and make-up.

From the account in the 2nd chapter of Genesis we learn that God gave the man a woman (and not a second man) to complement him and complete him as a person. The woman is someone with whom the man can be united physically because of their matching anatomy, that is, the complementarity of the reproductive organs. Union cannot be achieved in any other way. These are the maker’s specifications. Anything else is outside the specifications; it is against nature, and is not what the human body was made for.

With the first marriage God also gives the command to have children: “Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it” (Genesis 1:28). So with the arrival of children, what we call a family is formed. The Bible is full of references to the family and instructions to its members, and it also presents the blessings that go with this institution established by God (see, for example, Ephesians 5:22 to 6:4 and Psalm 128).

It is important, then, to explore from various angles whether anything that differs from these meanings given to marriage and the family can be recognised as of equal standing.

From the beginning of history, various forms of marriage are found in human societies, but without exception they were always between a man and a woman. Of course, we also find cases where homosexual relationships were formed, but these were never recognised as equal to marriage, so as to be made officially lawful, with the having of children following and so the creation of some kind of “family”.

Many times in recent decades there have been attempts to give homosexual behaviour a genetic basis. But extensive research published about 3 years ago in the journal Science “showed clearly that there is no gay gene; there is no genetic explanation for sexual orientation”[1].

Finally, we should mention that even if the theory of evolution were true, natural selection (the driving mechanism of evolution, which favours the organism that is better adapted to its environment and leaves more offspring) would dictate heterosexual union, and the benefits of the heterosexual family, as the only way to perpetuate and improve the species; while any homosexual behaviour would work against these aims and would be rejected by the “sieve” of natural selection.

We conclude, then, that a same-sex union is not supported biblically, historically, genetically, or even in evolutionary terms. But what if there is “love”? Do we then have the right to redefine marriage and the family? Does “love make a family”? We will answer these questions in the 2nd part of our feature.
