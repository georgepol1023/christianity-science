The inspiration and inerrancy of Holy Scripture

As the crowning point of the proof of the historical and archaeological reliability of the Old and New Testaments comes the finding that Holy Scripture contains no errors at all, because it is a book given by inspiration of God; that is, its writing is the result of inspiration that comes from God.

That Holy Scripture is given by inspiration of God implies that it is also without error, and this is a question on which every reader is called to take a position. Denying the inspiration and inerrancy of Holy Scripture breeds questions and doubts, which begin to lead to the rejection of one verse and passage after another, and in the end to the rejection of the whole of Holy Scripture; for proving the existence of even one error would act like pulling one thread from a woollen jumper, which inevitably unravels the whole jumper. For the purpose of Holy Scripture is to reveal to us the character of God, and if God lies, then the whole of Holy Scripture collapses.

First of all, let us make clear that inspiration refers to the original handwritten texts and not to any particular translation or copy, although the copies of the original manuscripts have been shown to be more than 99% faithful to the original texts.

The first and very obvious sign that there are no errors is that all the writers, as their writings show, set a very high bar of objectivity and reliability.

Moreover, the Lord Jesus Christ Himself guarantees that Holy Scripture is given by inspiration of God. So whoever denies this quality of Holy Scripture indirectly attacks the reliability and authority of the person of Jesus Christ. To begin with, He Himself divided the Old Testament into the law, the psalms and the prophets (Luke 24:44–45), or the law and the prophets (Matt. 5:17 & 7:12), since the book of Psalms has a strongly prophetic character and can be counted among the prophetic books (indeed the Israelites often said “the prophets” to mean the whole Old Testament apart from the Pentateuch).

As for the Old Testament, for example:

  • The Pentateuch (Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy) is what the Israelites call the Law, or Torah in Hebrew. The Pentateuch states in many places that it was given by God:

    “And the tables were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, graven upon the tables” – Exod. 32:16

    Besides, the books of Leviticus and Numbers begin by saying that what is written in them are words spoken by God Himself. There is no direct claim of inspiration in Genesis, but since Genesis is one of the five books of the Law, it carries the same authority that Jesus Christ gives to “the Law”. Jesus Christ also used verses from Genesis, saying that they are “words of God” (Matt. 19:4–6).
  • As for the inspiration of the prophets, it is shown by the phrase “Thus saith the Lord”, which comes before most of the written prophecies of the Old Testament. But there are other references too, the most striking being Zechariah, who, as one of the last prophets of the Old Testament, says that the words of the prophets before him were the words of God Himself (Zech. 7:12), something the apostle Peter also confirms (2 Pet. 1:21).
  • The inspiration of the Psalms and of the other poetic books of the Old Testament is shown in the same way. Indeed, the Lord Jesus Christ and the apostles state expressly that the Psalms are words of the Holy Spirit (e.g. Mark 12:36 & Acts 1:16)

As for the New Testament, for example:

  • The Lord Jesus Christ Himself promised beforehand that the writing of the New Testament would be given by inspiration of God, through the working of the Holy Spirit:

    “But the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you” – John 14:26

    So the four Gospels remind us of the life of Christ and all that He taught, while whatever further teaching we need we receive through the epistles.
  • The apostle Peter, moreover, counts the epistles of Paul among “the scriptures” (2 Pet. 3:15–16).
  • The crowning verse on the inspiration of the whole of Holy Scripture is:

    “All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: that the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works” – 2 Tim. 3:16–17

As for inerrancy, there is no verse that states expressly that Holy Scripture is without error in all it says. The same is true, however, of the doctrine of the Trinity, which is nonetheless clearly taught in Holy Scripture. In the same way inerrancy follows from the fact that, as we have seen, Holy Scripture is a book given by inspiration of God; that is, what it says are the words of God, and God does not lie, nor can He make a mistake: His words are true. In other words, the inerrancy of Holy Scripture follows logically from its inspiration, and so to question it is an indirect charge against the character of God.

The inerrancy of Holy Scripture matters greatly, among other things, for events such as the story of Adam and Eve, of Noah and the Flood, of Sodom and Gomorrah, of Jonah and others, to which Jesus Christ Himself referred, confirming that they are real events and not symbols.

A particular pillar of the inerrancy of Holy Scripture is the archaeological and historical confirmation of the events it describes (as we saw in earlier articles), and also the fact that it does not err in any statement that falls within the field of modern science and that could not have been checked with the means available when each book of Holy Scripture was written

It is also very important to say that the inspiration and inerrancy of Holy Scripture are not a recent invention of some of its keen supporters, but a truth stated from the beginning by respected Church Fathers and theologians such as Augustine, Thomas Aquinas, John Chrysostom and others.

The inspiration and inerrancy of Holy Scripture show that every word in Holy Scripture is not placed there by chance but has its own particular significance. Indeed, Jesus Christ said: “For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled.” (Matt. 5:18). And if this holds for the Old Testament, how much more for the New Testament.

To sum up, Holy Scripture is indeed a book given by inspiration of God, and precisely because it is given by inspiration of God it is also without error, since God cannot err or lie. Besides, those who question that the whole of Holy Scripture is given by inspiration of God are obliged to admit that they are unable:

  • To separate the passages that are given by inspiration of God from those that are not
  • To set out the criteria by which that separation is made

So if questioning the inspiration and inerrancy of Holy Scripture is a starting point that can have harmful results for a person's spiritual search, on the other hand, affirming the inspiration and inerrancy of Holy Scripture is a blessed starting point that leads to receiving the Word of God into the heart, and so to salvation through Jesus Christ.
