Haeckel's forgery
Ernst Haeckel (Ernst Haeckel, 1834 – 1919) was a distinguished German biologist and professor of anatomy, and a keen supporter of Darwin's theory of the evolution of species and of the materialist view of the world in general.

What Haeckel is best known for is the “fundamental biogenetic law”, or “Haeckel's law”, or the “law of recapitulation”, according to which “ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny” (“ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny”). This means that an embryo, as it develops during pregnancy (ontogeny), passes in brief through all the stages of the evolution of its species (phylogeny).

For example, a human embryo begins its life at the stage of a single-celled organism (a fertilised egg), just as life on Earth supposedly began when a cell was formed by chance. Next, the embryo has some folds like gills and passes through the “fish stage”, since all organisms supposedly evolved from ancestors that lived in water. The embryo also shows a tail, so it is thought to pass through the evolutionary stages when man's ancestors had a tail. Later, when it has developed further, it has a form that looks like an ape and at the final stage it takes the form of a human being.

To support this theory, Haeckel published pictures of the embryos of a fish, a salamander, a tortoise, a chicken, a pig, a cow, a rabbit and a human being, which he set side by side at three stages of their development. These drawings seemed to show not only that each embryo lives through the evolution of its species in brief, but also that the embryos of all these animals at the early stages of their development are very similar, which according to Haeckel bore witness to the existence of a common evolutionary ancestor from which all species came.

But it was very soon revealed that Haeckel's biogenetic law, or law of recapitulation, was a fraud. Two scientists of Haeckel's time, Rütimeyer (Rütimeyer) and His (His), published scientific papers revealing that the pictures Haeckel had published did not show the real forms of the embryos, but were falsifications and arbitrary drawings, in which he left out basic differences in the shape of the embryos and selectively exaggerated certain similarities. Indeed, Haeckel was known to have a talent for drawing.

In the following years many other scientists exposed Haeckel's falsifications and forgeries, so that he himself was forced to reply with a full-page article in the German newspaper “Volks-Zeitung” of 29/12/1908, in which he admits that he forged and falsified the pictures, but, as he says, altered only 6 to 8%, and that hundreds of other scientists do the same! And instead of a scientific answer, he attacks with insults those who exposed his frauds.

But apart from the fact that Haeckel's drawings were forged, the biogenetic law also collapses in the light of modern scientific knowledge about each stage of the embryo's development:

  • A fertilised egg is completely different from a single-celled organism such as an amoeba.
  • The folds of the embryo have nothing to do with gills, since they have no slits or openings, as a fish's gills do so that it can breathe in water. On the contrary, they are small bony ridges from which various organs will form (jaws, larynx and others). Besides, the embryo does not breathe using the amniotic fluid, so it has no need of gills as fish do.
  • The embryo's “tail” is simply a protruding part that is absorbed as the embryo's body develops. It has no similarity to the formation and function of an animal's tail.
  • Accurate drawings show that at no stage of its development does the human embryo look like a “little ape”.

The consequences of accepting such a theory are also especially serious. On the one hand, making man the same as the animals tries to establish atheistic ideas; on the other, if it is assumed that an embryo during pregnancy is not a human being from the start but passes through the stage of a fish, an ape and so on, then there is nothing to hold anyone back from an abortion – that is, the killing of a human life!

So Haeckel's theory collapsed at the beginning of the 20th century (by 1910 it was already considered out of date), while full proof of the extensive falsification of the data came thanks to the thorough work of the British evolutionary biologist Michael Richardson in the 1990s. But what makes Haeckel's case unique in the history of science is that this false theory was included in textbooks, in which parts of it can still be found today!


Vestigial organs
At this point someone might wonder about the existence of so-called “vestigial organs”. These are structures and organs found in various animals and in man too, which give the impression of being remnants of organs that lagged behind in development, and do not seem to perform any function in the body. Supporters of the theory of evolution believe that they are “leftovers” of organs that worked in ancestral evolutionary forms. A typical example is the coccyx, a bone at the base of the human spine that gives the impression that there was once a tail there, which wasted away in the course of evolution and disappeared, leaving a remnant behind. Likewise, the appendix seems to be a useless outgrowth of the intestine.

But the idea that “vestigial organs” support the theory of evolution is overturned by the following facts:

  • More and more it is being discovered that in various organisms many organs thought to be “vestigial” do in fact perform at least one useful function for the body. For example, the appendix is part of the lymphatic system and helps to remove impurities from the body's fluids. And the coccyx is an attachment point for the muscles that allow upright posture, walking and other supporting functions (and every case of a person born with some kind of “tail” has been found to be due either to a birth defect or to some kind of spina bifida). In general, the number of vestigial organs in man fell from 180 (as listed up to 1890) to 0 in 1999!
  • Losing a whole structure, or even just the function of a structure, means a loss of genetic information from the genes. But such an event is the opposite of the mechanism by which evolution supposedly proceeds, according to which genetic information is added at random (through mutations), leading to more complex organisms.

Homologous organs
A related question is that of “homologous organs”, that is, organs which in the various kinds of organisms show great similarities in their structure and form even though they may perform different functions. A typical example of homologous organs is the forelimbs of various animals , which, among themselves and compared with the human arm, have the same basic sequence of bones: upper arm, radius and ulna, wrist bones and in some cases metacarpals and finger bones.

According to supporters of the theory of evolution, such similarities in structure and form bear witness to a common ancestor from which the various species that have homologous organs today evolved. But such a position is entirely arbitrary, because it rests not on proof but simply on an opinion. So the view that homologous organs are signs not of evolution but of creation must be considered equally valid. For it is entirely reasonable that one creative Mind used the same basic design to build similar structures in organisms. After all, the fact that a car and a lorry have “homologous organs” (wheels, steering wheel, engine and so on) is due to the same basic design being used for all vehicles, and not to some kind of evolution that happened to the car – entirely by chance at that.

So the view that homologous organs support evolution harks back to times of scientific darkness, when a simple observation was hastily declared an undeniable scientific conclusion without further investigation (for example, rotting meat gives birth to flies – see “Abiogenesis”). On the other hand, modern findings in biology show the view of Creation to be the better explanation for the existence of homologous organs:

  • There are organs and structures that are called “homologous” but which form during pregnancy from different groups of embryonic cells in the various kinds of organisms. For example, the forelimbs of the lizard and of man form from different points of the embryo's trunk.
  • Organs called “homologous” do not always come from similar sequences of genetic code, and conversely, similar sequences of genetic code do not produce homologous organs.
  • It is reasonable and to be expected that for organs that have to meet similar functional demands, designs with a common basis are preferred, since any alternative design would not be suitable or as efficient. For example, wheels of all kinds are round because of the common need to roll, and for this purpose the most suitable design is the circle.

Biochemical similarity
Besides the similarities in form of homologous organs, there are also similarities in the biochemical functions of cells: all living organisms use 20 particular amino acids, have DNA as the carrier of genetic information, have similar metabolic machinery and so on. But these similarities can be taken as evidence of a Creator just as much as they can be taken as evidence of common evolutionary descent , who used the same basic design to build homologous biochemical processes in living organisms.

One wonders why supporters of the theory of evolution would expect to see the various kinds of organisms built with completely different biochemistry before they would consider the possibility of a Creator. If that were so, it would make no sense, because:

  • The Creator would have made organisms that could not feed on one another, since they would have no biochemical similarity. The only way to survive would be cannibalism, because of the lack of biochemical compatibility.
  • The conclusion would follow that there is not one Creator but many creators, each of whom followed a different design.
So the view that there is a Creator is the better explanation for biochemical similarity too , since if evolution had happened, then over the billions of years in which such great differences in form arose between species, should there not also have been correspondingly great differences in the biochemistry of life? Yet no such thing is observed anywhere. On the contrary, we see that there are organisms, such as yeast and man, between which there is a huge “evolutionary gap”, and yet both use almost identical families of biochemical molecules.
Genetic similarity
Finally, one more argument for the theory of evolution is based on genetic similarity, the most popular example being the fact that human DNA is 96% to 98% or more similar to the DNA of various kinds of ape. First of all, the percentage of genetic similarity found varies from study to study , because it depends on which regions of DNA are compared, how much DNA is used and which computational techniques are applied. So, as regards the similarity between ape and man, different studies of different parts and amounts of DNA give figures for similarity that range between 87% and 96%.

One example of how subjective these percentages are is the fact that chimpanzee DNA is 12% larger than human DNA, so even if all human DNA were identical to the chimpanzee's, the maximum similarity would be 88%! (since the remaining 12% does not exist in man).

Besides, genetic similarity does not seem to lead to clear evolutionary conclusions. For example, the chimpanzee is thought to have the greatest genetic similarity to man (estimates go as high as 99%). Yet the gibbon, with less genetic similarity, is more similar to man in form when it comes to walking on two legs. And the gorilla, also with less genetic similarity to man, has certain genes that are more like the human ones than the chimpanzee's are.

Moreover, it was recently discovered that the horse is genetically more similar to the bat than the horse is to the cow. On this basis it follows that the horse and the bat have a closer common evolutionary ancestor than the horse and the cow! Yet on the basis of similarity of form, the opposite conclusion follows! So which of the two criteria should we rely on as regards the theory of evolution: similarity of form or genetic similarity? And if one species really evolved from another and there are common ancestors, how can there be cases where these two criteria contradict each other?

There are also animals which, without having as great a genetic similarity to man as an ape, nevertheless in some cases show abilities and intelligence closer to human ones (for more details see the article “Decoding the dogma of DNA similarity” in the right-hand column).

Indeed, since human DNA consists of about 3 billion “chemical letters”, even if the similarity with an ape's DNA reached 99%, the difference of 1% would amount to about 30 million “chemical letters”. But it is impossible for such an extensive change in the genetic material to have happened through evolution by means of mutations (see the related article “Neo-Darwinism: are mutations a source of new species?”).

Finally, similarity in the code of DNA does not necessarily mean similarity in the information it carries. For example, the sentences:

“There are many scientists today who question the evolutionary model and its atheistic philosophical consequences”

“There are not many scientists today who question the evolutionary model and its atheistic philosophical consequences”

are almost 97% the same, yet have nearly opposite meanings!

So from the above it is clear that the much-advertised genetic similarity between ape and man is aimed solely at creating a false impression.

So the newer scientific findings dismantle the theory of evolution and lead naturally to the conclusion that God the Creator exists.
