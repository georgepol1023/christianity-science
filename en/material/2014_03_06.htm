When does a human being begin to exist?

Many views have been put forward on the question of when a human being begins to exist as an entity whose life has value.

Looking back through history from antiquity to today, we meet various views:

  • The Stoic philosophers believed that the soul enters the body (so that the person is a complete entity) when the newborn takes its first breath. This means that the unborn embryo is not a complete human being but merely a biological organism that grows for nine months and, as soon as it is born and takes its first breath, suddenly receives a soul and becomes a complete human being.
  • Aristotle developed a theory of progressive ensoulment. He believed that at first a plant soul develops, which then becomes an animal soul and finally a human one, adding that abortion was permissible early in pregnancy, before a certain stage at which the embryo was thought to have developed a “human” soul.
  • Hippocrates and the Pythagorean philosophers declared that fertilisation is what marks the beginning of human life, and that the human soul is created at the moment of fertilisation.
  • According to Hinduism, the soul enters the body at conception. This view, of course, rests on the belief that a soul that already exists and has lived before, in an earlier age, enters the body at conception (reincarnation).
  • Tertullian believed that the soul comes from the parents and is formed alongside the making of the physical body, a view that was condemned as heresy (it recalls Aristotle's view of progressive ensoulment).
  • Saint Thomas Aquinas and Saint Augustine of Hippo put forward the view that embryos were “quickened” (using Aristotle's term for ensoulment) around the 40th day after conception. Yet both judged abortion to be a serious wrong at any stage of pregnancy.
  • The Eastern Orthodox Church and the Catholic Church declare that human life must be respected and fully protected from the moment of conception.

With the modern knowledge provided by the sciences of medicine and biology about the stages of the embryo's formation during pregnancy, various points that could mark the beginning of a human being's existence have been proposed:

  • The moment of fertilisation of the egg by a sperm to form the zygote, the first cell of the new human organism.
  • The moment of implantation of the blastocyst (the mass of cells into which the zygote has divided) in the womb. This happens about a week after fertilisation.
  • When segmentation can no longer happen, that is, roughly after the 3rd week, when the embryo loses the possibility of a division that would result in a second or even third embryo forming in the same pregnancy (identical twins).
  • When the first heartbeats appear.
  • “Brain birth” (the opposite of brain death): either when the first brain waves appear in the lower brain, that is, the brain stem (6th – 8th week of pregnancy), or when the first brain waves appear in higher centres of the brain, that is, the cortex (22nd – 24th week of pregnancy).
  • When the embryo begins to move.
  • When the embryo is able to feel pain.
  • If the embryo is finally judged viable, in the sense that it will be able to survive outside its mother's body and does not put the pregnant woman in danger.
  • When it is born.
  • At various stages after birth, depending on whether the baby can be regarded as a person, by the criterion of the ability for some basic thought (newborn awareness) and so on.

As for the views put forward in past centuries – that a human being is a complete entity when they take their first breath, or when their soul is progressively formed into a human one, or about the soul coming from the parents or from reincarnation – we do not think they need further comment, since they lack any scientific basis.

So we will examine the possible points proposed on the basis of modern scientific knowledge, working backwards:
  • If after birth the baby meets various conditions (for example awareness): to begin with, this view rules out the possibility of finding treatments for such cases. So if treatments are found, will the individual once deserving of death suddenly acquire the human status they did not have until then?! Also, by this logic, those who lose the ability of awareness wholly or in part (for example through an accident) at any moment of their life also lose their human status to a greater or lesser degree, and so the right to exist and live, and should be put to death.
  • At the moment of birth: this rests on the assumption that until the moment of birth the embryo is part of the pregnant woman's body. But in reality it is a completely separate organism, which is simply housed and fed by the pregnant woman's body until birth. So there is no reason why this organism should be considered less “human” before its birth.
  • If the embryo is judged viable: this refers mainly to cases of genetic abnormality or premature birth, where the baby is not viable unless placed in an incubator. By this logic, being called “human” depends on the quality of one's biological make-up (thoroughly refuted by the many great artists, writers and scientists over the centuries who were born with some genetic abnormality) or on the invention and perfecting of the incubator.
  • When the embryo is able to move or feel pain: as above, there is no reason why the fact that the abilities to move or feel pain have not yet developed should make the embryo a non-human organism. Likewise, do those who through some accident lose these abilities at any age stop being human?
  • “Brain birth”: the idea that there is a neurological end-point to human life has led to attempts to define a corresponding neurological starting point. This effort has led to the idea of the “birth” of the brain, the reverse of brain death. But in earlier articles we have shown in detail that the human mind (the “I”) is something distinct from the brain as an organ. The “I” continues to exist after brain death, so we cannot equate the beginning of the “I” with the brain beginning to work. Simply, when so-called “brain birth” takes place, the human mind (the “I”) is now able to express itself through bodily movements.
  • The loss of the possibility of segmentation: supporters of this view hold that an embryo cannot be an individual, because if every human being is an individual, one cannot be divided from oneself. But in reality this kind of reasoning works as a refutation of this view, because by this logic not only have we no right to say that there is no individual at conception, but there is at least one and possibly more. Through the process of segmentation individuals are reproduced without the individuality of any person being destroyed.
  • The moment of implantation: it is a fact that at the moment of implantation the early embryo (the blastocyst) makes its presence known biochemically and pregnancy begins. But even before this moment it was already a living (undeveloped) human organism, separate from the mother.
  • The moment of conception (fertilisation): this is the moment when a sperm joins an egg (each containing half the DNA, from the father and from the mother respectively) and the zygote is formed, which is a cell with a complete genetic code, coming from its parents but different from both the father's and the mother's. So the zygote is the first cell of a new human organism. Indeed, at the moment of fertilisation all the genetic characteristics are formed once and for all (for example sex, skin colour, eye colour and so on).

So we can conclude with certainty that the moment of conception marks the beginning of a human being's existence, since the fertilisation of the egg by a sperm is the decisive point after which there is immediately something completely different from what existed just before (that is, a new human organism instead of two reproductive cells). And by general agreement, many members of the worldwide medical community accept fertilisation as the point at which life begins.

Indeed, to the argument that the embryo can be compared to a house not yet built, that is, that it is merely “materials” (for example, if a builders' yard catches fire, we do not say that 30 houses burned down, but that the materials from which 30 houses would have been built burned), the answer was given that the right word for the formation of the embryo is not “construction”, as for a house or a car, but “development” (progressive shaping and completion), as with the developing of a photograph or the growth of a sapling into a tree. Human beings develop; the view that they are “constructed” is flatly wrong, because the child develops from the beginning; it is not built by adding parts through outside intervention, since its specified form is already within it (in its DNA) and gradually takes shape and appears.

With the naturally reached view that a human being's existence begins from the moment of conception, and so that the unborn embryo counts as a human being, Holy Scripture also agrees; here are some examples from it:

  • God says that He chose the prophet Jeremiah as a person before he was fully formed in his mother's womb:

    “Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations”     (Jeremiah 1:5)
  • The holy writers call unborn babies “babes” and “children” – names that should be used only after birth, if before it they were not real human beings:

    “And it came to pass, that, when Elisabeth heard the salutation of Mary, the babe leaped in her womb; and Elisabeth was filled with the Holy Ghost”     (Luke 1:41)

    “And Isaac intreated the LORD for his wife, because she was barren: and the LORD was intreated of him, and Rebekah his wife conceived. And the children struggled together within her” (Genesis 25:21–22)
  • The law of Moses regarded harm to the life of an unborn child as a crime deserving punishment by taking the life of an adult:

    “If men strive, and hurt a woman with child, so that her fruit depart from her, and yet no mischief follow: he shall be surely punished, according as the woman's husband will lay upon him; and he shall pay as the judges determine. And if any mischief follow, then thou shalt give life for life” (Exodus 21:22–23)
  • David speaks of the days before his birth in the first person singular, so regarding himself as a “human being” from that time:

    “For thou hast possessed my reins: thou hast covered me in my mother's womb. I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well. My substance was not hid from thee, when I was made in secret, and curiously wrought in the lowest parts of the earth. Thine eyes did see my substance, yet being unperfect; and in thy book all my members were written, which in continuance were fashioned, when as yet there was none of them” (Psalm 139:13–16)
  • Likewise, Job speaks in the first person singular from the very moment of his conception, a process he compares to milk curdling into cheese:

    “Hast thou not poured me out as milk, and curdled me like cheese? Thou hast clothed me with skin and flesh, and hast fenced me with bones and sinews” (Job 10:10–11)
  • The prophet Hosea, warning of the childlessness (the loss of glory) of Israel (“Ephraim”), sets a child's conception as the starting moment of its existence:

    “As for Ephraim, their glory shall fly away like a bird, from the birth, and from the womb, and from the conception” (Hosea 9:11)

The Word of God also tells us that man, though he has a beginning, has no end, because his soul continues to exist after the death of the body, either in the presence of God (eternal life) or far from it (eternal punishment). So God, in order to secure eternal life for every person, gave His Son as a sacrifice on the Cross for the forgiveness of sins, and raised Him from the dead on the third day, so defeating death on behalf of all people.

So each of us is called to accept God's gift, believing in Jesus Christ and repenting of our sins. For God is ready to forgive and blot out every sin of the past, giving a person a new beginning in Christ Jesus:

“And the times of this ignorance God winked at; but now commandeth all men every where to repent: because he hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness” (Acts 17:30–31) “Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new” (2 Corinthians 5:17)
