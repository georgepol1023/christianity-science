In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth

The scientific discoveries of the 20th and 21st centuries in astronomy and cosmology support this so brief yet so true statement in the 1st verse of Holy Scripture: “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth” – Genesis 1:1

The initial conditions of the creation of the universe have not yet been fully worked out and are now studied with modern nuclear physics experiments. But it is established that the universe is expanding.

In 1929 the famous astronomer Edwin Hubble, observing with a telescope, found that the space between the galaxies is growing; that is, the universe is expanding – something that Albert Einstein had predicted a few years earlier (in 1915–1917) from the equations of the general theory of relativity, though he believed he had made a mistake.

So, starting with the Russian physicist George Gamow (1948), models were developed in which the universe came from an initial point where all energy and matter were concentrated, and which began to expand. This event is known as the “Big Bang”. But let us be clear that the term “Big Bang”, in its strictly scientific sense, means a very fast and sudden, but very carefully designed and controlled, release of matter and energy, and not an explosion like a bomb. Indeed, the maximum disorder brought about by a bomb exploding cannot result in the order and harmonious working that exist in the universe today. So it is not literally an explosion, but a very fast expansion of space and time, which was mockingly named the “Big Bang” (Hoyle – 1950). Before the Big Bang there was neither space nor time; that moment marks the beginning of the existence of space and time.

The trace left by the Big Bang is the cosmic microwave background radiation, which was predicted in theory, and whose existence was confirmed experimentally by Penzias and Wilson in the 1960s. Two modern satellites, COBE (1989–1992) and WMAP (2003), studied the cosmic microwave background radiation and its variations (ripples), from which the stars and galaxies formed, more precisely. In this way the age of the universe was estimated (about 13.7 billion years) and its components identified: 4% ordinary matter, 23% invisible dark matter and 73% dark energy (the last two components are so named because scientists do not yet know their nature).

A direct consequence of the expansion of the universe is the finding that the universe is not infinite and eternal, but has a beginning and a cause of its existence; that is, the 1st verse of Holy Scripture is confirmed: “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth” – Genesis 1:1. The Word of God also speaks of the expansion of the universe in at least 10 different passages of Holy Scripture: in Job, the Psalms, Isaiah, Jeremiah and elsewhere (see the related material in the right-hand column).

Since the universe has a beginning, it cannot be either infinite or eternal, as many ancient peoples believed (Assyrians, Babylonians, Egyptians), as well as ancient Greek philosophers – a view that lasted until the early 20th century. This finding matters because theories of cosmology and of the origin of life that rest on chance collapse. This is because the extremely small probabilities that the harmony of the universe and life on earth came about by purely random processes might perhaps be realised at some point in a universe that had existed for an infinite time. But the discovery that the universe and the time it has existed are finite leaves no time for the tiny probabilities required by theories that rest on the world arising by chance to come true.

But the ideas of an infinite, eternal and static universe are refuted not only by astronomical observation but also by the 2nd law of thermodynamics. This is a physical law that rules the universe, under which everything moves from an initial order towards disorder, until all the energy in the universe has degraded into heat, so that no more useful work can be produced. Since in the universe there is a constant movement from an initial order towards disorder (for example, the burning of the stars' fuel), from being “wound up” to “winding down”, the universe really is finite and not infinite or eternal – just like a clock: when we watch it winding down, we know it cannot have been winding down for ever, but must have been wound up not long ago; that is, a starting event must have taken place a finite time ago.

The force of the 2nd law of thermodynamics also overturns the idea that the universe is self-existent, that is, that it is the cause of its own creation. According to the 2nd law of thermodynamics, heat flows in one direction only (from the hotter to the colder body); if this flow is drawn as an arrow, the arrow will always point from the past to the future and never the other way round, so the whole universe is set within a framework of time that flows one way. Here the principle of causality (a fundamental principle of modern physics) automatically comes into force, which states that “every phenomenon and every event in the realm of time has its cause, which is independent of it and greater than it”. So the universe requires a cause of its existence greater than itself, outside and beyond it.

Based on the characteristics of the universe, the cause of its existence, its Creator, must have the following characteristics:

– Since He created space, He must be outside it!
– Since He created time, He must be Eternal!
– Since He rules over matter and energy, He must be Almighty!
– Since He brought free persons into existence, how much more is He Himself a person!
– He from whom the world of mathematical ideas and the workings
of thought and reason come must be All-wise!
– He who created and sustains life must be the eternally Living One!

Holy Scripture, showing astonishing consistency with the principle of causality, attributes exactly these properties to the person of God (see the related material in the right-hand column). No other book of any religion matches these characteristics. All the ancient creation stories presuppose the existence of the universe and of the matter from which the gods themselves are made (theogony). Only Holy Scripture speaks of creation out of nothing and of a God outside creation. Unlike “in the beginning was chaos”, that is, something eternal, infinite and static, the Word of God declares: “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth” and is fully vindicated by modern scientific findings.
