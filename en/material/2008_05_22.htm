The length of the 6 days of Creation

As “Christianity and Science”, we felt it necessary to touch on the length of the 6 days of Creation, since from time to time it has been the subject of intense attention, concern and disagreement among various Christian communities, and between Christians and the scientific community.

Many Christians hold that the world was created in 6 consecutive 24-hour days, 6,000 to 10,000 years ago (young-earth creationists). Another group of Christians believe that the 6 days mentioned in Genesis correspond to a thousand years each (after 2 Peter 3:8); another group, that they are long periods of time in general, in step with the estimates of science (old-earth creationists); while others believe that God created everything in a single moment.

It should be noted that the first, apostolic Church did not think it important to deal with this question, but in the first centuries AD the Fathers began to express various views, which never became a cause of conflict or a subject of theological dispute.

In the 17th century, after careful study of the historical books of the Old Testament, Ussher and Lightfoot calculated, down to the hour, that the Creation dates to the year 4004 BC – an entirely inaccurate assumption that nevertheless spread so widely that it prevailed and found its way as a footnote into translations of Holy Scripture, becoming a stumbling block for people, mainly of Asian nations, in accepting the truth of the Word of God, since they saw elements of their own culture dating from before the supposed year of the world's creation. This carelessness on the one hand opened the way to questioning Holy Scripture and to an easier acceptance of the coming theory of evolution (19th century), and on the other caused a split in the unity of Christians and disputes that last to our own day.

From the 1960s and 70s, treatises and publications began to circulate in the United States trying to prove scientifically that the 6 days of Creation correspond to a few thousand years. In reply, scientific articles began to be published showing the errors and dangers hidden behind scientific creationism.

This created a huge polarisation between the scientific and the Christian world, which brought nothing but discord, strong reactions and in the end drove people away from the churches and from Christ Himself. The Christian community itself was also divided, to the point that churches would not accept as members believers (even of the same denomination) who held a different view of the length of the 6 days of Creation. So the interpretation of the first chapter of Genesis came to be regarded as one of the basic doctrines of the Christian faith, alongside the Deity and the Resurrection of Jesus Christ.

To understand what the truth is about the length of the 6 days mentioned in the 1st chapter of Genesis, we must bear in mind that time is a relative physical quantity. In 1905 Einstein put forward the special theory of relativity, one consequence of which was the relativity of time. This means that time cannot be measured in an absolute way at different points in the universe, but depends on the frame of reference we are in.

If we take two people, one moving and the other at rest, then for the one who is moving, time passes more slowly. How much more slowly? The greater his speed relative to the person at rest, the slower it passes. Indeed, for this effect to be large enough to be easily observed, the moving person's speed must be very high, close to the speed of light. But we should note here that these people do not notice the time lag, because when each looks at his own watch, time seems to flow normally. In reality, though, one second for the moving person corresponds to more than one second for the person at rest (for a better understanding of the effect, please see the related articles in the right-hand column). The rate at which time flows is also affected by the gravitational field we are in. The greater the mass of a body (and so the stronger its gravitational field), the greater the time lag. So on the surface of a planet with a much greater mass than the Earth's, time flows more slowly, and a 24-hour day on that planet may correspond to many Earth years. Indeed, right at the event horizon of a black hole, whose gravitational field is so strong that not even light can escape, time almost stops.

This property of time (and of space) to be distorted by gravitational attraction leads to the conclusion that there is no fixed time for every point in the universe. Millions of “cosmic clocks” are ticking, each at its own rate: the local time above every star, every galaxy, every black hole, every planet and every moon. Each clock shows the right time for its own frame of reference, but they cannot be synchronised with one another, since the rate at which they tick depends on the gravity that governs them.

We saw in an earlier article that science today, in agreement with the Word of God, says that the universe had a beginning; that is, it started from a point where all matter and energy were concentrated. This beginning is known as the “Big Bang”, a term that, as we said, is not scientifically accurate but has become established in everyday speech. We also know today that the universe has been expanding since the moment of the Big Bang. So, in line with what we said about the relativity of time, because the curvature of space and the density of matter change as the universe expands, the result is that the quantity we call “a second of time” is also constantly changing. That is, the length of a second, during the different phases of the life of the universe, was sometimes longer and sometimes shorter, depending on the density and curvature of the universe. So in the first moments of the life of the universe, when its density and curvature were infinite, the length of a second was practically infinite. On this basis, even the figures and estimates given for the age of the universe (13.6 billion years) are more a convention that satisfies the human scientific outlook than a reality, since that age is the sum of units of time (let us say seconds) of unequal length.

After all we have said, we hope that the phenomenon of the relativity of time has become understandable to some degree. So now, reading once more the account of Creation in the 1st chapter of Genesis, we can understand that, compared with time as we perceive it flowing on the surface of the Earth, God's clock during Creation was probably ticking slowly, perhaps very slowly, given that God can treat the whole universe as a single unit. In this way, six days (even 24-hour days) may correspond to thousands, millions or even billions of Earth years. Indeed, in many cases in Holy Scripture (see the related article in the right-hand column) the word “day” is used for many human years.

So we cannot define the length of the 6 days of Creation in terms of time as we measure it, nor claim that they correspond to 6 twenty-four-hour days as we measure them. The length of Creation, then, really is 6 days, but not according to our own rate of measuring time, and so there is no reason to think that the accuracy of Holy Scripture is called into question.

So the length of Creation should not become a cause of disagreement and dispute, because it is not the question “when was all this created” but the question “who created it” that will lead a person into a relationship with the Creator. Besides, from the creation of Adam onwards, as Holy Scripture shows, God synchronised His clock with the clock of human history and turned all His attention to it, and when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth His Son to be sacrificed on the Cross, “that we might receive the adoption of sons” (Galatians 4:4–5).
