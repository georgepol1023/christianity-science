Why the world does not end on 21 December 2012

In recent years the rumour that the end of the world is coming on 21 December 2012 has spread worldwide. This date was chosen because, according to the calculations of various researchers, that day the calendar of the Maya ends – one of the greatest ancient civilisations, which lived in the region of Central America. According to the predictions circulating on the internet, on 21 December 2012 events will happen that will cause widespread destruction on Earth.

In this article we will examine whether any of the doomsday scenarios can come true, and why the eschatology of Holy Scripture stands out as the only reliable one compared with every other end-of-the-world rumour that comes to the fore from time to time.

First of all, the civilisation of the Maya was one of the most advanced of antiquity, with impressive works of art and monuments, a complex writing system, extensive knowledge of astronomy and various systems for measuring time.

One of their calendars, used to measure long periods of time, was the Long Count calendar, according to which:

20 days (20 “kin”) = 1 month (1 “winal”)
18 months (18 “winal”) = 1 year (1 “tun”)
20 “tun” = 1 “katun”
20 “katun” = 1 “baktun” (a little more than 394 years of our calendar)
13 “baktun” = 1 complete calendar cycle (about 5,125 years of our calendar)

So every 13 “baktun” (that is, every 5,125 years or so) a complete cycle was finished and the calendar started again from the beginning.

It is calculated that the last Long Count calendar of the Maya began on 11 August 3114 BC, and so, making the right calculations, it should end on 21 December AD 2012. Scholars argue that this start date of their calendar (11/08/3114 BC) was very probably chosen as the beginning of a new period of time after some major climate changes or destruction that had gone before. Given that Noah's Flood is estimated to have taken place around 3,000 BC, it would not be far-fetched to suppose that this is probably the event that marked the beginning of the last Long Count calendar of the Maya.

The fact that the Maya Long Count calendar ends on 21 December 2012 does not mean that the world ends on that day too. Simply, the calendar will start again from the beginning, just as happens with our own calendar every 31 December. It would be foolish to argue that because our calendar has no more days after 31 December, the world ends there. That is not so; the counting of time simply starts again from the beginning, moving on to 1 January of the following year.

Besides, in the finds of the Maya civilisation, the end of the Long Count calendar was not linked with the end of the world but with holding a great festival. Of course, other sources appeal to certain mythological accounts according to which at the end of each calendar the gods destroyed the world with a flood and created a new and better one: in the 1st calendar the gods created the Earth, in the 2nd people made of mud, in the 3rd people made of wood, and the 4th world is us, with flesh made of maize flour, as they believed. So if someone accepts that the Maya calendar has the prophetic power to predict the end of the world, they must also accept as true the mythological accounts that are inseparably bound up with it.

Moreover, an older Maya calendar was recently discovered, which is calculated to reach as far as 17 “baktun” or even later, that is, well beyond 21 December 2012.

Even so, in an attempt to keep the “prophetic” power of the Maya calendar, but stripped of the related mythological accounts, various pseudo-scientific scenarios have been invented in recent years that try to argue for the destruction of the world on 21 December 2012. Sadly, though, the lives of many people who are persuaded by such pseudo-scientific rumours are deeply affected, leading them to panic or even to desperate acts.

Some of the best-known doomsday scenarios for 21 December 2012 are the following:

  • An alignment of the planets:
    According to this scenario, on 21 December 2012 the planets of our solar system will form, together with the Sun, an imaginary straight line, and because of the strong gravitational effects great disasters will happen on Earth.

    Rebuttal:
    First of all, on 21 December 2012 the planets of the solar system will not be in alignment. On the contrary, at the end of 2012 the planets will be in positions that make a scattered picture in the sky. But even if an alignment did happen, the gravitational effect of the other planets on the Earth would be negligible. The only heavenly bodies whose gravity has an effect on the Earth are the Sun and the Moon. Besides, such alignments have already happened in past decades (1962, 1982, 2000) without any disaster.
  • An alignment of the Earth and the Sun with the black hole at the centre of the Galaxy:
    In this scenario too, an extremely strong gravitational pull (because of the black hole) is supposed to cause disasters on Earth.

    Rebuttal:
    This cannot be so, on the one hand because the centre of the Galaxy is at the unimaginable distance of 30,000 light years from the Earth, and on the other because such an alignment happens every December, without any disaster ever having happened.
  • Planet Nibiru, or Planet X, or Planet Eris:
    This is a planet supposedly discovered by the ancient Sumerians, which orbits the Sun with a period of 3,600 years and on 21 December 2012 will come dangerously close to the Earth or even collide with it. All this rests on the claims of certain writers (Zecharia Sitchin and Nancy Lieder), who also speak of visits by “ancient astronauts” from this planet and of communication with extraterrestrials who are warning the Earth of the coming disaster. At first this was expected in May 2003, but in the end it was moved to 21 December 2012 so as to match the end of the Maya calendar too.

    Rebuttal:
    First of all, “Nibiru” is another name for the ancient Eastern god Marduk. Specialists in Sumerian civilisation say that this name does not refer to a new planet discovered by the Sumerians (who in fact did not know of the existence of other – real – planets such as Uranus, Neptune and Pluto), but is another name for the planet Jupiter.
    An attempt to support this scenario was made when in 1983 a NASA survey found sources of infrared radiation in the outer part of our solar system, which could point to the existence of a new planet. But studies published in the following years proved that the sources of infrared radiation were some distant galaxies and a nebula, and that there is in fact no new planet.
    Finally, if a planet really were coming so close to the Earth that there was a risk of collision, this would have been noticed years ago, and as 21 December 2012 approached it would be visible even to the naked eye as the brightest object in the sky. Here, of course, the supporters of this theory claim that for some reason the planet Nibiru is invisible. But again this does not stand, because even if it were invisible, as it approached it would noticeably affect the orbits of the other planets through gravitational interactions, which would also be felt on Earth.
    A new claim by the champions of this theory replies that NASA has observed this planet but is keeping it secret so as not to cause panic. But this claim does not stand either, since about 10,000 professional and 100,000 amateur astronomers around the world observe the night sky every day. Moreover, the largest telescopes in the world are not controlled by NASA, but are run by universities and academic institutions that simply supply NASA with the data from their observations. So it would be impossible for such an imminent worldwide danger to have been kept a closely guarded secret.
  • A surge in solar activity:
    According to this scenario, on 21 December 2012 the Sun's activity will be so intense (solar storms) that it will have the power to cause widespread destruction and wipe out life on Earth.

    Rebuttal:
    It is a fact that solar activity follows a cycle of about 11 years of minima and maxima. The last maximum took place in 2001, and the next maximum is expected not on 21 December 2012 but in May 2013. During the maxima of solar activity there are more sunspots, flares and coronal mass ejections. Because of these phenomena huge amounts of energy are released, and particles are thrown out that often reach the Earth. But our planet, thanks to its magnetic field (the magnetosphere), is protected to a great extent. So however strong these phenomena are, they are no threat to life and certainly cannot cause natural disasters. The only danger concerns electronic systems, mainly those of satellites, or sometimes telecommunications, which may be damaged. In rarer cases power grids may also be affected, resulting in temporary power cuts.
  • A reversal of the Earth's geographical poles:
    For some reason the Earth's poles will be reversed (equivalently: the direction of the Earth's rotation will change), resulting in great disasters all over the world.

    Rebuttal:
    This is impossible. It is known that the Earth's axis is not perpendicular to the plane of its orbit around the Sun but is tilted by about 23.5 degrees. This is due to the gravitational interaction with the Moon, which ensures the stabilisation of the Earth's axis of rotation in this tilted position, unlike other planets of our solar system, whose axes of rotation are not stable because they do not have moons to achieve this stabilisation.
  • A reversal of the Earth's magnetic poles:
    Like the previous scenario, only here the reversal concerns the magnetic and not the geographical poles.

    Rebuttal:
    First of all, we must distinguish the geographical from the magnetic poles. The geographical poles are particular areas at the northernmost and southernmost points of the Earth. The magnetic poles are what compass needles point towards. Because the Earth's magnetic field is created by processes that take place inside it, the magnetic poles do not correspond to fixed areas on the Earth's crust but move over time. For thousands of years the magnetic north pole, although it does not coincide with the geographical north pole, has been close enough to it that when a compass needle points we can safely assume that its direction also shows geographical north.
    The magnetic poles move at such a speed that their positions are reversed about every 400,000 years. So we can see that this is not an event that can happen within a few hours or days, so that we could speak of a sudden reversal of the magnetic poles on a particular date. And a reversal of the magnetic poles has no reason to cause any disaster on Earth, except perhaps the need to readjust compasses.

After rebutting such scenarios, someone might observe that Holy Scripture too contains information about the end of the world, and indeed the book of Revelation describes phenomena that may resemble the groundless rumours we saw above (for example, Revelation 6:13–14 & 8:10–11 speak of heavenly bodies falling and causing widespread destruction on Earth, while Revelation 16:18–20 speaks of a very great worldwide earthquake).

So the question arises: why should anyone accept the eschatology of Holy Scripture as reliable, especially since all the dates and predictions for the end of the world made from time to time have been proved false? What is it that makes the eschatology of Holy Scripture more reliable than all the other rumours about the end of the world that circulate from time to time?

  1. When Jesus Christ was asked by the disciples (Matthew 24:3, Mark 13:4, Luke 21:7 and Acts 1:6–7), instead of a date He gave certain signs, which are easy for anyone to observe and which we can indeed see beginning to be fulfilled in our days. The various tellers of falsehoods cannot do this, and they try to convince people with signs that supposedly exist but cannot be observed, are secret and so on (for example, they claim the planet Nibiru exists but is invisible).
  2. The various false prophets of the end of the world, precisely because they have no real signs to show, do not hesitate to set specific dates (implying that they have made scientific calculations) so as to appear reliable. On the contrary, Jesus Christ, although He could give a date, does not do so, because He has no need to base the reliability of His words on such information. Besides, knowing the date would either cause panic or put humanity in the dangerous temptation of “scheduled” repentance shortly before the end.
  3. The various opportunists who advertise the end of the world try to frighten people and often to exploit them for their own benefit. On the contrary, all the eschatological passages of Holy Scripture give a message of comfort and peace to the human heart. The apostles, speaking of “the Day of the Lord”, do not aim to create a climate of panic in order to manipulate the mass of believers, but urge them to carry on a normal life in holiness and purity, watching and waiting for the coming of the Lord (see 1 Thessalonians 4 & 2 Thessalonians 2).
  4. All the prophecies of the Old Testament about the person of Christ and about other events are historically shown to have been fulfilled. Also a great many prophecies of the New Testament about the time of the last days have been fulfilled and continue to be fulfilled. So the validity of the prophetic and eschatological word of Holy Scripture is established.

So as faithful Christians we know that the world really will end at some point (see 2 Peter 3:10–13). But what we are waiting for is not the coming disasters but the rapture of the Church (see 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18 and 1 Corinthians 15:50–54), which will come before all these calamities:

“Watch ye therefore, and pray always, that ye may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of man” (Luke 21:36)
