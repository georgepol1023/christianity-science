The reliability of Holy Scripture – biblical archaeology

Man's highest means of communication, unlike the animals', is speech. In the same way Holy Scripture, which is the Word of God, speaks directly into the heart, the spirit, the mind and the conscience of the sincere person.

Holy Scripture was written over a period of about 15 centuries by about 40 different writers. It is made up of the Old Testament and the New Testament, and it makes a very serious claim: “It is the Word of God”.

This claim proves itself when a sincere person takes Holy Scripture into their hands. Even so, we want to look at the reliability of Holy Scripture not only as the result of its effect on a person's life, which is the primary evidence of its reliability (1 Thess. 2:13), but also through archaeology and the study of ancient texts.

The writers of Holy Scripture are about 40 different people who lived in different eras and different social and political settings. Yet the result is a unified book: reading Holy Scripture, you do not feel that you are moving from one book to another, but that it is the same book throughout.

This refutes the claim that the texts of Holy Scripture were an attempt by priesthoods and various centres of power to steer and control ordinary people, especially since, from time to time, it was the priesthoods of the day that persecuted those who believed and followed these texts.

In our coming topics we will look in more detail at the finds and conclusions of the famous scholar and archaeologist Sir W. Ramsay , who took up archaeology with the sole aim of proving that the account in the book of the Acts of the Apostles was wrong; yet after 25 years of research he proved the opposite and turned from atheism to Christianity.

A basic question is: how do we know that the text we read in the Old Testament today is the same as the original Hebrew text? First of all, as far as the text is concerned, there is the Septuagint translation into Greek, made in the 3rd century BC, and the Masoretic text, whose most recent manuscripts date from the 9th century AD, which is why their reliability was questioned. But in 1947 the Qumran manuscripts (the Dead Sea Scrolls) came to light; they date from the 2nd and 1st centuries BC, and they confirm the reliability of the Masoretic text.

An important factor that ensures that the Old Testament texts we hold today are faithful to the originals is that the copying of the manuscripts was entrusted to specialist copyists called Talmudists, who kept truly strict rules when copying the sacred texts, so as to avoid any error.

The differences between Holy Scripture, and especially the Old Testament, and the mythological accounts of ancient peoples are also obvious. The texts of Holy Scripture give a clear placing of events in space and time, complete consistency with historical and scientific facts, and impartiality in describing people: the people of Holy Scripture are not idealised; often their weaknesses are shown too. The clear purpose of all the texts is to exalt one person alone, Jesus Christ.

To confirm the above, we can, for example, compare the texts of Genesis about the Creation with the corresponding creation accounts of other ancient peoples, and above all with the Babylonian epic Enuma Elish. The superiority and distinctiveness of Genesis lie in the simplicity and consistency of its description, and in the uniqueness of its idea of creation out of nothing by a God who is outside space and time, and who is exactly one God, not many. This overturns the claim that the biblical account came from other ancient accounts, since the observed development of religion in human civilisation runs from monotheism to polytheism and never the other way round, and this is where we see the biblical account stand apart.
