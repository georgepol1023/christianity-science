A review of the scientific achievements of 2012

A few days before the end of 2012, the editors of the journal “Science” chose the 10 most important scientific achievements of the year. This article briefly lists and comments on some of them, while more details on each are given in the “Related material” in the right-hand column.


The Higgs boson:
This was chosen as the most important scientific achievement of 2012. It is an elementary particle whose existence was predicted in theory in the 1960s by the English physicist Peter Higgs, and on the basis of which it can be explained how matter acquires mass.

In July 2012 the scientists at CERN (the nuclear research centre on the French–Swiss border) announced that in the experiments they are carrying out they had detected a particle which, with a fairly high level of certainty (it has not yet been fully confirmed), they can say must be the Higgs boson.

Through such experiments studying elementary particles, scientists find that the behaviour of the building blocks of matter is governed by laws and equations, and by a wonderful harmony that they are trying to analyse. The mathematical equations that describe the principles of physics are not irregularly complicated but are marked by simplicity and symmetry. This is also why the universe we live in is not chaotic but obeys specific laws. And the conclusion that follows logically and naturally is that since there are laws, there must also be a Lawgiver, a Mind that created all these and set them working.

The genome of Denisovan man:
In 2012 German scientists published the genome (the complete genetic material) of an extinct hominid regarded as an evolutionary relative of modern man. It was named “Denisovan man” because its remains (a finger and 1–2 teeth) were found in a cave at Denisova in southern Siberia.

In earlier articles we have shown that the theory of evolution is overturned by modern scientific findings, and also that all the finds to date concerning some evolutionary ancestor or relative of man are unproven hypotheses or well-designed frauds.

As for Denisovan man, there are some similarities with the human genome, but this alone does not prove that it is an evolutionary relative of our species. It has in fact been found that 4%–6% of the DNA of Denisovan man exists in modern Melanesians (inhabitants of the islands north-east of Australia), and so some hastened to suppose that the Denisovans interbred with modern humans. But it could equally be supposed that the Denisovans were also modern humans who were geographically isolated, so that their genes are not found across much of today's human population. Moreover, for the whole Denisovan species the only finds are a finger and 1–2 teeth (which are even said to have belonged to different individuals!). So it is clear that such a small sample of finds can hardly represent the full range of genetic variety of the whole Denisovan species, and so no safe conclusions can be drawn.

The landing of the Curiosity rover on Mars:
The Curiosity rover is a robotic vehicle and a mobile laboratory whose mission is to explore the planet Mars, its main aims being to examine whether there is water and to discover any organic molecules that would be possible signs of living organisms.

This is NASA's most important mission to Mars, since the Curiosity rover alone cost about $2.5 billion, and it travelled for about 8 months, covering more than 560 million kilometres at a speed 17 times the speed of sound.

A particularly critical phase of the mission was the complex landing process, carried out with the innovative “sky crane” system, which was completed successfully on 6 August 2012.

At the end of November a rumour spread that the Curiosity rover had discovered some evidence pointing to the existence of life. But NASA denied the discovery. At this point we should recall that in an earlier article we discussed the anthropic principle, according to which the planet Earth is the only place in the universe that bears life, since it is mathematically impossible for the conditions needed for life all to come together, and to the right degree, anywhere else in the universe.

“Switching” genes on and off:
In 2012 an advanced protein “tool” called the “TAL effector” was found, which gave scientists the ability to intervene in particular genes, increasing their activity or “silencing” it. It is a revolutionary method of targeting genes, just as effective as existing methods but cheaper. Researchers now hope to look deeper into genes and their mutations, hoping to discover treatments better suited to the particular case of each person's DNA.

These are indeed praiseworthy efforts by the scientific community to “unlock” the secrets of the genetic material, but through them it becomes ever clearer that DNA is a remarkable molecule, whose complexity, and whose property of carrying information organised in an astonishing way, bear witness to the existence of a creative Mind that made it. We have said more on this subject in an earlier article of ours.

Withdrawing the term “junk DNA”:
Of all human DNA, only 2% (that is, about 20,000–23,000 genes) is used to produce proteins. The remaining 98% was until recently thought to be a useless “evolutionary leftover”, that is, genes that worked in the organisms of man's ancestral forms; but as man supposedly evolved, these genes gradually fell out of use, and because DNA is passed on to offspring by copying, all this useless part accumulated and still exists in human genetic material today. That is why it had been named “junk DNA”.

But in September 2012 the “ENCODE” research project presented an “updated edition” of the “encyclopedia of DNA”, which revealed the discovery that the part of DNA until then regarded as “junk” is a huge hidden “operating system” that controls the human genome. In all, about 80% of DNA has now been shown to carry out some kind of useful biochemical function. Even some “pseudogenes”, regarded as dead evolutionary leftovers, were found for some reason to be switched on again in some cells and in some people.

“The term ‘junk DNA’ should now itself be... thrown in the junk. It is clear from the new research that a much larger part of the genome is biologically active than we thought.” (Ewan Birney – European Bioinformatics Institute,
head of the international research team).

Through this scientific achievement the argument of “vestigial organs”, with which people tried in the past to support the theory of evolution, is overturned from one more angle. So it is shown even at the level of molecular biology that nothing is made by chance and without purpose, or as a leftover of evolution; rather, all the body's structures and the structures within the cell carry out some useful process, forming a harmoniously working whole, whose wise design bears witness to the existence of a Creator.

To conclude, we would like to say that all these scientific achievements, which truly inspire admiration, should not lead man to pride and boasting. For we must not forget that our knowledge is comparatively limited, if we consider that everything we know refers at most to 4% of the universe, while the remaining 96% is a mystery to science. Besides, alongside great scientific achievements, 21st-century humanity also has great wounds to show, such as unemployment, hunger, crime, wars and so on.

So the achievements above, and many others, are indeed successes for science, but it would be a greater success for man if he had the honesty to admit that through science he is led naturally to the conclusion that nothing happened by itself and by chance, but behind the wonderful workings of nature is a wise Creator whom he is invited to know.

“Because that which may be known of God is manifest in them; for God hath shewed it unto them. For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse” (Romans 1:19–20)

For although man is proud and boasts of his limited successes, God, on the contrary, though He is the Creator and Architect of the whole universe, humbled Himself and became like men in the person of Jesus Christ, to die on the cross for their sins, paying a debt for which He was not responsible, so as to restore the broken relationship with His creation and give it eternal life through His resurrection.

“...who [Jesus Christ] was delivered for our offences, and was raised again for our justification”. (Romans 4:25).
