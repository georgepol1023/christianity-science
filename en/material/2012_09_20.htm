Where is the soul? Evidence of man's spiritual nature

=============================================
Where is the soul?
 ◄ Evidence of man's spiritual nature ► =============================================

One of the greatest questions, still unanswered by science, is what human consciousness consists of. If man is simply a biological machine, then there should be corresponding centres in the brain that produce what we call consciousness, or self-awareness, or awareness of ourselves and our existence.

For the difference between a human being and a machine really is fundamental. A computer, however complex and however fast it can calculate, is not conscious of its own existence. There is no machine that itself perceives its own existence.

But the well-known scientist Ray Kurzweil has estimated that in the near future (2019) a computer costing just $1,000 will have the computing power of a human brain, and a few years later (around 2050) a $1,000 computer will have the computing power of all the human brains on Earth. This power and complexity, again according to Kurzweil's estimates, will make computers acquire self-awareness and feelings, just like people.

This view is a logical extension of the theory of evolution, according to which all kinds of animal and plant organisms came about by chance through the gradual evolution of simpler common ancestral forms (Note: the theory of evolution collapses in the face of modern scientific knowledge, as we have seen in an earlier series of broadcasts and articles). The whole physical world, according to evolutionists, works through mechanical processes blindly directed by the laws of nature, and there is no soul or anything spiritual. So all of man's spiritual faculties must be purely the product of the working of his brain, and appeared as the human brain, evolving over the ages, reached a high degree of complexity. Then consciousness, self-knowledge, feelings and so on sprang out of this biological machine.

But practice shows that such a thing cannot happen. “You can expand the power (of computers) all you want and they still won't be conscious, because all they will be doing is shuffling symbols”, as John Searle, a professor at the University of California, Berkeley, who works on questions of the mind, has said.

But can man, who has spiritual faculties, really be simply a biological machine, and his consciousness merely the product of brain activity? Modern findings and the results of recent scientific research give evidence for the opposite view:

  • If consciousness springs from purely biological functions, then there should be some centre or centres in the brain that produce consciousness. And if these centres are destroyed or stop working for some reason (for example through illness or an accident), the person should lose their personality and their self-awareness.
    But many cases of patients have shown that this does not happen. A typical example is a man named Roger, a large part of whose brain was destroyed by severe viral encephalitis. Specifically, three areas thought to be connected with our self-awareness were almost completely destroyed. Even so, recent medical tests showed that this man's self-awareness remained intact, and apart from memory loss his other mental functions are within normal limits (the newspaper “To Vima”, 23 Aug 2012 – see the related article in the right-hand column).
  • The famous “father of modern neurosurgery”, Wilder Penfield, during his career tried to prove that the mind and consciousness spring solely from brain processes. But he himself revised his original views because of what he observed while operating on more than 1,000 epileptic patients. Specifically, while the patients were conscious, he stimulated certain motor centres of the brain (for example for the hand) with an electric current, causing the corresponding limb to move involuntarily. At the same time he asked the patients to prevent this movement by their own will (for example by using the other hand). So as the patients “fought” against themselves, they admitted that the involuntary movement was not happening by their will. In other words, they regarded themselves as a being separate from their body, and no electrical stimulation affected this perception, or their will, so as to make them believe the movement was voluntary. Penfield dared to give the explanation that man has not only a brain (which is material and was stimulated to cause movement) but also a non-material reality (the mind) that interacts with the brain.
  • A great number of scientific studies and observations around the world provide evidence that human consciousness continues to exist even after the brain has stopped working, that is, while someone has been declared clinically dead. A typical example is a 2001 study by Sam Parnia (a physician) and Peter Fenwick (a neuropsychiatrist at the Institute of Psychiatry in London) of a group of 63 heart attack victims who were declared clinically dead but were later resuscitated and interviewed. About 10% reported that while their brains were not working, they had very well organised and clear thought processes, with formed memories and reasoning (the possibility that this sensation was an impression created in the brain by lack of oxygen or the side effects of drugs was ruled out). The evidence from such research, and from many scientifically verified accounts of near-death experiences (in which people described exactly scenes and conversations that they could not have known about because of their condition – a typical example: Pam Reynolds), shows that self-awareness, thought and memory continue to exist after the brain stops working, and so brain and mind are not the same, but distinct entities.
  • If self-awareness arises from purely mechanical processes taking place in the brain, then it is subject solely to the laws of physics and chemistry and is blindly directed by them. On this view, in effect there is no free will; every reaction of a person is the result of their brain being “programmed” by their genes and the laws of nature. So all people, even criminals, are not responsible for their actions and should not be held accountable for them, because their will is solely the product of a machine (the brain) that works in a particular way and cannot do otherwise.
    But the absurdity and contradiction of this reasoning are obvious, since free will and changing one's mind are the common experience of all people.
    At this point we have the opportunity to mention also that materialists and atheists often accuse Christians of abolishing the idea of free will through faith in God. Yet they themselves, believing that there is no soul, are the first to deny that free will exists, as we said above. So with this world view materialists and atheists, while they supposedly fight to break the chains of faith in God so that people may have free will, are in the end the first who, not believing in the existence of soul and spirit, fall into the contradiction of arguing that free will does not exist.
  • Many people who, because of an accident or illness, fell into a “vegetative” state, when they were at some point able to communicate with those around them (through special computers) stated that despite the condition of their brain, they were fully conscious of themselves and aware of what was happening around them. This shows that even when the brain suffers such damage, the person as a personality continues to exist and to perceive themselves. They are simply unable to communicate and respond to the stimuli they receive.
    So the relationship between brain and mind can be compared to the relationship between a radio set and radio waves. That is, just as the radio is not itself the source of the broadcasts it plays but expresses the information carried by the radio waves transmitted from a station's aerial, so the brain is not the source of consciousness and the mind but the means of their expression. When a radio set cannot produce sound because of a fault, that does not mean the radio station has stopped broadcasting. In the same way, when because of some damage the brain cannot reproduce the expressions of the mind, that does not mean that the mind and self-awareness have ceased to exist or been destroyed.

All the above findings, even for a strictly rational thinker, are very strong evidence that brain and mind are different things, and that man, besides his material body, also has a spiritual nature through which he continues to be aware of himself even when his body and brain stop working wholly or in part.

Holy Scripture also speaks about this subject; here are some examples from it:

  • From the following words of the apostle Paul it is clear that the “I” is not the same as the body, but is something spiritual (the soul) that lives in the body and continues to exist after death:
    “For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain. But if I live in the flesh, this is the fruit of my labour: yet what I shall choose I wot not. For I am in a strait betwixt two, having a desire to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better: nevertheless to abide in the flesh is more needful for you”
    (Philippians 1:21–24)
  • The story of the rich man and Lazarus (Luke 16:19–31) is a statement by Jesus Christ that the soul is something different from the body, and that a person continues to exist and to be aware of themselves, with feelings and memories, after death.

    • The same is implied in the following passage from the words of Jesus Christ:
    “And I say unto you my friends, Be not afraid of them that kill the body, and after that have no more that they can do. But I will forewarn you whom ye shall fear: Fear him, which after he hath killed hath power to cast into hell; yea, I say unto you, Fear him (that is, God)”
    (Luke 12:4–5)

But the promise of eternal life with God concerns not only the soul but the whole person, who consists of soul, spirit and body (see 1 Thessalonians 5:23). Indeed, a person's deeds, done through the body, will be what determines their eternal destiny (see 2 Corinthians 5:10). So God does not despise the human body (which indeed becomes a temple of the Holy Spirit – see 1 Corinthians 3:16–17), but is going to honour it by making it incorruptible at the rapture of the church (1 Corinthians 15:53), so that each believer, not as a “naked” soul but as a whole person, may live for ever in the Kingdom of God. And the “firstborn” of this course and this destiny is Jesus Christ, through His sacrifice on the Cross and His resurrection (Colossians 1:18).
