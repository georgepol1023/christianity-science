The teenage brain and behaviour

The human brain, although it weighs about 1.5 kilos and has an average volume of only 1,200 cubic centimetres, is the most complex system in the universe! It has about 200 billion nerve cells , which are connected to one another by nerve synapses. In the cerebral cortex alone there are 125 trillion synapses– as many as the stars of 1,500 galaxies!

Inside the brain there are areas (centres) that give the commands for carrying out all the functions of the body, and also areas for higher functions such as thought, memory and so on.

In this article we will focus on two areas of the brain:

  • The prefrontal cortex: it lies just behind the forehead and is involved in high-level cognitive functions, decision-making, planning for the future, social interaction and holding back inappropriate behaviour.
  • The limbic system: it includes some areas that lie beneath the cerebral cortex. This system is involved in functions such as processing emotions and the feeling of reward for an effort, and it also gives the urge to take risks.

The brain also contains grey matter, the layer of brain tissue in which mental processes take place (thought, memory, judgement and so on); it gets its name from its colour (the Greek word φαιός means grey).

Until about 15 years ago, scientists believed that the human brain develops almost fully in the first years of life. At that time, of course, it was not possible to observe a living human brain directly. But in the past decade the technology of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was developed, which makes it possible to observe the inside of the brain of a living person. Specifically, with the help of functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) the working of the brain can be imaged in real time and recorded on video.

Many years of research at many research centres around the world have led to the finding that the development of the brain is not completed in the first years of life, but the brain continues to develop during adolescence and into the twenties, sometimes even the thirties.

Scientists have found that during adolescence the prefrontal cortex undergoes significant changes. Specifically, the amount of grey matter in this area increases until about the age of 14, when it reaches its peak, and then decreases as the person approaches adulthood.

This decrease, of course, does not mean that people become less intelligent as they grow up. Rather, because the nerve synapses in the grey matter are shaped by the knowledge and skills a person acquires, as they approach adulthood their brain does away with the synapses it will not need and keeps only those for the knowledge and abilities needed to meet the demands of the particular environment they live in. So this decrease in the amount of grey matter is essentially a “pruning” (“pruning”) of the surplus synapses and a specialising adjustment (“fine-tuning”) of the brain.

====================================================================

Conclusion 1: Adolescence is a period of life when the brain is especially adaptable and malleable. It is a unique opportunity for acquiring knowledge and skills , as well as for learning to develop social relationships.

That is why the Word of God urges:

“Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it” (Proverbs 22:6)

====================================================================

At the same time, in adolescence the limbic system is hypersensitive to the desire for the feeling of reward. For this reason stimuli very often arise that make a teenager's behaviour marked by intense mood swings, reckless actions and risk-taking, questioning of established norms and so on. On the other hand, the prefrontal cortex, which holds back such behaviour (that is, prompts a person to weigh up and reconsider a step before taking it), is not yet fully developed.

So although a teenager has fully formed physical and mental abilities, they do not yet have full critical thinking and composure when it comes to making decisions and planning their future. Moreover, the impulsive behaviour that comes from the stage of development their brain is at can cause very serious problems for themselves and those around them.

And since the development of the brain, as we have seen, continues after adolescence, the above also applies to many young people over the age of 20.

====================================================================

Conclusion 2: The teenager , and more generally the young person, has abilities, but not fully developed judgement. So they need guidance and counsel in facing many of life's questions.

====================================================================

Indeed, recent research by the University of Melbourne points out that the behaviour of 18-year-olds not only does not show maturity but rather childishness, with very few exceptions. This has especially harmful effects, since already at this age the law gives them the right to drink alcohol, smoke and drive – things which, unable to handle them, they end up turning into destructive abuse. The research concludes that adulthood should be recognised in law at the age of 24, because by then the parts of the brain that can handle such excesses are considered fully developed.

So science also confirms that the limits set by older people for the young, which often look like “oppression”, are in reality safety limits. Practice has shown that granting unlimited freedom leads young people to destroy themselves, because they cannot handle it.

As for guidance and counsel, parents play the leading role, together with the family setting, and then the school and the church. And again the Word of God's exhortation to the young is confirmed:

“My son, hear the instruction of thy father, and forsake not the law of thy mother: for they shall be an ornament of grace unto thy head, and chains about thy neck (that is, precious equipment for dealing successfully with life's questions)” (Proverbs 1:8–9)

Indeed, since the source of all wisdom is the fear of God (see Proverbs 9:10), it is essential that the teaching and guidance of parents have the Word of God as their basis. This is exactly where the church helps, when the family too shows the necessary diligence in going to church.

How blessed and privileged teenagers and young people who know the Word of God and grow up in the church should feel! In Holy Scripture there are many shining examples of people who, thanks to their knowledge of the Word of God, though they were young, had the good sense to avoid traps and the wisdom to deal successfully with difficult situations. Daniel and the three young men were young when they were brought captive to Babylon, but God gave them such good sense that they refused to break God's law, and because of their wisdom they rose to become officials of the Babylonian empire. Joseph, at the age of 17, had the good sense to refuse the immoral advances of Potiphar's wife, and the wisdom to oversee prisoners, while later (at 30) he governed the whole Egyptian empire, dealing successfully with the terrible problem of famine.

“I understand more than the ancients, because I keep thy precepts”
(Psalm 119:100)
