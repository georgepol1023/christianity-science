The historical evidence for the resurrection of Jesus Christ

The resurrection of Jesus Christ, the foundation of Christianity, is an event with an entirely consistent historical background, which shows that the resurrection of Christ is an event that undeniably took place within human history.

A strictly rationalist thinker might reasonably ask whether it is legitimate even to examine such a possibility, since an event like the Resurrection is beyond the limits of reason. But such an attitude would be unscientific, because if science followed the rule “what we do not understand, we do not even examine”, no progress would ever have been made in human knowledge. So it is entirely legitimate to investigate whether such a matter of faith can have a historical background, because a consistent historical background gives faith in the Resurrection a firmer foundation. On the other hand, a prejudiced belief, such as the rejection of the resurrection of Christ, which lacks any historical background, cannot stand and must be torn down.

A logical approach to the question of the Resurrection, then, allows only two possibilities:

  • The Resurrection happened
  • The Resurrection did not happen

On this simple statement rests the question of the human soul. For an answer to this question settles whether life continues or not. In other words, it comes down to the question of whether the human soul has value or not. This is why it matters to examine the question of the Resurrection.

If the resurrection of Christ is not a true event, the whole building of the Christian faith collapses, as the apostle Paul says in the 15th chapter of the first epistle to the Corinthians.

The first Christians based the Christian faith on the resurrection of Christ, treating it as an established fact and as the foundation of salvation: “That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved” (Romans 10:9)

The fulfilment of Christ's work was deliberately founded on His resurrection, as He Himself proclaimed. Indeed, when the Lord was asked to perform a sign, He pointed to His resurrection as the most reliable of all the signs and miracles He had done! (see Matthew 12:38–40 & John 2:18–22)

From the four Gospels, which have been shown to be reliable historical sources (see earlier articles), we know more about the events of the death and burial of Christ than we know about the death and burial of any other person in ancient history. No one ever disputed that the tomb was empty. Whether what happened was a resurrection or not, we will examine. But the fact that the tomb was empty was never disputed by anyone. The meaning of the Resurrection may be a theological question, but the fact of the Resurrection is a historical question.

Let us look at the historical facts:

1. Jesus really was dead

  • He was savagely scourged and beaten, and wore a crown of thorns.
  • They “brought him” to Golgotha because He was so exhausted that He could not carry the cross Himself.
  • Crucifixion was a terrible torture. With all His wounds and inflammation He hung half-naked from the nails and, despite His exhaustion, had to push up with His legs in order to breathe.

Death does not take long to come in such conditions; indeed it was a release.

  • The blood and water that came out when the spear pierced Him show medically that death had already taken place (death was not caused by the spear wound).
  • Even if He had not been dead, the spear, passing through the lungs and very probably the pericardium, would have killed Him at once.

2. The tomb

  • We know exactly who took charge of the burial of the body of Jesus.
  • We know the owner of the tomb.
  • We know where the tomb was.
  • The preparations for the burial are described in detail.

3. The burial: the body of Christ is placed in the tomb and secured

  • The grave clothes were wrapped very tightly around the body (the word used is “wound”) and were stuck to the body by the spices, forming a solid casing. It was impossible not only for Him to get out of the grave clothes by Himself, but also for anyone else to unwrap the body. And even if He had not been dead, the sharp smell of the myrrh would have suffocated Him.
  • The entrance of the tomb was covered by a great stone that took several men to roll.
  • The entrance of the tomb was sealed with the Roman seal. By sealing the tomb, the Roman guard certified that they had received a particular dead body. And the seal was protected at all costs by the superbly trained and harshly disciplined Roman soldiers, since on the one hand it represented the authority of the Roman Empire, and on the other, if anyone broke it, the guards faced the death penalty. In short, it was impossible to break into the tomb.

4. The tomb is empty!

  • This fact was never disputed, not even by the enemies of Christ. It was a given, and easy for people of that time to check. Some attempts were made to give a different explanation of what had happened. And all these attempts did nothing more than confirm, on the one hand, that the tomb really was empty and, on the other, that the evidence that it was empty was too overwhelming for anyone to dare claim the opposite.
  • The fact that the grave clothes, tightly wound and stuck together with sticky spices, were empty, and that the napkin that had covered the head of Christ was wrapped together in a place by itself, a little apart, shows that the body had come out of the grave clothes by itself and calmly.

5. Attempts to explain the empty tomb

a. He lost consciousness: Christ did not die on the cross but lost consciousness. He was buried alive and came round many hours later. Answer: We saw above that Jesus was undoubtedly dead. But even if He had not been, after the ordeal He went through and the wounds He had, without medical help and bound in the grave clothes with the strongly toxic spices, He would more likely have died than recovered. And even if He had recovered, it would have been impossible for a weakened man covered in wounds to free himself from the grave clothes on his own, roll away the huge stone, overpower the Roman guard, walk several kilometres and present himself to the disciples. b. The body of Jesus was stolen: Someone stole the body from the tomb during the night. Answer: The enemies of Jesus had no motive, and His frightened disciples had no power to do such a thing. Anyone who tried to steal the body would have had either to overpower the Roman guard first, or to hope that all the guards would fall asleep (which is impossible), and then roll away the stone in complete silence and steal the body in a hurry – not take the trouble to remove the stuck grave clothes, let alone fold them. The story of the stolen body in fact led the Roman soldiers into a contradiction: they spread the word that they had fallen asleep (though they knew this was punished by death), and yet they knew that those who stole the body were, supposedly, the disciples. c. Hallucinations: The disciples seeing the risen Christ was a mass hallucination. Answer: Hallucinations happen to particular kinds of people, when they are in a particular psychological state. Hallucinations are also very individual and extremely subjective. The New Testament says that more than five hundred people of different temperaments, and in different psychological states, met the risen Christ – conditions that do not favour hallucinations – and all their testimonies agree with one another. Nor was the ground fertile for hallucinations, because no one expected to see the Lord risen. Indeed, He Himself had difficulty convincing them of His resurrection.

Since, then, the tomb is empty and none of the above scenarios holds, we are led logically to the conclusion that the Resurrection happened. The way it happened is miraculous, but the event is historical!

Besides, the account of the Resurrection has none of the features of a mythology:

  • A myth needs a good deal of time to spread, so that the generation of the time it refers to has passed away and there is no risk of anyone being able to refute it. But the resurrection of Christ was held to be an undeniable fact from the 3rd day after His death, while on the day of Pentecost and in the years that followed, no one from that generation, or from that area (Jerusalem), came forward to refute the fact of the Resurrection.
  • The hostile chief priests and Pharisees had many motives, and the power and means, to fight and stamp out the spread of the news of the Resurrection. But they could not succeed despite threats and persecution, because everyone was convinced that it was true.
  • The account of the Resurrection is not embellished or decorated with various details meant to impress the reader, as is usual in mythologies.
  • The Gospels present some women as the first witnesses of the Resurrection, which would make no sense for the writer of a mythology who wanted to convince people of the events he describes, since at that time a woman's testimony had no value and her word was not accepted in court.
  • It would have been impossible for the Christians, and especially the apostles, to agree to give up their lives for something they knew very well was a lie. Rather, their love for their Teacher would have turned to hatred when they realised that, although they had left their families and possessions to follow Him, His promises had in the end proved false.

But the risen Christ showed Himself “by many infallible proofs” for forty days, and to more than five hundred people. He appeared to particular people, in particular places and at particular times, which are recorded in detail in the texts of the New Testament (Mark 16:9–14 & 1 Corinthians 15:6–8). The Resurrection was also bodily. The disciples admit with complete honesty and objectivity that they themselves found it hard to be convinced that they were seeing the risen Christ before them, not as a spirit but with a body. And once they were sure of it, there was no room for any doubt in them that their Teacher had truly risen.

The resurrection of Jesus Christ, as a real event, had and still has results:

  • It leads to the transformation of a person's life through new birth and baptism with the Holy Spirit. Death is undone as the consequence of sin, and the person is set free, receives eternal life and becomes a child of God. None of this could still be happening today if Christ had not risen.
  • It confirms the truth of all the words of Christ, since He Himself linked His reliability to His power to rise from the dead. So, since this was true and came to pass, everything else He has said is also true and comes to pass.

If Christ had not risen, the disciples would have felt disappointed and betrayed by a man who persuaded them to leave everything and follow Him, relying on a false hope. But we see the disciples transformed into fearless preachers of the Resurrection, so certain of it that they were willing even to die for this faith. And all the knowing Christians through the centuries who confess their personal experience of Jesus Christ become true witnesses of His resurrection.
