Observing the sky – telescopes

Astronomy is the science that studies the heavenly bodies and their relationships and movements, and is concerned with the observation and interpretation of the related phenomena that take place in the sky. Through the science of astronomy we gain information about the origin, development and physical and chemical properties of the heavenly bodies.

But about 1,000 years BC, Holy Scripture, by the hand of David, declares that from the whole process of observing the sky a final conclusion can be drawn that is decisive for human life, since it reveals the existence of God the Creator:

“The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork” (Psalm 19:1)

In an earlier article we have already seen how newer scientific findings support this statement, while in this article, after giving some history of astronomy and the telescope, we will again arrive at the same conclusion.

The history of astronomy can be divided into three periods:

Ancient period (from the depths of antiquity up to Copernicus – 16th century AD): In this period astronomy developed to serve purely practical needs (keeping a calendar & finding one's direction) and was linked with astrology, since the stars were believed to influence people's lives. Although a great many ancient peoples made astronomical observations (Egyptians, Babylonians, Indians, Chinese, Incas, Aztecs, Maya and others), the scientific development of astronomy is found in ancient Greece (6th century BC), where the first attempts were made to state the laws that govern astronomical phenomena. The most important Greek astronomers were Pythagoras, Aristarchus, Eratosthenes, Hipparchus and Ptolemy. After the fall of Greek and Roman civilisation, astronomy passed into the hands of the Arabs and returned to Europe during the Renaissance.

From the Renaissance to the end of the 19th century: Copernicus puts forward the heliocentric system. The most basic instrument of astronomy, the telescope, had not yet been invented, and all observations were made with the naked eye. The Dane Tycho Brahe was the last astronomer to make important astronomical observations without the help of a telescope. Based on these observations, a little later Kepler put forward his empirical laws for the orbits of the planets. A decisive step for the progress of astronomy was the invention of the telescope at the beginning of the 17th century, with the help of which Galileo made the first important observations.

Specifically, the telescope was invented in 1608 in the Netherlands and its invention is credited to Hans Lippershey (Hans Lippershey) and Zacharias Janssen (Zacharias Janssen), who worked independently, and also to Jacob Metius (Jacob Metius). At first it was a very simple device made of a long tube with a lens at each end, which could magnify the objects observed about three times. Very soon Galileo learned of this invention and began to build improved telescopes with magnifications of up to 30 times, which he used to observe the heavenly bodies, making discoveries that were important for the time. During the 17th century various scientists made great efforts to improve telescopes, because the image they gave until then was blurred and poorly focused, producing chromatic and spherical aberrations.

Modern period (from the beginning of the 20th century to today): Technical means were developed for building large telescopes, so that it became possible to observe and study better formations outside our solar system (stars, galaxies). At the same time techniques for analysing the radiation of the heavenly bodies began to be applied, from which a wealth of information about them is gathered and extracted. So astrophysics was born , which led to an understanding of the structure and development of the stars. Moreover, the formulation of the theory of relativity by Einstein (early 20th century) gave new dimensions to the study of the universe and especially to cosmology.

A decisive invention of this period was the radio telescope (1937). Unlike optical telescopes, which use lenses or mirrors to observe visible electromagnetic radiation (visible light), radio telescopes are essentially receivers (aerials) that can pick up the invisible electromagnetic radiation coming from the heavenly bodies, and analysing it can yield very important information about their age, composition, orbit, condition and so on. A further advantage is that observations with radio telescopes are not affected by optical distortions in the atmosphere or by bad weather, and are not blocked by sunlight and so on. The most powerful radio telescope today is the “ALMA” array (Atacama Large Millimeter Array), which is in the permanently cloudless desert of Atacama in Chile, at an altitude of more than 5,000 metres in the Andes, an ideal spot for astronomical observation. It is an array of 66 large radio dishes linked together. The data come from their combined operation. “ALMA” is expected to lead to many new discoveries. An important discovery already made is the detection of the most distant water molecules yet found in the universe, at a distance of 12.7 billion light years.

Important steps in improving observation with optical telescopes have also been telescope arrays, that is, the combination of many neighbouring optical telescopes so that they work as a single instrument, and also space telescopes, that is, telescopes launched into space which orbit the Earth, and so achieve observations unobstructed by the atmosphere. The first space telescope was the British “Ariel 1” (1962), while the best known is “Hubble” of NASA, which was put into orbit in 1990 at a distance of 568 km from the Earth and is still working today, having taken more than 100,000 detailed photographs of the universe, which have led scientists to important observations and discoveries. Indeed, a new space telescopeis already being prepared, named “James Webb”, with capabilities far beyond those of “Hubble”, which will be placed in orbit at a distance of 1.6 million kilometres from the Earth around 2018. Note that there are both optical space telescopes and radio telescopes in space, or a combination of the two.

Perhaps the most important discovery of astronomy in the 20th century, made through observations with telescopes, is the expansion of the universe. Specifically, in 1929 the famous astronomer Edwin Hubble, observing with a telescope, found that the space between the galaxies is growing, that is, the universe is expanding. This means that, looking back into the past, all the matter and energy of the universe was concentrated in an ever smaller space. So models began to be put forward of the universe originating from an initial point in which all the matter and energy was concentrated, and which began to expand , creating what we today perceive as space within the universe. This event is known as the “Big Bang”, a rather misunderstood term which, however, in its strictly scientific sense, denotes a sudden and very fast but very carefully “designed” and “controlled” release of matter and energy, and not an explosion like a bomb, which would rather have resulted in maximum disorder and not the formation of heavenly bodies and, more generally, the formation of a universe governed by physical laws , which moreover are not chaotic but can be described mathematically.

A direct consequence of the expansion of the universe is the finding that the universe is not infinite and eternal, but is finite and has a beginning and a cause of its existence. This fact confirms the declaration of the first verse of Holy Scripture:

“In the beginning God created the heaven (that is, the universe) and the earth” (Genesis 1:1) This overturned the opposite view of many ancient peoples, which had also been adopted by later scientists (until the beginning of the 20th century), of an infinite, eternal and static universe. Indeed, the expansion of the universe is one more wonderful confirmation of the validity of the prophetic word of Holy Scripture, since there are at least 10 verses in the Old Testament that speak of the expansion of the universe (“the heaven”). More details are given in source no. 16 in the right-hand column.

Another consequence of a universe that is not eternal and not static is the principle of causality, a fundamental principle of modern physics, according to which every phenomenon and every event in the universe has a cause that is independent of it and greater / wider than it (that is, it transcends it). So even the universe itself requires a cause of its existence greater than itself, outside and beyond it.

Based on the characteristics of the universe, the cause of its existence, its Creator, must have the following characteristics:

– Since He created space, He must be outside it!
– Since He created time, He must be Eternal!
– Since He rules over matter and energy, He must be Almighty!
– Since He brought free persons into existence, how much more is He Himself
a person!
– He from whom the world of mathematical ideas and the workings of
thought and reason come must be All-wise!
– He who created and sustains life must be the eternally Living One!

Holy Scripture, showing astonishing consistency with the principle of causality, attributes exactly these properties to the person of God. No other book of any religion matches these characteristics. Indeed, all the ancient creation stories presuppose the existence of the universe and of the matter from which the gods themselves are made (theogony). Only Holy Scripture speaks of creation out of nothing and of a God outside creation.

So we see that modern scientific findings lead inevitably to the conclusion that there is a Creator, who moreover is a Person with particular characteristics. What is more, the validity of Holy Scripture is confirmed, as it shows wonderful accuracy in what it says on these subjects.

Going one step further, we can also conclude that since this Creator took such care of man, placing him on a wonderful planet in an inhospitable universe, He is a good Person, with love for man. And given the validity of Holy Scripture, we learn from it that this Person, God, loved us not only as regards our earthly life, but with a love that extends beyond this life, into eternity. That is why, to the care He already shows for His creature, He has added the fact that He can secure for it a future of eternal life with Him.

And this eternal security is given freely to every person who repents and calls on the Lord to come into their life, believing that Jesus Christ is God who became man, was crucified and died for the sins of every person, and rose again from the dead:

“That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved” (Romans 10:9).
