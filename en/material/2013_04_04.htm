Electronic implants & neo-evolution

The term “implant” means an artificial object or device that is placed in the body to replace a part of the body that is missing or does not work. Implants are widely used in medicine, typical examples being dental implants (which hold artificial teeth), artificial joints in places where the natural joints have worn out (for example the shoulder or knee) and so on.

But besides mechanical implants there are also electronic implants, which usually work on the basis of microchips. The microchip (or simply chip), also called a microprocessor, is a small integrated circuit that can store a significant amount of information, which it can exchange (that is, send and receive) by interacting with suitable machines.

As is obvious, such an electronic device, which can moreover be carried on various objects (cards, passports, cars and so on) and also implanted in the human body, can have a great many applications, medical ones in the first place. In this article we will focus on implanting microchips in the human body.

Some examples of the present-day use of electronic implants with microchips for health reasons are:

– Pacemakers to keep the heart working properly
– An implanted microchip for measuring blood values
– Disabled people moving robotic limbs with a microchip implanted in the brain
– Electronic implants to restore hearing and sight.
– A microchip that controls appetite to treat obesity
... and others.

All the above applications of electronic implants with microchips aim to restore the function of a part that is either missing from the body or present but not working. But in recent years researchers worldwide have been making a growing effort aimed not only at restoring bodily functions in people with illnesses, but also at using electronic implants to extend the bodily abilities of healthy people. The central idea of this undertaking is extending sensory abilities: extending the abilities of the senses and also acquiring new senses, so that people can perceive things in their environment for which they have not until now had the right sense organs.

The philosophical background of this effort is belief in the theory of the biological evolution of species, according to which all living organisms have come about through purely random and blind processes that turned earlier, simpler organisms into more complex ones. This is, of course, a theory that is neither scientific nor able to stand as an idea in the face of modern scientific findings, but is completely overturned by them, as we have seen in an earlier series of articles.

But given the belief in the theory of evolution, the idea is now beginning to emerge and spread that, since the human species has reached a state of “evolutionary equilibrium”, the time has come when we ourselves must “take evolution into our own hands”. It is thought that this can be achieved:

  1. either through genetic engineering, that is, intervening in genes to modify them or even to add new ones, the hoped-for result being improved abilities (memory, physical condition and so on) and also the elimination of many serious diseases (cancer, diabetes and others).
  2. or through electronic implants, which will give new senses that the body has not had until now. Here are some examples from trials carried out on laboratory animals and also on people:
  • Implanting magnets in the fingertips, which, connected to a suitable external circuit and corresponding sensors, can give a sense of the distance or the temperature of objects in the surroundings.
  • Placing infrared sensors on the head, connected to a microchip implanted in the part of the brain that processes information from touch. So although infrared radiation is normally invisible, when the laboratory animals are exposed to it they feel it as touch.
  • In the same way, corresponding implants can be placed directly on nerves to sense, in addition, ultrasound, radio waves, magnetic fields and so on.
  • A “remote” interface, that is, connecting an electronic implant over the internet with sensors or robotic limbs that may be anywhere in the world. In this way the signals these remote sensors receive from their surroundings (pressure, temperature and so on) are felt as if the person were physically present in that remote place (“extending the body”).
  • A “brain-to-brain” interface, that is, transmitting brain signals directly from one person to another. Trials have already been carried out on laboratory animals, transmitting simple brain signals from the brain of one directly into the brain of another. The hoped-for goal is more direct communication between people and the direct transfer of thoughts, images and feelings, without going through their “poor” description in speech.

Through such interventions in the human body the aim is to achieve a new kind of evolution, which will be not only guided but also accelerated, since a process that according to the theory of evolution needs tens or hundreds of thousands of years is thought to be achievable even within a few hundred years.

At this point we would like to make clear that we are not opposed to the progress of technology and its medical applications, nor are we trying to demonise them. On the contrary, we welcome scientific progress, which, however, is used rightly when it is used in the right direction. Indeed, science is a gift of God for improving and easing human work and human life in general. But growing social corruption carries the danger of using such great scientific achievements in a way that will in the end lead to the degradation of the person and to an uncontrolled attempt to redefine the concept of “human being”.

There is of course no objection at all to using technology to cure illness and ease disability. But the mindset fostered alongside the attempts to “improve” human nature is that human life has no special meaning in itself, but the value of each person is determined by the measurable abilities (physical & mental) they may have managed to improve or acquire by the artificial means above.

So if such technologies are affordable only for the wealthier social classes, we are led to an unprecedented class divide of superhumans against ordinary, evolutionarily inferior humans. On the other hand, if such technologies are available to everyone, we are led to a society of inhumanity and harsh competition, where each person will seek to prevail and rise above the rest through an endless effort to keep surpassing their limits by artificial means.

All this, of course, assuming that all these technologies can one day be made to work – something strongly disputed by many members of the international scientific community.

In any case, humanity has had a taste of such situations through the attempts to apply eugenics (early 20th century) and through the harsh and inhuman competition that already rules modern society. And if these mere tastes caused destruction and misery and showed our inability to manage such situations, how much more painful consequences should we expect from these new undertakings?

“There is a way that seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death” (Proverbs 16:25)

So, overlooking these factors, presenting such technologies in an attractive way leads humanity to become familiar with the idea that implanting devices in the body is necessary. Besides, apart from improving bodily abilities, another main area of application of microchip implants in the near future will be locating and tracking people, storing personal details and medical data in the body itself, and financial transactions. And all this on the pretext of safety: avoiding the loss of people (for example the elderly or young children), the loss of personal documents, the theft of money and so on. But imposing such close control and surveillance of every person amounts to a worldwide dictatorship, whose coming and characteristics are so accurately prophesied in the book of Revelation, especially in the 13th chapter, where the calamities and destruction that will follow are also prophesied.

But for the children of God there is a way out: the rapture of the church (see Luke 21 & 1 Thessalonians 4). If there were no way out, all this information and the fulfilment of the prophecies of the Word of God would have no meaning at all, but rather would lead to despair. But because there is hope, the Christian is called and led to holiness and to greater love for Jesus Christ, whom they await as their deliverer from the coming calamities. This, after all, is the character of God as it is revealed throughout Holy Scripture: warning of the coming calamities, and also providing a way out for the person who repents, calls on the name of Jesus Christ and makes the Word of God the compass of their life:

“Because thou hast kept the word of my patience, I also will keep thee from the hour of temptation, which shall come upon all the world, to try them that dwell upon the earth” (Revelation 3:10).
