The 1st & 2nd horses of chapter 6 of Revelation

The 6th chapter of the book of Revelation describes the sending out of four horses (white, red, black and pale) throughout the world, which express prophetically certain conditions that are going to come upon the Earth.

Let us recall that the events described from the 4th chapter of Revelation onwards concern the period after the Christian church has left the Earth at the rapture (see 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18 & 1 Corinthians 15:51–54).

But although the conditions these four horses express are going to reach their peak and full development at that time, we can already observe that the conditions that will lead to them have already begun to take shape. Historical studies and various research give these observations a scientific framework, showing once again the reliability of Holy Scripture and the validity of the prophetic word. In this article we will deal with the 1st and 2nd horses.

1st horse: white

“And I saw, and behold a white horse: and he that sat on him had a bow; and a crown was given unto him: and he went forth conquering, and to conquer” (Revelation 6:2)

Interpretation:

The rider of the white horse is the Word of God who became man, that is, Jesus Christ, as is also made clear in the 19th chapter:

“And I saw heaven opened, and behold a white horse; and he that sat upon him was called Faithful and True, and in righteousness he doth judge and make war. His eyes were as a flame of fire, and on his head were many crowns [...] and he was clothed with a vesture dipped in blood: and his name is called The Word of God. [...] And he hath on his vesture and on his thigh a name written, KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS” (Revelation 19:11–16)

The Word of God is said to ride a horse to show the rapid spread of the preaching of the Gospel worldwide.

The spread of the Word of God is a historically recorded fact that began to take place widely and with great speed from the first centuries of Christianity: by AD 400 it is estimated that about 17% of the world of the time were Christians, while about 40% of people had been evangelised and the Scriptures had already been translated into 11 languages.

A decisive event for the spread of Holy Scripture was the invention of printing by Gutenberg in the middle of the 15th century. Indeed, Holy Scripture was the first book to be printed , and from then until today it has been the most widely circulated book in the world, since its reprints exceed those of any other book (it is the biggest best seller of all time!).

Today Christianity has spread to every continent, reaching even the most “difficult” areas (for example China). It is estimated that every year more than 100 million copies of Holy Scripture (whole or in part), translated into about 2,500 languages, are sold or given away around the world. Moreover, the modern electronic means of communication (mainly the internet: text, sound and video in digital form) are helping to complete the prophesied spread of the Word of God worldwide.

Returning to the features of the 1st horse, we can also say that it is white because the Word of God is completely true, holy and pure. So the mission of the Word to run through the Earth as a white horse, that is, as the bearer of pure truth, is necessary in the face of the multitude of deceptions and of the false christs who, according to the prophetic word of Jesus Christ (Matthew 24:4–5, 23–24), have already appeared from time to time, and will also appear in the period after the rapture of the church (the “great tribulation”).

Also, the fact that the white horse's rider holds a bow is explained in Psalm 45, where the following is said prophetically of Jesus Christ:

“Thine arrows are sharp in the heart of the king's enemies; whereby the people fall under thee” (Psalm 45:5)

In this verse the shooting of arrows (and so the use of a bow) signifies the success of the preaching of the Word of God and the rout of those who tried to hinder this work.

Finally, the rider of the white horse is crowned, because it is ordained from the beginning that He will conquer and in the end prevail (“he went forth conquering, and to conquer”).

2nd horse: red (in the original Greek “pyrrhos” = the colour of fire)

“And there went out another horse that was red (in the original, “pyrrhos”): and power was given to him that sat thereon to take peace from the earth, and that they should kill one another: and there was given unto him a great sword” (Revelation 6:4)

Interpretation:

The red horse, whose rider has the mission of taking peace from the earth, clearly signifies the outbreak of armed conflict, which is also why it is red like fire, while the rider holds a great sword.

As for the phrase “that they should kill one another”, it would not be unfounded to suppose that this horse refers mainly to civil wars. Indeed, it is a fact that civil wars have increased over the centuries, and in the 20th century more civil wars were fought, and with greater frequency, than in all the past centuries of human history.

The next two horses are the “black” horse, which signifies famine on the Earth, and the “pale” (yellowish-green) horse, which signifies the coming of death to a great number of people from various causes and events during the great tribulation (see Revelation 6:5–8).

We hope that in a later article we will have the chance to deal with the next two horses as well, since for them too current events, recorded historical evidence and statistical research show that these are real conditions heading towards a climax, of which we have a foretaste in our days.

“Watch ye therefore, and pray always, that ye may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of man” (Luke 21:36)
