A meteor falls in Russia – the 540th anniversary of the birth of N. Copernicus

On 15 February 2013, a small asteroid with a diameter of 17–20 metres and a mass of about 11,000 tonnes entered the Earth's atmosphere over Russia (at about 09:20 local time) at a speed of about 18 kilometres a second, and became visible as a bright meteor in the northern region of the Ural mountains. Before it entered the atmosphere, because of its relatively small size, it had not been detected by the space agencies responsible, which focus on detecting objects at least 100 metres across.

In the end the meteor exploded in the air at a height estimated at 15 to 25 kilometres, over the Chelyabinsk region, causing an intense flash, fragments and a very powerful shock wave. The force of the explosion was 20 to 30 times greater than that of the atomic bomb dropped on Hiroshima in 1945.

More than 1,000 people were injured, the overwhelming majority by the after-effects of the explosion (damage to buildings from the shock wave, for example broken windows) and not by the meteor itself.

By coincidence, on the same day, but about 16 hours later, another asteroid, named “2012 DA14”, came closer to the Earth than any heavenly object whose orbit had been predicted beforehand. Specifically, “2012 DA14” passed just 27,700 kilometres from the Earth – a distance smaller not only than that of the Moon from the Earth, but even than the distance at which many communications satellites orbit. The space agencies NASA and ESA confirm that the two events are not related.

The orbit of “2012 DA14” had been calculated precisely by NASA, and any possibility of impact had been ruled out. However, if such an object, about 30 metres across and with a mass of about 40,000 tonnes, struck the Earth, it would release energy of about 2.5 megatonnes of TNT, as much as a powerful nuclear weapon. Such an event happened in 1908, when a meteor 30–40 metres across flattened 1,200 square kilometres of forest in the uninhabited Tunguska region of Siberia.

To explain some of the terms used above, let us make clear that:

  • Asteroids are rocky bodies of relatively small size (a few tens of metres to a few hundred kilometres) in orbit around the Sun.
  • Comets are heavenly bodies of greater size (up to a third of the Earth's diameter) that orbit the Sun; as they come close to it, the volatile materials they are mainly made of turn to gas as the temperature rises, creating a gaseous envelope called the coma, which in most cases stretches out to form the comet's tail.
  • Meteors are the luminous phenomena that appear in the Earth's atmosphere when a meteoroid enters it – that is, a rocky body ranging in size from a few millimetres to several metres. Friction with the atmosphere ionises the air molecules and so gives off light. Small meteors are also called shooting stars, while larger ones are called fireballs. Because of friction with the atmosphere, meteors overheat and vaporise before they reach the Earth's surface.
  • A meteorite is a heavenly body that first enters the Earth's atmosphere and appears as a meteor, but then, because of its great mass, does not have time to vaporise completely and reaches the Earth's surface, possibly forming a crater.

The appearance of meteors and the sense of threat from heavenly objects are mentioned in Holy Scripture as signs of the days that will come before the second coming of Jesus Christ to receive His church (the rapture of the church):

“And great earthquakes shall be in divers places, and famines, and pestilences; and fearful sights and great signs shall there be from heaven” (Luke 21:11)

While later, during the seven years of the Antichrist (the “great tribulation”) and before the Second Coming of Jesus Christ, catastrophic falls of meteorites are also spoken of:

“And the third angel sounded, and there fell a great star from heaven, burning as it were a lamp, and it fell upon the third part of the rivers, and upon the fountains of waters” (Revelation 8:10) “But in those days, after that tribulation, the sun shall be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars of heaven shall fall, and the powers that are in heaven shall be shaken. And then shall they see the Son of man coming in the clouds with great power and glory” (Mark 13:24–26)

Nicolaus Copernicus (19 February 1473 – 24 May 1543) was a Polish astronomer who became known because, shortly before his death, he published the work “Six Books on the Revolutions of the Heavenly Spheres” (“De Revolutionibus Orbium Coelestium Libri VI”), which was the foundation for the development of modern astronomy. In this work Copernicus argued for the heliocentric theory, the view that the Sun, and not the Earth, was at the centre of our solar system, as had been believed until then.

The view that the Earth is motionless at the centre of the universe and all the heavenly bodies move around it (the geocentric system) is found as early as the Presocratic philosophers (6th century BC), but the basic principles of this model were made clear by Plato and his pupil Aristotle in the 4th century BC. Later, in the 2nd century AD, Ptolemy refined and established the geocentric system (also called the Ptolemaic system), introducing the right mathematics and the famous epicyclic motions. This model explained the movements of the planets very well (with an error of only a few degrees), which is why it prevailed for about 14 centuries. But some centuries before Ptolemy, Aristarchus of Samos (3rd century BC) had proposed a heliocentric model for the solar system.

Copernicus, having studied Ptolemy's system, considered that the geocentric system, with all its complicated epicyclic motions, could not match reality. At the same time, with Aristarchus's theory in mind, he put forward the view that the Sun is at the centre, and all the other planets, including the Earth, move around it and also turn on their own axes, which simplifies the explanation of the apparent motion of the heavenly bodies as seen from the surface of the Earth. For fear of being misunderstood by the people around him, by the intellectual circles of his time and by the Roman Catholic Church, Copernicus did not publish his theory until a few years before his death , and under pressure from his student Rheticus. Even then, as a precaution, the preface of his work pointed out that this theory did not necessarily match reality, but was merely a hypothetical approach that would make the study of the movements of the heavenly bodies, and the calculations about them, easier.

Until then, Copernicus's view had spread in fragments or in summary, but it had reached the pope of the time, Clement VII, who received it favourably. After the death of Copernicus (1543) there was at first no reaction, and by the beginning of the 17th century a good number of astronomers and educated people of the time knew of and supported the new theory.

As for the Roman Catholic Church, its initially favourable attitude was reversed in less than a century. In 1600 the monk Giordano Bruno, a supporter of the Copernican theory (who also added to it the view that the countless stars are suns like ours with planets around them), was condemned to death by burning in Rome after seven years' imprisonment. In 1616 the court of the Inquisition condemned the heliocentric system, and the work of Copernicus was placed on the list of forbidden books until 1835.

These reactions were due to the fact that the discovery of heliocentrism went against the infallibility and authority of the religious establishment, since on the one hand it challenged the belief that Holy Scripture supports the geocentric system, while on the other it could foster a cultural revolution in which accepted values, set ideas and established authorities would be questioned by people who had until then submitted to them obediently.

As for the first point, we should make clear that the idea that Holy Scripture supports the geocentric system is mistaken. It comes from misinterpreting a passage in the book of Joshua (10:12–13), in which the narrative seems to give the impression that the Earth stands still while the Sun moves. But we must not forget that Holy Scripture, although it contains consistent scientific statements, is not a science textbook but is written in simple, everyday language. So that passage can in no way be used as an argument that Holy Scripture supports the geocentric system, because the narrative describes the event in the simple language of an observer on the surface of the Earth. Even today we often use expressions such as “the Sun went behind the trees”, not because we believe in the geocentric system but because we want to express the event simply, as observers on the surface of the Earth.

As for the second point, it is true that the scientific discoveries of the following centuries broadened humanity's intellectual horizons and freed it from blind submission to religious establishments. But with the collapse of all these systems the truths of Holy Scripture should not be flattened too; in reality they do not rest on suppressing scientific progress, nor do they need to be imposed by force, but their power shows itself in a way that is natural and irrefutable.

So, often, under the mistaken impression that science has overturned facts of Holy Scripture, the following line of reasoning is put forward:

  • Copernicus, with the heliocentric system, drove the Earth out of the centre of the world, putting it in its real place: an insignificant grain of sand in the universe, an ordinary planet among so many others that must have life.
  • Darwin, two centuries later, drove man out of the centre of creation, revealing his animal descent and that his existence is simply the result of a random, blind process (evolution).
  • The modern view (itself the result of the evolutionary view of the world) that the mind and self-awareness are solely the products of the biological working of the human brain gave man the final blow, revealing to him that he cannot even claim to be the centre of himself.

It was thought that the results of this reasoning would lead man to psychological freedom, progress and a strengthened personality, because they would lead him to “an awareness of reality”. Yet we see that belief in man's purely random and animal (and so insignificant in the universe) origin takes away life's meaning, and so makes the pursuit of personal advancement and physical pleasure the main goal.

But such a world view is a failure, not only because it is responsible for the confusion and unhappiness that run through modern human societies, but also because all three steps of the reasoning above are overturned by the newer scientific findings (more details are given in the related material in the right-hand column):

  • The theory of evolution is overturned by modern findings in molecular biology, medicine, palaeontology and other fields of science, which point to the intervention of a Creator, not to random evolution.
  • The idea that the mind and self-awareness are products of the biological working of the brain and that there is no soul is refuted by scientific studies showing that consciousness and reason continue to exist in cases of damage to a large part of the brain, or even when it stops working altogether.
  • The planet Earth is not an insignificant “grain of sand” in the universe, one of countless planets on which life happened to appear. On the contrary, it is the only planet that has the right combination of all the conditions needed for life (the anthropic principle). If even one of these parameters (of which there are more than 300) had a slightly different value, life on Earth would be impossible. Moreover, it is statistically impossible for all these coincidences to exist all at once, each to the right degree, anywhere else in the universe.

    The Earth is indeed a very small planet in the vast universe, but not at all a chance one. It is not the centre of the universe or of the solar system, but it is the centre of God's attention. And He did not show this attention by placing the Earth at the centre of the universe, as the various religious systems supposed (indeed, the universe has no centre), but by showing His love in action: He shaped the Earth to be a hospitable environment in the cold, dark expanse of space, so as to place on it His beloved creature, man (who really is the centre of God's creation), and moreover, on this planet God, though He Himself transcends the whole universe, chose to walk as a man, in the person of Jesus Christ, to shed His blood on the cross for the sins of every person, and to give them eternal life through His resurrection.
