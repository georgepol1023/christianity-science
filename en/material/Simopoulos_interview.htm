Interview with the physicist and astronomer Dionysis Simopoulos

Interview with the physicist and astronomer Dionysis Simopoulos:

  • The initial conditions of the creation of the universe have not yet been worked out, but they are being studied with modern nuclear physics experiments, and efforts are being made to unify the general theory of relativity and quantum mechanics.
  • The expansion of the universe is an established fact. Mr Simopoulos briefly described the history of the discovery of the expansion of the universe, which was discovered by observation by Hubble in 1929, who saw that the space between the galaxies is growing. This had been put forward in theory a few years earlier by Lemaître, who based it on Einstein's equations of general relativity; although these showed that the universe should be expanding, Einstein believed he had made a mistake.
  • So, starting with the Russian physicist George Gamow, models were developed in which the universe came from an initial point where all energy and matter were concentrated, and which began to expand. This is the so-called “Big Bang”. It is not literally an explosion, like for example a bomb exploding, but a very fast expansion of space and time, which was mockingly named the “Big Bang”.
  • The remnants of the Big Bang are microwaves – the so-called cosmic microwave background radiation, which was predicted in theory and whose existence was confirmed experimentally by Penzias and Wilson in the 1960s. Two modern satellites, COBE and WMAP, studied the cosmic microwave background radiation and its variations, from which the stars and galaxies formed, more precisely. In this way the age of the universe, 13.7 billion years, was made clear, and its components were identified (matter as we know it, and the so-called dark matter and dark energy, which we do not yet know exactly what they are).
