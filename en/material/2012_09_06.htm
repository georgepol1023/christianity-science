A worldwide epidemic of mental disorders

A simple search on the internet about mental disorders and how they are spreading is enough to understand the size of the problem that exists worldwide in our time.

An indication of how serious the situation is, is the estimate that by 2020 depression will be the second cause of death worldwide, and the first cause of death in Western societies. Indeed, since 1994, 10 October has been established each year as World Mental Health Day, aiming to inform public opinion and raise awareness of a problem that has now taken on great proportions.

This really is an epidemic of mental disorders, since the number of people suffering from depression worldwide exceeds 154 million, those suffering from schizophrenia reach 25 million, and 95 million people suffer from disorders related to alcohol abuse. As for our country, the various mental illnesses affect 10–12% of the population, but only one in two seeks medical help. As for depression in particular, it is a condition that occurs in every country in the world, can appear at any age (note that it occurs twice as often in women as in men), and its onset is often not linked to any particular event.

There are, of course, factors that decisively affect the human psyche and can lead to depression, anxiety, phobias and other mental disorders. Some of the main ones are:

  • Traumatic experiences within the family, especially in childhood.
  • Modern social and economic conditions and demands (here unemployment also stands out as a major factor).
  • The news, information and stimuli the human mind receives every day. More specifically, the dizzying speed at which news and information spread today (which can often be exaggerated or even false when it comes from anonymous private sources, such as blogs, social networking pages and so on), together with the barrage of violent scenes on television.
  • Making the acquisition of money and the adoption of a particular lifestyle the most important values, overlooking the value of true love and often even of human life itself. So a lack of love, combined with failing to attain the (false) values held up to us, creates a sense that life has no meaning and can lead to a mental breakdown.

By contrast, as research shows, marriage is good for health and reduces the risk of depression and anxiety, unlike loneliness and separation.

It is also a fact that mental health is inseparably linked to physical health. People who suffer mentally are at risk of a great number of physical illnesses too, and the reverse is also true: people who suffer physically are at risk, because of their condition, of developing some mental disorder.

Holy Scripture, about 1,000 years before Christ, reveals the connection between physical and mental health:

“A merry heart doeth good like a medicine: but a broken spirit drieth the bones” (Proverbs 17:22)

At this point we would like to express our conviction that this spread of mental disorders to the point of an epidemic has deeply spiritual roots. Specifically, it is due to people moving away from the principles of the Gospel of Jesus Christ and from the fruit of the Holy Spirit.

And this becomes clear when we compare the main factors behind mental disorders mentioned above with the principles of the Gospel, in which:

  • Instructions are given for harmonious family relationships.
  • It turns people away from behaviour that creates oppression in working and social relationships.
  • Guidance is given for securing peace of mind even in the midst of turmoil, and it turns people away from violent behaviour.
  • The real value of human life is shown, which moreover has an eternal future, and the rottenness of the world view based on money and pleasure is laid bare. At the same time, the person of Jesus Christ is revealed as the one who gives life meaning, as a source of joy, as strength and companionship in sorrows, and as a firm foundation and a deliverer in difficult situations.
  • Marriage and social relationships are encouraged on the basis of offering love, the high point being God Himself becoming man in the person of Jesus Christ and His sacrifice on the Cross, in order to come into a fellowship of love with His creature.

(On all of this see, for example, the Epistle to the Ephesians chapters 5–6, the Epistle to the Philippians chapter 4 and the Epistle to the Colossians chapter 3)

A great many of the Gospel's instructions are at the same time recommendations of modern psychology too for preventing and treating mental disorders. But the Word of God does not stop at encouragement only; also gives a person the strength to move on to the practical part , and in the end secures for them the fulfilment of the goal, which is the safeguarding of mental health.

Yet, although the Gospel way of life and the presence of Christ in a person's life can heal them and keep them steadily safe from mental disorders, it sometimes happens that even born-again Christians see problems of this kind arise in their lives.

In such cases, the first thing a Christian should bear in mind is not to lose heart. For Holy Scripture recognises that as human beings (even though we are God's) we undergo changes and emotional ups and downs, from which even the strongest people of God were not spared at times (see David, the prophet Elijah, the apostle Paul and others). Secondly, we should know that God does not leave any of His children without help, even if they are in a state of emotional turmoil through their own fault. For God, as a perfect Father, does not intend to abandon His children but to support them:

“For he hath said, ‘I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.’ So that we may boldly say, ‘The Lord is my helper, and I will not fear.’” (Hebrews 13:5–6)

So the Lord shows understanding of our condition. That is also why He became man, so as to share fully in human weakness (Hebrews 4:15).

Next, we should identify the factors that shape this emotional state within us. The Lord Jesus Christ said that sorrows and trials will inevitably come into a person's life. But if someone receives His Word and puts it into practice, it is like building on the rock: the storms will strike, but the house will not fall (Matthew 7:24–27). So, by examining ourselves and reviewing our lives and habits, we should check whether our heart is turned towards the Word of God, so that it stays protected, or whether our attention has been focused on other things, so that the stress-inducing stimuli we receive meet no resistance as they enter our mind and so affect our emotional state.

So the effort to combat anxiety, depression and mental disorders in general focuses on two areas:

  • Care about the spiritual conditions we expose ourselves to
    For example, many hours spent on the media and on worldly entertainment, constant company with people whose outlook is foreign to the Christian one, various hobbies that are not sinful but absorb the mind and heart and so on, are certainly factors that threaten to wear a Christian down spiritually. But since some of these cannot be avoided, the time given to them must be managed properly. We should also avoid piling up useless burdens, which often trap a person into weighing themselves down in an effort to secure a richer way of life.
  • Cultivating a personal relationship with the Lord
    This is achieved through studying the Word of God and trying to put it into practice, through prayer, fellowship with one another and taking part in Holy Communion (see Acts 2:42). In these things a person experiences the presence of God, and then on the one hand understands that happiness does not depend on earthly things and circumstances, and on the other receives the assurance that their whole life is safe in God's hands. Then the joy and love of God prevail within them, and there is no room at all for depression, phobias and other mental disorders, even if outward circumstances are as bad as they can be.

Emotional ups and downs will of course come, as we said. But the more a person learns to abide in all the above, the more spiritual resistance and reserves are built up within them, through which they can face these troubles while remaining ever more firmly in the peace of God's presence.
