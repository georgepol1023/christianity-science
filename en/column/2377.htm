Scientists with a deep faith in God (Part 1)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

September 2025 · The “Christianity & Science” team

The history of science is rich in the stories of great people who not only revolutionised their fields but also kept a deep faith in God, which shaped their approach to research. These pioneers were not simply outstanding in their scientific pursuits; they also believed that their work was a way of revealing the divine order of the universe. For them, science was a path to understanding God’s creation, and their religious convictions fuelled their passion for exploration and innovation.

“When I consider thy heavens, the work of thy fingers, the moon and the stars, which thou hast ordained…” says the psalmist (Psalm 8:3).

From Isaac Newton’s laws of motion to Max Planck’s quantum theory, each of these scientists combined faith and reason in ways that continue to inspire. For them the natural world was a reflection of divine wisdom, and their scientific work was a form of worship and a way of coming into contact with the sacred.

This article shows how their faith in God influenced their pioneering discoveries. Contrary to the view held by many that it is incompatible for a scientist to be a Christian as well, the lives and careers of great scientists of the past, who decisively influenced the development of scientific knowledge, reveal exactly the opposite.

Isaac Newton (1642–1727)

Isaac Newton is considered one of the greatest scientists of all time, whose pioneering work in physics, mathematics and astronomy laid the foundations of modern science.

Scientific contribution

Newton’s best-known contributions are the three laws of motion and the law of universal gravitation. These laws provided a complete framework for understanding the mechanics of the natural world, and made it possible to predict the motion of all bodies, from falling apples to the orbits of the planets, showing that the same physical laws apply both on Earth and in the heavens.

Besides his work in physics, Newton independently developed calculus, a new branch of mathematics that provides powerful tools for studying change and motion.

Faith in God

Newton was not simply a scientist. Throughout his life he spent more time studying the Scriptures than scientific writings. He wrote at great length on biblical prophecy, on the nature of God and on the relationship between science and faith. Newton believed in a rational order in the universe, sustained by a divine Being, and saw his scientific discoveries as an effort to reveal the principles that God Himself had laid down.

Newton wrote: “This most beautiful system of the sun, planets, and comets, could only proceed from the counsel and dominion of an intelligent and powerful Being.” He believed that God had given people the ability to reason and to discover the natural laws that govern the universe.

Despite his remarkable scientific achievements, Newton was humble about his work, attributing his ideas more to divine inspiration than to his own intellect. The famous saying is his: “If I have seen further, it is by standing on the shoulders of giants!”, acknowledging the contributions of earlier generations of scientists, but also expressing a deeper spiritual humility.

The Bible tells us that “The fear of the LORD is the instruction of wisdom; and before honour is humility.” (Proverbs 15:33)

In conclusion, we would say that the real equipment of a scientist who wants to explore the wonders of creation through science is the fear of God and a humble heart.

In the next article we will go on to the life and work of the French mathematician and physicist Blaise Pascal, the French chemist Louis Pasteur and the great German theoretical physicist Max Planck.
