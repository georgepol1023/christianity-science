Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

Dangers to children from using electronic devices

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

May 2017 · The “Christianity & Science” team

Although we are already in the third month of this school year, we feel the need to raise the very important subject of the dangers of electronic devices for children of school age, and how parents can manage this in the family.

Under the headline “Screens are killing grades”, a well-known newspaper presents British research according to which every extra hour a teenager spends at home in front of a screen (television, computer, games console, phone) lowers his marks at school. Even when a pupil studies hard at home, if he spends the rest of his time “glued” to the internet or the television, he still gets relatively worse marks than pupils who do not spend so much time in front of a screen.

At the same time there is a real “explosion” of internet addiction: according to recent research, the number of Greek teenagers judged to be addicted to the internet doubled between 2006 and 2011.

These reports are only examples. Looking through recent news, we find a host of reports that show the size of the problem and the dangers ahead – a subject we have raised repeatedly in the past. So we prefer to focus on how to deal with such situations.

First we should recognise that the use of electronic devices is now established even at the youngest ages, for communication, work, study and so on. So the “lure” of technology and the internet is a given in our time. That is why we are not called to reject the devices or the internet, but to make their use healthy, by setting some sensible limits and above all by not allowing them to take the place of human relationships.

This is exactly where the key to the matter lies. For the need to take refuge in electronic media arises as a way out and an alternative when the refuge of human relationships is missing – in this case, healthy relationships within the family. So it is clear that dealing with the problem falls mainly on parents. Let us look, then, at some of the directions parents should take:

  • The home should be a peaceful environment. Problems certainly exist and will always arise, but when there is love, mutual understanding, patience, harmony and above all the handing over of our problems to the Lord Jesus Christ, then however great the problem, He will keep His peace in our hearts (Philippians 4:6–9) and will bring good outcomes (Psalm 37:5).
  • Parents themselves should give time to being with their children, whether in the children’s own interests (lessons, experiences at school and so on) or in various other activities in and out of the home in which the parents also take part, so that the child’s need to turn to electronic devices for entertainment or to pass the time is reduced. In this way, too, the child feels loved and accepted, and does not look for acceptance by creating virtual worlds and fake profiles on social networks, following the dictates of today’s corrupt fashion.
  • Encouraging the child to develop a personal relationship with the Lord, through teaching from the Word of God, admonition and, at the same time, the healthy example of the parents’ spiritual life.
  • Unconditional love, but with limits. We spoke about love above. As for limits, it is possible, after discussion and by mutual agreement, to set some limits – for example, deciding to stay away from smart devices at the “crucial” times that should be given without distraction to the relationships between members of the family (such as mealtimes).

But the crown of all this – what is needed for all of it to be deeply understood and applied successfully – is the wisdom that flows from the Word of God. Through this article we want especially to encourage parents to pray, for themselves and for their children, and above all to study the Word of God, so that, made wise by it and learning from their personal relationship with God, who is the perfect Father, they can guide their own children safely through the dangers that lurk in the misuse of modern electronic devices.
