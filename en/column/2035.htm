Does atheism have answers? (Part 4) The existence of man

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

January 2021 · The “Christianity & Science” team

“What is man, that thou art mindful of him? […] thou crownedst him with glory and honour, and didst set him over the works of thy hands” (Hebrews 2:6–7)

From time to time we have all seen drawings of “ape-men” with large jaws, jutting brows, hairy bodies, a vacant stare and so on, presented as ancestors of modern man. But how many of us know that these pictures are based on incomplete finds and fragments of small parts of skeletons, and so are for the most part imaginary, hypothetical reconstructions and not a record of reality?

The atheistic view of the world holds that man is the product of random processes of evolution from less perfect earlier forms to more perfect ones. But can such a view stand up scientifically?

Many may hurry to point to the roughly 95% similarity between the genetic material (DNA) of the chimpanzee and that of man. But this argument collapses in the face of the fact that human DNA is also significantly similar to that of the cat, the cow and even the fruit fly, while the similarity between human and banana DNA reaches 50%! What is more, a person would not survive a blood transfusion from a chimpanzee.

The human body is a real engineering marvel, made of water and proteins, salts, lipids, carbohydrates and two nucleic acids, DNA and RNA. The complexity of their structure, and the truly ingenious way each of these components is made and used, are the subject of constant research, and although they were supposedly “created by chance”, modern science has still not fathomed them completely. About 40 trillion cells make up the human body. According to the American chemist, biochemist and professor Linus Pauling, “a single cell is more complex than the whole city of New York”. All our cells have identical DNA, and yet they do not all express the same parts of it. So in the human body there are more than 100 different types of cell, which connect in particular ways to form the various parts of the body. They “know” exactly what they must do because of the proteins they produce, and so each carries out the right process at the right time and in the right place. How can all this happen if they were not created for a particular purpose, and how does atheism explain it all?

The well-known atheist Richard Dawkins admits that the human body is “incredibly well designed, beautifully designed and far more complex than any human machine”. Nevertheless he insists, vaguely and without foundation, that “the blind forces of physics could, over long periods of time, build these complex machines”. Today we know that a person’s life begins when just one of the roughly 300,000,000 sperm released in an act of union enters an egg and fertilises it. From that moment a flood of processes begins, and a host of specialised mechanisms are switched on, some of which, God willing, we will speak about in our next feature.

As we close our article, we would like to give as a message of hope for the new year the conclusion, from a scientific point of view too, that every person is unique and precious before God. The heavenly Father not only made him but also provided for his life into eternity, sending His Son to be born on Earth and to offer Himself as a sacrifice for every person personally, for the forgiveness of sins:

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.” (John 3:16)
