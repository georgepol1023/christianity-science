Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

The theory of evolution and the (dizzyingly) growing economic (and social) inequalities

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

February 2017 · The “Christianity & Science” team

“The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor…” (Luke 4:18)

In 1859 Charles Darwin put forward the theory of “the evolution of species by natural selection”. According to it, through the random interaction of matter and time one species was turned into another through the dominance of the strongest individuals of each species, those best adapted to the environment.

Science (biology, physics, mathematics, information science, palaeontology) no longer accepts this view, but recognises that apes were always apes, humans were always humans, and animals and plants were made from the beginning “after their kind”. In the social sphere, the theory of natural selection holds that the best society is one whose members look after only themselves and try to get ahead, if possible at the expense of others. So progress through natural selection requires brutality, and even the destruction of the weak and the free dominance of the “strong”. It requires the complete elimination of everything considered weak, and the domination of the strong over the rest. When individuals, groups or even whole nations embrace natural selection as a means of progress, the way opens to callous cruelty. Indeed, those who mercilessly eliminate the weak are praised, because in this way they help the evolution of “man”! “There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.” (Proverbs 14:12). A glaring example of the dead ends that come from rejecting the principles of the Gospel is (once again) the findings of the economic leaders who met again this January in Davos, Switzerland. The 8 richest people in the world have as much wealth as the poorest 50% of the planet put together. In a new report, the well-known NGO Oxfam warns of the ever-increasing and dangerous concentration of wealth. Last year Oxfam stated that the world’s 62 richest billionaires were as rich as half the world’s population.

But this number fell to just eight in 2017. The organisation is calling for a new economic model to reverse the inequality, which will cause uncontrollable reactions. But that is unthinkable for the follower of social Darwinism. It is truly unthinkable for the devotee of natural selection that “God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life” (John 3:16). And yet the Lord Jesus Christ, “by [whom] were all things created… and by him all things consist” (Colossians 1:16–17), “came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many” (Matthew 20:28). Indeed, in this world He did not choose the “strong”, the “clever” or the “noble” – in short, those whom people consider the best – but called all who labour and are heavy laden, to give them rest (Matthew 11:28). “…[He] is come to seek and to save that which was lost” (Luke 19:10).

True Christians, that is, the disciples of Jesus Christ (Acts 11:26), repent and take on the mind and attitude of their Lord. “Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus: who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: but made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: and being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross…” (Philippians 2:5–8). In their life before they knew Christ, they may have lived as “foolish, disobedient, deceived, serving divers lusts and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, and hating one another” (Titus 3:3). But since they came to know Christ and received Him into their hearts, they live with Him and for Him who, “though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich” (2 Corinthians 8:9). It is revealed to them that real strength is not cruelty and arrogance, but the truly strong bending down over the exhausted, the weak and the sick to help them up: that is, real strength is godly love. “We then that are strong ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not to please ourselves. Let every one of us please his neighbour for his good to edification.

For even Christ pleased not himself…” (Romans 15:1–3). The only One who can and will make the great change, at His coming from heaven, is the Lord Jesus Christ. “And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.” (Revelation 22:17).
