The social conformity experiment and where the Christian stands! (Part 2)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

August 2021 · The “Christianity & Science” team

In the previous article we looked at the social experiment of the psychologist Solomon Asch, which took place in 1950. We saw the effect the opinion of the wider social group can have on a person’s decisions, and how easy it is for someone to give in to the opinion of the majority, even when it is clearly wrong.

In the basic experiment a volunteer was asked to compare the length of some reference lines in front of a group of 6 people who had already given a wrong answer by prior agreement. But the researcher went on to two variations of the basic experiment, from which he found very important aspects of the human psyche that have great spiritual consequences in the Christian’s life.

In the 1st variation, unlike the basic experiment where all the other participants gave a wrong answer, there was also a so-called “partner” who gave the right one. The result was that the group’s influence lost almost all its power, and the subject answered most of the questions correctly.

It seems, then, that a group’s power lies not in its numbers but in its unanimity. When that is broken, the influence it exerts is lost too! In the spiritual realm this has direct consequences. God gave Daniel three blessed companions in Babylon, so that he could resist the influence of the other young men who compromised with the king’s commands and the customs of the time. Let us pray that the Lord will give such blessed companions to our children in their school and university communities.

There is power in unanimity and unity – either for good (“but we ourselves together will build unto the LORD God of Israel” Ezra 4:3), or for evil (“Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do” Genesis 11:6). As the Church of Christ we must always take care to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace. Indeed, we should reach the point “that ye all speak the same thing, and that there be no divisions among you; but that ye be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment”. For then God’s blessing will be abundant in our lives, and we will enjoy the peace of God!

“Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!” Psalm 133:1

In the 2nd variation of the experiment, the volunteer was asked this time to state his answer in writing rather than openly, so avoiding exposure to the others and the “reproach” that goes with it. As a result, the group lost its power and the subject again answered correctly in most cases – but this time for a different reason. This reminds us of the secret disciples in the time of Christ: “Nevertheless among the chief rulers also many believed on him; but because of the Pharisees they did not confess him, lest they should be put out of the synagogue: for they loved the praise of men more than the praise of God.” John 12:42–43. It is easy to think inwardly that you hold the truth, while outwardly conforming to the dictates of society: to be a secret disciple, so that you have the world’s acceptance and a quiet conscience too.

But it is a time for decisions! The hour will come when you can no longer walk two roads, when your faith will either be expressed in public or wither away in compromise.

That is why the Lord calls you today to confess Him publicly. For it is written: “Whosoever therefore shall confess me before men, him will I confess also before my Father which is in heaven.” Matthew 10:32
