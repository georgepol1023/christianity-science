Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

Human life, the highest good: are we missing something?

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

April 2022 · The “Christianity & Science” team

Many views have been put forward on the question of when a human being begins to exist as an entity whose life has value. In our day, when with the help of science we can know many details about every stage of an embryo’s development in the womb, it is common to propose some point in pregnancy at which a basic bodily function appears: for example, when the heart begins to beat, when brain activity can be detected, when the foetus begins to move, when the foetus can feel pain, when the foetus is considered viable, and so on.

But, again thanks to modern scientific knowledge, we can conclude with certainty that the moment that marks the beginning of a human being’s existence is the moment of conception. The fertilisation of the egg by a sperm is the decisive point at which something completely different comes into existence from what was there just before: a new human organism instead of two reproductive cells. From the very first moment the fertilised egg (the zygote) is a cell with a complete genetic code, which comes from the parents but is different from both the father’s and the mother’s. It is the first cell of a new human organism whose genetic characteristics (sex, skin colour, eye colour and so on) have all been fully determined from the start. So it is a new human being that has just begun its journey of growth!

In our time, perhaps more than ever, the ideal of protecting human life and dignity is exalted and promoted. So we constantly hear condemnation of acts of violence against vulnerable people – minors, defenceless women, sports fans and others – and rightly so, and the proper authorities take care that justice is done. Even the health measures and the painful restrictions imposed during the coronavirus pandemic were aimed at protecting the highest good, human life.

On the other hand, it is striking that statistics report about 350,000 abortions a year in our country (officially declared). By some simple calculations, such a number means that on every working day of the year more than 1,000 unborn lives are taken!

Why this great contrast and contradiction in the values of modern human societies? Human life is recognised as the highest good, yet its taking is justified with remarkable ease, on the altar of enjoying the pleasures of the flesh without wanting to take responsibility, or as an attempt to “correct” some unfortunate or painful event by committing an even worse one.

“He (the devil) was a murderer from the beginning, and abode not in the truth, because there is no truth in him.” (John 8:44).

“The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.” (John 10:10).

These are the words of the Lord Jesus Christ, for whom in a few days we will be exchanging the Easter greeting “Christ is risen”. He is the One who created us and gives life and an eternal soul to every child that is conceived. He is the One who, to save us from eternal destruction, became man and shed His blood on the cross. But He is also the One who rose again for our justification, intercedes for us, and is coming soon to receive His church, and then to judge the world in righteousness.

Dear reader, today you are invited to accept the gift of eternal life. God is ready to forgive and wipe out every sin of the past, giving you a new beginning:

“And the times of this ignorance God winked at; but now commandeth all men every where to repent: because he hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness” (Acts 17:30–31)
