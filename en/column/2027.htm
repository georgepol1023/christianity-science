Does atheism have answers? (Part 2)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

November 2020 · The “Christianity & Science” team

The creation of the world

The scientific discoveries of the 20th and 21st centuries in astronomy and cosmology come to support this so simple yet so true description in the 1st verse of the Bible:

“In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.” (Genesis 1:1)

Why, then, do so many people stubbornly deny the existence of a Creator God? How scientific can the British professor of chemistry Peter Atkins’ statement really seem, that “spacetime generates its own dust in the process of its own self-assembly”? Why should “the random rearrangement of nothing”, according to the cosmologist Lawrence Krauss, create the elegant, finely tuned laws of physics that govern the universe?

In physics there is a fundamental scientific principle called the principle of causality: “Every event in time has its cause, which is independent of it and greater than it.” So if the universe had a beginning, it must necessarily owe its existence to a cause outside and beyond itself.

The ancient peoples (Assyrians, Babylonians, Egyptians) and the ancient Greek philosophers tried to get round this difficulty by claiming that the universe has no beginning and no end. It is eternal and the source of all things. So it is not subject to the principle of causality and owes its existence to itself (a belief that reigned until the middle of the 20th century).

But modern scientific observations have shaken this rotten belief.

In 1929 the famous astronomer Edwin Hubble, making observations with a telescope, found that the space between the galaxies is growing; that is, the universe is expanding.

In 1948 the Russian-born physicist George Gamow, analysing the experimental data, proposed a model of the origin of the universe from an initial point in which all energy and matter were concentrated.

In the 1960s Penzias and Wilson observed the trace left over from this initial point: the cosmic microwave background radiation.

The prophet Isaiah, writing by the Holy Spirit in the 8th century BC, says: “that stretcheth out the heavens as a curtain, and spreadeth them out as a tent to dwell in” (Isaiah 40:22).

The direct consequence of these scientific discoveries is the realisation that the universe is not infinite and eternal but has a beginning and a cause of its existence. And this cause that brought the whole universe into existence is not an impersonal force but has clear and definite characteristics:

Since He created time and space, He must be outside them, and Eternal!

Since He rules over matter and energy, He must be Almighty!

Since He brought free persons into existence, how much more must He Himself be a Person!

He from whom the world of mathematical ideas and of logic springs must be All-wise!

He who created and sustains life must be the eternally Living One!

Only the Bible speaks of creation out of nothing and, at the same time, of a God outside creation. In contrast with “in the beginning was chaos” – that is, something eternal, infinite and static – the Word of God declares: “For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him” (Colossians 1:16)

How, then, can atheism answer these facts? How can it explain the wisdom of the Word of God? And above all, what has it to offer in place of the love of God, who “…gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life”? (John 3:16)
