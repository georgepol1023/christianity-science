The insects cry out: mankind, you are leading the planet into ecological disaster (too)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

December 2017 · The “Christianity & Science” team

It sounds very strange that insects can cry out, warning and advising people. And yet the Bible urges us to observe the life of, for example, the ants: “The ants are a people not strong, yet they prepare their meat in the summer” (Proverbs 30:25).

Together with wasps and bees they make up the order Hymenoptera, and it is estimated that there are 22,000 species of ant, of which more than 12,500 have been classified. Ants form colonies ranging in size from a few dozen predators living in small natural cavities to highly organised societies that cover large areas and are made up of millions of members. Colonies are sometimes described as superorganisms, because the ants seem to behave as a single being, working together to support the colony. They thrive in almost every ecosystem. Their success in so many environments has been attributed to their social organisation and their ability to modify habitats, use resources and defend themselves. All colonies show a division of labour, communication between individuals and the ability to solve complex problems. These parallels with human societies have been a source of inspiration and a subject of study.

Insects in general make up the most numerous class in the whole animal kingdom, with at least a million recorded species (the real number is estimated to be many times that), and they make up two thirds of the earth’s biomass. Besides pollinating flowers and fruit-bearing plants, insects are food for many animals, birds, fish, reptiles and amphibians, while (some) as scavengers keep parasites in balance, cleaning the environment. Their existence and work are essential for life on earth. But on 18 October 2017 a paper was published that literally shook the scientific community

(1). According to it, in the last 25 years the total biomass of flying insects has fallen by three quarters. The findings are all the more significant because the research was carried out in protected nature reserves in Germany, where every measure had been taken to create a suitable environment. A professor at the University of Sussex in England stated that a horrific decline in the number of insects is taking place, and that if we lose the insects everything will collapse.

(2) The ecosystem is built so that one species depends closely on another. In a dramatic tone, scientists warn that humanity is heading very fast towards an “ecological Armageddon”, with very deep effects on human society. The causes of this disappearance remain shadowy, although the widespread use of pesticides is blamed, together with the conversion of wild forests into monocultures that cannot provide the environment needed to maintain biodiversity and the number of insects (and not only insects). But the basic cause is man’s independent course without God, defying the Creator and mocking, even now, the only Saviour, our Lord Jesus Christ. The prophetic word declares: “The earth mourneth and fadeth away, the world languisheth and fadeth away, the haughty people of the earth do languish. The earth also is defiled under the inhabitants thereof; because they have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, broken the everlasting covenant.” (Isaiah 24:4–5).

The all-good God is (still) speaking, through His Word and His church. He is calling man to return and live with Him for ever. He speaks in many ways, even through the insects… How good God is. Let us hear His voice and repent from the heart while there is still time. The invitation is personal. “Save yourselves from this untoward generation.” (Acts 2:40)

1. PLOS ONE journal, 18 October 2017

2. The Guardian: “Warning of ‘ecological Armageddon’ after dramatic plunge in insect numbers”, 18 October 2017
