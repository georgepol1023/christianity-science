Homeostasis and the immune system

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

April 2024 · The “Christianity & Science” team

As we close our series of articles on the wonder of the human body, we would like to shed a little more light on another property of living organisms which often goes unnoticed but is decisive for their survival: homeostasis.

Homeostasis is the ability of an organism to keep the conditions inside it relatively stable, whatever the conditions of the outside environment. This is achieved by a series of mechanisms that regulate body temperature, the acidity (pH) of the blood, the concentration of glucose in the blood, and so on.

To give one example: to maintain body temperature, there are special thermoreceptors (temperature sensors) in the skin that inform the brain of changes in the surrounding temperature, and the brain switches on the appropriate mechanisms. On a very hot day, for instance, the circulation changes so that more blood flows from the warm interior of the body to its outer surfaces. At the same time the sweat glands produce more sweat, which cools the skin as it evaporates. This lowers the temperature of the blood flowing in the dilated blood vessels, which then returns to the inside of the body, preventing the body temperature from rising. Mechanisms working the other way also keep body temperature steady on cold days.

Besides environmental factors, though, the smooth working of the body can be disturbed by outside invaders (viruses, bacteria) that cause disease. In these cases the body’s immune system goes into action. Its role is both to keep invading micro-organisms out and to defend the body by destroying them.

Very briefly: things like mucus, sebum, saliva and sweat help keep pathogenic micro-organisms from getting in. If an invader does manage to get in, mechanisms such as inflammation and fever are switched on, so that increased blood flow and a higher temperature create a hostile environment for the microbes. Everything we have mentioned so far makes up what are called the non-specific defence mechanisms, which give general protection against any invader.

Even more wonderful are the so-called specific defence mechanisms, which rely mainly on the action of various groups of lymphocytes. These mechanisms recognise the cause of the illness (the antigen) in a specialised way, leading to the production of specialised proteins – the antibodies we all know about. Each antibody is made to recognise and destroy one particular antigen. The action of each antibody against a particular antigen is so specific that it is compared to a key and a lock. Antibodies also create a “memory”: when the body comes into contact with the same antigen again, a multitude of antibodies is produced very quickly, neutralising the invader at once, often even before any symptoms of illness appear.

We believe that every honest reader should find it obvious that modern medicine’s investigation of all these mechanisms, and its effort to understand them, establish at least two conclusions:

  • these mechanisms contain a specified complexity that points to intelligent design, and
  • the investigation should go on to ask who is the Person who brought all this into being, and what relationship I personally can have with Him.

To any of our readers who wish to set out on this blessed search, we recommend two things to take with them: the name of Jesus Christ, and the Bible, which is the Word of God.
