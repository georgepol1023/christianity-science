The historical evidence for the Resurrection of Jesus Christ

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

May 2018 · The “Christianity & Science” team

The Resurrection of Jesus Christ, the foundation of Christianity, is an event with a completely consistent historical basis, which shows that the Resurrection of Christ is an event that undeniably took place within human history.

A strictly rationalist thinker might reasonably ask whether it is legitimate even to examine such a possibility, since an event like the Resurrection lies beyond the limits of reason. But such an attitude would be unscientific, because if science followed the rule “what we do not understand we do not even examine”, no progress would ever have been made in human knowledge. So asking whether such a matter of faith can have a historical basis is perfectly legitimate, because a consistent historical basis gives faith in the Resurrection an even firmer foundation. On the other hand, a prejudiced belief such as the rejection of Christ’s Resurrection, which lacks any historical basis, cannot stand and must be demolished.
So a rational approach to the Resurrection allows only two possibilities:

  • There was a resurrection
  • There was no resurrection

The question of the human soul rests on this simple statement. For an answer to this question settles whether life continues or not. It comes down to the question of whether the human soul has value or not. That is why it matters to examine the Resurrection.
If Christ’s Resurrection is not a real event, the whole building of the Christian faith collapses, as the apostle Paul says in the 15th chapter of his First Epistle to the Corinthians.

The first Christians based the Christian faith on Christ’s Resurrection, regarding it as an established fact and as the foundation of salvation: “if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved” (Romans 10:9)

The fulfilment of Christ’s work rested deliberately on His Resurrection, as He Himself declared. Indeed, when the Lord was asked to perform a sign, He pointed to His Resurrection as the most reliable of all the signs and miracles He had done! (see Matthew 12:38–40 and John 2:18–22)

From the four Gospels, which have been shown to be reliable historical sources (see earlier articles), we know more about the events surrounding the death and burial of Christ than we know about the death and burial of any other person in ancient history. That the tomb was empty was never disputed. Whether what happened was a resurrection or not, we shall examine. But the fact that the tomb was empty has never been disputed by anyone. The meaning of the Resurrection may be a theological question, but the fact of the Resurrection is a historical question.

Let us look at the historical facts:

1. Jesus was really dead

  • He was savagely scourged, beaten and made to wear a crown of thorns.
  • They “brought him” to Golgotha, because He was too exhausted to carry the cross Himself.
  • Crucifixion was a great torment. With all His wounds and inflammation He hung half-naked from the nails and, despite His exhaustion, had to push up with His feet in order to breathe.
  • Death does not take long to come in such conditions, and indeed it was a release.
  • The blood and water that came out when the spear pierced Him confirm medically that death had already taken place (death was not caused by the spear).
  • Even if He had not been dead, the spear, passing through the lungs and very probably the pericardium, would have killed Him at once.

2. The tomb

  • We know exactly who took charge of burying Jesus’ body.
  • We know the owner of the tomb.
  • We know where the tomb was.
  • The preparations for the burial are described in detail.

3. The burial:

  • Christ’s body is placed in the tomb and secured.
  • The grave clothes were wound very tightly round the body (the word “wound”, literally “bound”, is used) and stuck to it because of the aromatic substances, forming a solid mass. It was impossible not only for Him to get out of the grave clothes Himself, but also for someone else to unwrap the body. And even if He had not been dead, the pungent smell of the ointment would have suffocated Him.
  • The entrance to the tomb was covered by a great stone that needed several men to roll it.
  • The entrance to the tomb was sealed with the Roman seal. By sealing the tomb, the Roman guard certified that they had received a particular dead body. The seal was protected at all costs by the impeccably trained and strictly disciplined Roman soldiers, since on the one hand it represented the authority of the Roman empire, and on the other, if anyone else broke it, the guards faced the death penalty. In short, the tomb could not be broken into.

We will go on with the rest of the evidence which, as unshakeable logical proofs, can convince every honest student that CHRIST IS INDEED RISEN FROM THE DEAD!
