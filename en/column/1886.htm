Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

The empty tomb has cried out for centuries: Christ is risen!

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

May 2019 · The “Christianity & Science” team

The Resurrection of Jesus Christ, the foundation of the Christian faith, is an event that undeniably took place within human history. No event is as well documented historically as the death on the cross, the burial and the Resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ…

Asking whether such a matter of faith can have a historical basis is perfectly legitimate, because a consistent historical basis gives faith in the Resurrection an even firmer foundation. On the other hand, a prejudiced denial, such as rejecting the Resurrection from the outset, cannot stand and must be demolished.
So a rational approach to the Resurrection allows only two possibilities:

  • There was a resurrection
  • There was no resurrection

If Christ’s Resurrection is not a real event, the whole building of the Christian faith collapses, as the apostle Paul says in the 15th chapter of his First Epistle to the Corinthians.

The fulfilment of Christ’s work rested on His Resurrection, as He Himself declared (see Matthew 12:38–40 and John 2:18–22…).

From the four Gospels, which have been shown to be reliable historical sources, we know more about the events surrounding the death and burial of Christ than we know about the death and burial of any other person in ancient history. Whether what happened was a resurrection or not, we shall examine. But the fact that the tomb was empty has never been disputed by anyone. The meaning of the Resurrection may be a theological question, but the fact of the Resurrection is a historical question.

Let us look at the historical facts:

  1. Jesus was really dead when He was buried. Even His enemies confirm it! (Matthew 27:63)
  2. The tomb. Impossible to break into, sealed with the Roman seal and guarded.
  3. The tomb is empty! Where is the Lord’s body? The disciples did not have the power to steal the body, nor would they have given their lives for a lie. His enemies, by producing the body, would have silenced every claim that Jesus had risen from the dead.

We are led logically to the conclusion that there was a resurrection. The way it happened is miraculous, but the event is historical! The Resurrection was bodily. The disciples admit, with complete honesty and objectivity, that they themselves found it hard to be convinced that they were seeing the risen Christ in front of them, not as a spirit but with a body. And once they were assured of it, there was no room for any doubt in them that their Teacher had truly risen.

The risen Christ showed Himself “by many infallible proofs” for forty days and to more than five hundred people. He appeared to particular people, in particular places and at particular times, which are recorded in detail in the texts of the New Testament (Mark 16:9–14 and 1 Corinthians 15:6–8).

The Resurrection of Jesus Christ, as a real event, had and still has results:

  • It leads to the transformation of a person’s life through new birth and the baptism with the Holy Spirit. Death is undone as the consequence of sin, and the person is set free, receives eternal life and becomes a child of God. None of this could still be happening today if Christ had not risen.
  • It confirms the truth of all Christ’s words, since He Himself linked His trustworthiness with His power to rise from the dead. So since this was true and came to pass, everything else He said is true and is coming to pass too.

If Christ had not risen, the disciples would have felt disappointed and betrayed by a man who had persuaded them to leave everything and follow him, relying on a false hope. But we see the disciples transformed into fearless preachers of the Resurrection, so sure of it that they were willing even to die for this faith. And all those Christians through the centuries who have known Him, and who confess their personal experience with Jesus Christ, become true witnesses of His Resurrection.

So let us say with the apostle Peter:

“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, which according to his abundant mercy hath begotten us again unto a lively hope by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead, to an inheritance incorruptible, and undefiled, and that fadeth not away, reserved in heaven for you…” (1 Peter 1:3–4)

The Lord is risen and is coming to receive His church!

“And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.” (Revelation 22:17)

“He which testifieth these things saith, Surely I come quickly. Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus.”

(Revelation 22:20)

Published in the team's column in the newspaper “Christianity”, May 2019.
