Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

“But ask now the beasts, and they shall teach thee…” (Part 2)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

July 2017 · The “Christianity & Science” team

“…and the fowls of the air, and they shall tell thee: or speak to the earth, and it shall teach thee: and the fishes of the sea shall declare unto thee. Who knoweth not in all these that the hand of the LORD hath wrought this? In whose hand is the soul of every living thing, and the breath of all mankind.” (Job 12:7–10).

In our previous feature we wrote about the migration of various animals; here we would like to add one more wonderful feature of birds.

Many species of birds fly in a V formation on their migration. This aerodynamic formation saves energy for the birds flying at the back, as they make use of the whirls of air coming from the wingbeats of the birds in front. The birds on the “arms” of the V beat their wings in time so as to give more lift to those flying last, while the birds directly behind the leader beat their wings out of time so as to minimise the effect of the downwash created by the whirls from the “leader’s” wings. The members of the group take turns in the different positions of the formation so that they can rest.

The greatness of God is also seen in the following wonderful mechanisms of nature.

The way termites “cool” their nests is of great interest. Termite nests are known to be at the centre of a mound. Warm air rises through a network of air shafts near the surface. There the stale air spreads out through the porous sides, while at the same time clean, cool air is channelled down into an air chamber deep in the mound.

We could write much about bees too. We will confine ourselves to the wonderful geometry of the honeycomb, which is made of regular hexagons. Indeed, this shape gives the bee’s work more advantages than any other. This is because it ensures, in the best way, the greatest capacity with the smallest perimeter needed – that is, with the least material needed (wax). So the insect needs less time and less energy to build it. Although mathematically the circle is the shape that combines the greatest capacity with the smallest perimeter, such a shape would be unsuitable for covering a surface, because empty, unused space would be left between neighbouring circles, and the structure would not be very strong. But the hexagon leaves no gaps, and so combines saving space with strength, as the cells touch one another.

It is worth noting that mobile phone networks cover areas with their signal in a cellular way, using the hexagonal shape. In this way they achieve the greatest number of subscribers and cover large geographical areas while making the best use of the available radio spectrum.

It is also worth mentioning that to find the food they need, bees make in the course of a year a million flights by pollen gatherers and four million trips by nectar gatherers. Information about the exact location of food passes between bees through the smell of special substances called pheromones. But if the food is a long way off, the so-called “bee dance” takes place: a bee returning from a rich source of food will “dance” on a vertical surface, tracing a circle. On each round the bee cuts across the circle diagonally. The angle, measured from twelve o’clock, represents the angle the other bees must fly, with the sun as the reference point. As the bee dances, she also waggles her abdomen as she crosses the circle. The number of waggles tells the other bees how far from the hive the nectar is.

In the face of all this – just a small sample of the wonderful mechanisms of nature – we realise that the belief that all of it came about by itself, as a product of chance, collapses completely. The theory of evolution cannot explain how such systems could have arisen by gradual evolution.

“Because that which may be known of God is manifest in them; for God hath shewed it unto them. For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse.” (Romans 1:19–20)

The One of whom nature bears witness, we have the opportunity to know personally today. He is ready to cleanse us from our sins with the blood He shed on the cross of Golgotha, and to give us eternal life and fellowship with Him through the Holy Spirit, as the hour of His coming draws near.

“And that, knowing the time, that now it is high time to awake out of sleep: for now is our salvation nearer than when we believed. The night is far spent, the day is at hand: let us therefore cast off the works of darkness, and let us put on the armour of light.” (Romans 13:11–12)
