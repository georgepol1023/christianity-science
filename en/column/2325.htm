Scientific references in the Bible (Part 1)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

November 2024 · The “Christianity & Science” team

In various places in the Bible there are references to natural phenomena and, more generally, to subjects that fall within the scope of the sciences, with remarkable consistency with modern scientific knowledge. In this article we will look at some of the most important examples:

  • The number of the stars

“… I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven” (Genesis 22:17)

“As the host of heaven cannot be numbered, neither the sand of the sea measured: so will I multiply the seed of David my servant, and the Levites that minister unto me.” (Jeremiah 33:22)

The number of grains of sand on the whole Earth is estimated at between 1020 and 1024. The number of stars in the universe is estimated at 1021–1022. The message of these verses is that these are quantities so vast that they cannot be counted. But we notice that the orders of magnitude implied in these verses are also mathematically comparable with each other, even though this was not known then: there were no telescopes, and with the naked eye only about 3,000 stars could be seen in the night sky.

  • The expansion of the universe

“that stretcheth out the heavens as a curtain, and spreadeth them out as a tent to dwell in” (Isaiah 40:22)

In 1929 the famous astronomer Edwin Hubble, making observations with a telescope, found that the space between the galaxies is growing; that is, the universe is expanding. Albert Einstein had predicted this a few years earlier (1915–1917) through the equations of the General Theory of Relativity, but thought he had made a mistake. It is truly wonderful that the Bible speaks of “stretching out the heavens” in at least 10 different passages, in the books of Job, Psalms, Isaiah, Jeremiah and others.

  • The Earth is suspended, and it is round

The ancient Egyptians believed that the Earth is flat and held up by 4 pillars at the edges (and one in the middle for safety); the Indians believed that the Earth rests on a huge elephant standing on the back of an enormous turtle swimming in the sea; the Mongols believed that the Earth rests on the back of a frog, and whenever it jumps there is an earthquake; and the ancient Greeks believed that the Earth is held up by the Titan Atlas. In contrast to all this, the Bible declares that God:

“hangeth the earth upon nothing”! (Job 26:7, about 2000 BC) and “sitteth upon the circle of the earth” (Isaiah 40:22, 8th century BC)

  • The weight of the air

“For he looketh to the ends of the earth, and seeth under the whole heaven; to make the weight for the winds; and he weigheth the waters by measure.” – Job 28:24–25

The idea that air could have weight was unimaginable at that time. Even today it sounds strange, especially to people who know no physics or chemistry. Today we know that the air – that is, the atmosphere – is a mixture of chemical elements in gas form that have mass, and indeed it is the weight of the air that keeps the layer of the atmosphere around the Earth.

In our next article we will give some more examples of scientific references in the Bible. We must always bear in mind that the Bible is not a science textbook or a kind of encyclopaedia, so that we should demand strictly scientific language or descriptions in 21st-century terms. But we see that all these descriptions that fall within the scope of science are given in the Bible in simple, understandable language from the point of view of an ordinary observer, and at the same time with wonderful consistency with today’s scientific knowledge. After all, the purpose of the Bible is not to describe natural phenomena scientifically, but to bring people to the knowledge of the person of Jesus Christ, so that they may receive salvation and eternal life.
