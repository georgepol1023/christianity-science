BrainChart: an innovative tool – a multimeter for the brain

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

June 2022 · The “Christianity & Science” team

The human brain, although it weighs around 1.5 kilos and has an average volume of only 1,200 cubic centimetres, is the most complex system in the universe! It has about 200 billion nerve cells, connected to one another by synapses.

In the cerebral cortex alone there are 125 trillion synapses – as many as the stars in 1,500 galaxies! In the brain there are areas (centres) that give the commands for all the functions of the body, and areas for higher functions such as thought, memory and so on. The grey matter we have all heard of is the layer of brain tissue in which mental processes (thought, memory, judgement and so on) take place, and it is called that because it is grey in colour.

Could such an organ be the result of a long process of evolution over the ages?

About two months ago, the journal Nature published a research paper called BrainChart by an international team of researchers (including two Greek professors working abroad). It describes the development of a clinical tool that could in future be used like children’s growth charts, by which a paediatrician follows the normal development of their height and weight. The set of curves this team of neuroscientists has created, and publishes freely online, covers brain development from 15 days after conception to the age of 100.

As you can understand, it is an extremely laborious undertaking, with an enormous amount of information processing behind it. The scientists hope that, just as each child’s physical growth is followed against typical values that set the normal range, the same assessment can be made of brain development, to study and prevent mental disorders and related neurological diseases.

So far the study has been based on 125,000 brain MRI scans. Analysing them needed about 2,000,000 hours of computer time to process 1 petabyte (1015 bytes) of information, carried out by the University of Cambridge supercomputer cluster, which worked day and night to produce the results – so that, as the reports on it remark, this tool could be created for studying the development and functioning of the most perfect “electrochemical computer” there is: the human brain.

Returning, then, to our original question, we put two simple questions to your judgement:

If it is generally accepted that the brain is a device comparable to a computer (“perfect” and “electrochemical”), and far more complex than any that has yet been built, can it possibly have come about as the result of random processes? If studying the data about the brain needs the intensive work of a cluster of computers built by human intelligence, how much more must the object they are studying be the creation of a mind that designed and built an organ with unrivalled wisdom?

Dear readers, we thank the Lord because the truth cannot be hidden. Every step by which science advances resoundingly demolishes every atheistic belief, and reveals the One who created all things and is “upholding all things by the word of his power”: the Son and Word of God, Jesus Christ, “the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person”, who, “when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high” (Hebrews 1:3).
