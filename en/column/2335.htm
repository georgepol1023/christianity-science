Advice for teenagers (Part 1)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

December 2024 · The “Christianity & Science” team

The teenage and youth years are a very special period in a person’s life, when many biological and physical changes take place as he passes from childhood to adult life.

Physically, there is an explosion of hormones that brings drastic and sudden changes to a young person’s body. A young man grows taller, his voice deepens, his muscles grow stronger and in general he becomes a man. A young woman is prepared to be able to bear children and to sustain the new life that the Lord will give her.

Alongside these changes comes a young person’s urge to discover new things, to spend more time with friends and to become independent of the family: to learn, to love and to dare – in a word, to live…

The brain forms new connections at great speed and discards those it does not use. In this way it establishes new skills, settles outlooks, and becomes bound, in mind and body, to people and situations. We could say that it is the most productive and creative period of a person’s life.

The Word of God has very useful advice to give to young people, recognising how important this period of life is and what a decisive part it will play in their later development. It is no accident that the Lord used many young people powerfully and very fruitfully in His work. Joseph, Daniel, Samuel and most of the disciples and apostles were young when the Lord called them and worked with them.

In this article and the next we will look at some precious advice that the Bible gives to teenagers:

Be joyful:

The Preacher writes: “Rejoice, O young man, in thy youth; and let thy heart cheer thee in the days of thy youth” (Ecclesiastes 11:9). The Lord wants His children to be joyful and to live a full and abundant life. The apostle Paul reminds the Philippians: “Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice.” (Philippians 4:4).

But joy is not found in an adrenaline rush or in the passing pleasure of sin. It is the fruit of the Holy Spirit and the outcome of our obedience to His will. So the young person who lives enjoying the life that is in Christ, and who waits for the Redeemer, is joyful. And that is the Lord’s will for his life.

The Lord does not want you to live a miserable, monotonous life. He Himself said: “…I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.” (John 10:10)

Redeem your time:

Youth is a very productive period of life, and the enemy of the soul wants to fight against it by getting young people busy with pointless and useless things, wasting the precious treasure called time. The average Greek teenager spends about 4 hours a day in front of a screen, apart from the time spent on work for school or for a job. Proportionally, that means he wastes more than 50% of his free time.

The apostle Paul advises: “See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, redeeming the time, because the days are evil.” (Ephesians 5:15–16). The days are evil because they want to steal from a young person one of the most precious things he has: his time.

The Preacher also urges: “Remember now thy Creator in the days of thy youth, while the evil days come not…” (Ecclesiastes 12:1).

Young man, young woman, the only thing that belongs to you is today. The only thing you can manage is today. So give your whole heart to Christ today and dedicate your youth to Him. What could be more beautiful than to put the strength, the time and the energy of your youth at the service of Jesus Christ?
