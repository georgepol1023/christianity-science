Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

“We have also a more sure word of prophecy” (2 Peter 1:19)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

October 2021 · The “Christianity & Science” team

It is relatively easy for an honest person to see the truth and accuracy of the prophetic word of the Bible, even through scientific approaches such as probability theory. To make ourselves clearer, here is an example.

According to the prophecies of the Old Testament, the Messiah would come from the tribe of Judah (see, for example, Genesis 49:10). Since there were 12 tribes of Israel, the probability of this prediction being fulfilled is 1 in 12 (as a fraction: 1/12). The prophet Micah also says that the Messiah would be born in Bethlehem (Micah 5:2). Since there were about 100 villages and towns in the wider region of Judea, the probability of the prophecy coming true is 1 in 100 (as a fraction: 1/100).

According to the law of probability, the probability of both these prophecies being fulfilled together is equal to the product of the individual probabilities (1/12 × 1/100 = 1/1,200). That is, the probability that the Messiah would come from the tribe of Judah and be born in Bethlehem is 1 in 1,200. We could go on, calculating the probability of each individual Messianic prophecy being fulfilled and multiplying all the fractions together at the end.

Such a study was carried out by Henry Morris (1918–2006), who gave his students at a Christian college the task of calculating the individual probability of each of 20 Messianic prophecies of the Old Testament. In the end, after each piece of work had been checked for correctness, the final result was: 1 in 10175! And this for only 20 of the roughly 300 Messianic prophecies of the Old Testament.

Similar studies were carried out by Professor Peter W. Stoner (1888–1980), which were also checked for correctness by the American Scientific Affiliation. So it was calculated, for example, that the probability of 8 particular Messianic prophecies being fulfilled was 1 in 1017; of another 16 prophecies, 1 in 1045; and so on.

And yet the prophetic word was fulfilled completely in the person of Jesus Christ, even in details that were written hundreds or even thousands of years before His coming!

But the prophecies of the Bible go on, reaching to the last days in which we live. Chapter 13 of Revelation prophesies a cashless society – something unthinkable until a few decades ago – and transactions based on a number: χξς΄ = 666. Indeed, we see that today the barcode found on every product works on the basis of this number. If John had had to choose a number between, say, 600 and 700, we would say that with a good dose of luck he got it right, with a probability of 1 in 100. But since numbers have no beginning and no end, the probability of his having hit on the right number by chance is 1 in infinity (!) – that is, humanly impossible!

“no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation. For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost.” (2 Peter 1:20–21)

Today anyone can watch more prophecies moving towards their fulfilment in real time, just by reading the main stories in the day’s news. For example:

  • Fears of wars and unrest, infectious diseases and earthquakes (Matthew 24:6–8)
  • Pollution of the environment – climate change (Isaiah 24:4–6)
  • Financial crises and economic collapse (Revelation 18:11–19)
  • Fear and worldwide insecurity (1 Thessalonians 5:3, Luke 21:26)

And all this in the 21st century, when the progress of science and technology promised worldwide prosperity and peace. So the study of Scripture leaves no doubt that everything that has been prophesied will without fail be fulfilled with mathematical precision, and that signals only one thing:

“The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel.” (Mark 1:15)
