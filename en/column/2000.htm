Family time in the age of technology (Part 1)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

June 2020 · The “Christianity & Science” team

The Word of God tells us that God made man for a very glorious purpose: “And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness… So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them. And God blessed them.” (Genesis 1:26–28).

The first human beings could have communion with God, and reflect His glory and His holiness – virtues they would keep by knowing and obeying God. Prophetically, before the foundation of the world, God announces the plan of salvation for the creature He loved so much (John 3:16), which, despite the furious and cunning resistance of the devil and the fall of man, He will carry out in full. A basic element in salvation is the institution of marriage and the family, which God Himself established and blessed in Paradise.

Armed with the qualities and gifts his Creator has entrusted to him, man sets out on his journey through history. He discovers, creates, produces technology. At this point we should mention that technology, by a simple definition, is the use of scientific knowledge or skill to solve practical problems, together with the particular methods, materials and devices used to achieve this.

Very early in human history we see the art of music appear, and the technology of the stone (Genesis 4:17), bronze and iron ages (Genesis 4:22). In the last 100 years there has been an explosion of knowledge, and we have already reached the technology of the internet, computers and artificial intelligence. Advanced imaging methods let doctors see inside the human body, with much better diagnosis and treatment of disease as a result. Computers and the internet also present events and connect people all over the planet in real time.

Children are born into a world in which technology plays a vital role in society, education, healthcare and entertainment. From a very young age they are familiar with electronic devices. And according to a recent study in the USA, they spend 8 hours a day looking at electronic screens, starting from the age of 6. As for so-called family time, so necessary for healthy relationships to grow, even when the family is together, parents and children spend 90 minutes or more a day on electronic devices.

The age of technology is the period of human history in which people try to solve the problems that arise with even more technology… The existence of God is rejected, and quietly but very quickly a new religion is growing: the religion of science.

Events are moving very fast. Let us look at an example, and the Greek language will help us. In Greek the word “computer” should properly be ypologistiras, not ypologistis, the word that has wrongly become established. In the same way, “printer” should properly be ektypotiras, not ektypotis, which has also wrongly become established.

In Greek the ending -tiras (-tirio) denotes the OBJECT or machine that does a job, while the ending -tis denotes the PERSON who does it. So we have odostrotiras, the machine that surfaces roads (a steamroller); anemistiras, the machine that makes a breeze (a fan); vrastiras, the machine that boils water (a kettle); aporrofitiras, the machine that draws off cooking smoke and smells (an extractor hood); and so on.

By contrast, a thermastis was the man who fed the boilers on steamships (a stoker); a petalotis is the man who shoes horses (a farrier); a schediastis is the person who makes drawings in a technical office (a draughtsman), while a schediastirio is the object with which the drawings are made. And we use the word as an expression too, saying “he is a cold ypologistis” (a cold calculator) of someone who has thought out and calculated every step and every move precisely, and does nothing guided by chance or emotion. It is clear that we are talking about a person, not some Celeron or Pentium…

So the next time you sit down in front of a computer, take care that you do not, by spending your time without discernment, hand over to the machine the first place that belongs to you. “See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, redeeming the time, because the days are evil.” (Ephesians 5:15–16).

The Lord Jesus Christ IS COMING to receive His church.

The “Christianity & Science” team
