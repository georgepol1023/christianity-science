How the church is like the human body

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

June 2025 · The “Christianity & Science” team

“For as the body is one, and hath many members, and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body: so also is Christ.” (1 Corinthians 12:12).

In a single sentence the apostle Paul sums up the essence of the church, presenting it as many members which together make up one body.

Every body has a head, from which the brain gives each member the instructions it needs for the body to work smoothly and to respond properly in every situation. For the church, the head is Christ Himself (Colossians 1:18). Every member must be connected to Him and receive instructions from Him, through His Word and by the Holy Spirit, about how to live and act in every circumstance.

The members of a body are interdependent: if one member is missing or not working properly, the body cannot work to its full capacity, or may not be able to work at all. This is true even of the members that are smaller or seem the least important in the body.

So if someone has injured the little toe of one foot, the whole body feels the pain, and the weakness of this small member affects the walking and balance of the whole body.

And at the moment of injury the whole body springs into action to protect itself from falling and to give the injured member the care it needs straight away. The other members, although they were not hurt themselves, perceive the problem of another member and each, according to its role, takes care of its recovery.

“…that there should be no schism in the body; but that the members should have the same care one for another. And whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it; or one member be honoured, all the members rejoice with it.” (1 Corinthians 12:25–26).

Another important thing we see in the human body is that no member, however small or however minor it may seem, ever stops being useful and necessary. And especially when a member for some reason needs more care, the other members are not indifferent but work together to give it the attention or adornment it needs:

“Nay, much more those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are necessary: and those members of the body, which we think to be less honourable, upon these we bestow more abundant honour; and our uncomely parts have more abundant comeliness.” (1 Corinthians 12:22–23).

Finally, protecting the body from disease is vital. For this there are natural barriers (for example the skin and the mucous membranes), and also the immune system, which, if an infectious agent manages to get in, goes into action and, with specialised types of cells and substances, fights and neutralises the invader, so that the symptoms of the disease stop and health is restored. Indeed, besides the immune system, all the mechanisms that help to maintain and restore the stability of the body’s health carry out what is called homeostasis.

In the same way, the church as the body of Christ needs to be protected from spiritual dangers getting in (sin, false teaching, worldliness and so on) that can infect and harm it. The Lord has set the recognised workers and the ministries as “watchmen”, through whom any spiritual danger is pointed out so that it can be avoided; or, if it does manage to get in, the right steps are taken and the right measures applied to deal with it and to restore the purity and the smooth working of the church.

It is necessary, then, for us to realise that since the Lord has added us to His church, we must learn to work not alone but as members of one body, carrying out the function and the work that He has prepared for us, for the common good.

“Now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular.” (1 Corinthians 12:27).
