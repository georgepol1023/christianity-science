Beware of fake news

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

February 2022 · The “Christianity & Science” team

In the age of technology and worldwide connectivity in which we live, a huge volume of information circulates every day, mainly through the internet and social media, and we are constantly on the receiving end of it to a greater or lesser degree.

An important issue that has come up recently is fake news: false reports that spread misinformation, presenting events that are not true, in order to mislead as many people as possible so that some can gain by promoting or discrediting people, products, situations and so on.

The key features of fake news are how convincingly it is presented and how fast it spreads. Its creators invent a plausible story that selectively borrows certain real facts and dresses them up with fiction, creating impressions that become even more convincing when accompanied, as they often are, by photos and videos. Then, mainly through social networks, the false report spreads very easily, especially since in many cases it is presented as coming from a well-known news agency, or falsely cites scientific sources. Fake news is made to have wide popular appeal, and often shapes public opinion or even drives people, individually or en masse, into various actions.

How can we protect ourselves from false news? The experts are clear: check your sources. With every news item that comes to our attention we should be cautious and cross-check whether it is valid against other reliable sources.

In this article we want to draw our readers’ attention to the false news that concerns the human soul. This fake news has been circulating for centuries and convinces most people, with tragic results: “There is no soul; man is an animal”, “There is no life after death”, “There is no God; everything was created by evolution”, “You can be saved by your good works”, “God will not send anyone to hell”, and much more.

These days a (true) news item came to our attention about some recent findings on the Antikythera Mechanism, that astonishing ancient Greek invention that worked as an analogue computer and an instrument for astronomical observation. The researchers say that this mechanism reveals “a combination of astronomical knowledge, understanding of abstract mathematical concepts and high mechanical skill”1, and they note that “the genius of Archimedes seems to emerge more and more clearly from the distorted fragments”2.

Why, then, are similar conclusions not drawn from the study of the natural world? Does the structure and working of the universe, from the microscopic world to the galaxies, not reveal an all-wise mathematical mind and an almighty power that brought everything into existence and set it working? Why should the wisdom of God the Creator not emerge – not from distorted fragments, but from living beings and mechanisms of nature in full working order? Could it be that this knowledge is being dressed up in convincing, scientific-looking fiction? Could it be that we are falling victim to fake news?

Dear reader, it is time to look into the fake news that threatens your eternal future. It is time to decide to verify or reject what you hear around you by turning to the most reliable source of all, which is the Word of God. Jesus Christ calls you to believe not a theory but a lived experience: a personal revelation of Himself in your life. He is the One who loved you so much that for your sake He became man, was crucified for your sins and rose again for your justification. Don’t let yourself fall victim to fake news any longer! Take the matter of your soul’s salvation into your own hands!

“Thus saith the LORD, Stand ye in the ways, and see, and ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein, and ye shall find rest for your souls.” (Jeremiah 6:16)
