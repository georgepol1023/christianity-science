The origin of all things! | Part 1: The beginning of the universe and its astonishing fine-tuning

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

July 2026 · The “Christianity & Science” team

In a recent discussion published online under the title “The Origin of Everything”1, three distinguished scientists – the philosopher of science Stephen Meyer, the Oxford University mathematician John Lennox and the chemist James Tour – discuss one of humanity’s greatest questions: how did everything begin?

The discussion begins with two completely different approaches to reality. The well-known atheist biologist Richard Dawkins maintains that the universe we observe shows no purpose or design, only “blind, pitiless indifference” in natural processes. By contrast, the psalmist David writes: “The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork” (Psalm 19:1).

Which of these two positions fits better with the evidence we have today?

For many centuries the prevailing view was that the universe was eternal and unchanging. Such a universe needed no beginning, and so no Creator. Even Aristotle believed that the universe had always existed.

In the 20th century, however, this picture changed dramatically. Einstein’s equations, the work of Georges Lemaître and the astronomical observations of Edwin Hubble showed that the universe is expanding. The galaxies are moving away from one another, which suggests that in the past they were much closer together.

“that stretcheth out the heavens as a curtain, and spreadeth them out as a tent to dwell in”

(Isaiah 40:22)

If we follow this expansion backwards in time, we are led to an initial state in which matter, energy, space and time appear to have a common starting point. The discovery of the cosmic microwave background radiation in 1964 strengthened this picture even further, and the scientific community gradually came to accept that the universe did indeed have a beginning.

The question then becomes unavoidable: if time, space, matter and energy began to exist, what was the cause of that beginning? The scientists in the documentary point out that the cause cannot be part of the material universe itself, since the universe did not yet exist.

But the discussion does not stop there. The researchers also refer to what is called “fine-tuning” of the universe. The basic physical constants seem to have exactly the values needed for stars, planets, chemical elements and, in the end, life to form.

James Tour compares the situation to a control room full of hundreds of switches. If even one of them is moved ever so slightly, the result is ruined. In the same way, even tiny changes in the fundamental constants of nature would make life impossible.

It is also worth noting that the universe is not only understandable but can be described mathematically. The laws of nature are expressed in mathematical relationships, while biology itself rests on a four-letter “alphabet” of information in DNA. It is no wonder that the Gospel of John begins with the words: “In the beginning was the Word.” The Greek idea of the Word (Logos) is connected not only with speech but also with reason, intelligence and order.

In the next article we will look at an even deeper mystery: the origin of biological information, and whether natural processes can explain the appearance of life.
