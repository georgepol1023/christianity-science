Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

Real lessons from the temporary collapse of virtual life!

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

October 2021 · The “Christianity & Science” team

On Monday 4 October 2021, a fault in the system’s servers led to a temporary 7-hour outage of four of the most popular social media apps.

More than 3 billion people around the world had no access to social media, which for them are not simply ways of communicating; we might say they are a way of life. Some outlets described it as: “Seven hours… in the Middle Ages”, while other sources said it was “a foreshock and a warning from the future”1. How can a brief technological failure affect the lives of billions of people, most people wondered, along with losses of billions of euros?

But this episode brought to light some very basic and fundamental things that we, as people of God, are called to think over and be challenged by.

It took seven hours for us to understand how a single person can cause worldwide turmoil; to understand that in this “global technological village” in which we live, we depend directly on the services offered to us “free” by a small group of people.

This reminds us that at some point very soon in the future, ten leaders “…have one mind, and shall give their power and strength unto the beast.” (Revelation 17:13)

For a large part of the younger generation it was as if time had stopped – as if the electricity and the water had been cut off at the same time; as if there were no life outside the virtual one.

The technological failure exposed spiritual poverty.

It exposed the lack of real communication and joy: strangers in our own homes, having everything and in reality nothing.

For all these years many people thought they were in control of how they used these media, but reality showed that they are dependent on a technology platform and under its thumb. Away from the false, artificial profile of virtual reality, they returned to the depression and misery of the daily life they had neglected.

And as the Word of the Lord, which cannot lie, stresses: “While they promise them liberty, they themselves are the servants …: for of whom a man is overcome, of the same is he brought in bondage.” (2 Peter 2:19)

But we have good news too! For some, even if only a few, it was a chance to put the phone aside, and the false reality with it, and to live a real afternoon: to see the sunset they used to see only in photos; to eat dinner as a family, looking each other in the eye and listening to what was on each other’s hearts; to play with their children, away from the technological nanny that had looked after them all these years.

And most important of all, to invite the Lord Jesus Christ into their home and rebuild the broken-down family altar, whose fire had for years been put out under the waters of social isolation, in the name of a supposed social, virtual freedom: for the children to hear their father again, as head of the family, bringing prayer and supplication for them to God the Father; to have their mother at their bedside again, reading them stories from the Word of God; and at last to close their little eyes with the sound of the Word, and not with the images of the world!

So through this technological “blackout”, may we as children of God accept the call of the prophet Hosea, who more than two thousand years ago cried out by the Holy Spirit: “Come, and let us return unto the LORD: … and he shall come unto us as the rain, as the latter and former rain unto the earth.” (Hosea 6:1–3)
