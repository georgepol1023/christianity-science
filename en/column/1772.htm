Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

Implants with artificial intelligence against mental illness

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

February 2018 · The “Christianity & Science” team

“There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.” (Proverbs 14:12)

At the dawn of the new year, as we are one more year closer to the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, we see the events that make up the end-times scene around the world developing at dizzying speed. While voices calling for world government multiply, the atmosphere in the Middle East is tense, lawlessness is being legitimised and natural disasters are striking populations, in this feature we would like to focus on another piece of the end-times puzzle: the rapid development of technology, to the point where it intervenes in man’s very body and mind.

In earlier articles we have written at length about electronic implants. But from recent news[1] we learn of the new great leap that is about to be made in this field, as American researchers are developing the first brain implants that will have artificial intelligence, with the aim of the “automatic” treatment of mental illness. The whole venture is under the auspices of the American Pentagon, which has stated that it wants to use implants of this kind to treat soldiers and war veterans suffering from conditions such as depression, post-traumatic stress and obsessive-compulsive disorder. Trials in humans have already begun…

These innovative implants have special sensors that record neural activity, and “run” artificial intelligence algorithms that, when they judge that something is wrong, stimulate the brain with electrical pulses so as to change the person’s emotions and, as a result, his behaviour. In other words, these devices decide by themselves when to intervene in the working of the brain to bring it back to normal levels, using small-scale electric shocks, so making medication or any other kind of medical intervention unnecessary.

All this sounds very progressive and beneficial for humanity, and indeed we cannot deny the beneficial results of the development of technology, provided of course that it is properly managed. But it is obvious that a technology that aims to intervene in a person’s emotions and behaviour could make him the puppet of whoever programs the device – or of the device itself, which is able to decide on its own, since it has artificial intelligence. Indeed, several scientists commenting on this achievement have raised serious ethical dilemmas, since implants of this kind give real-time access to the human brain, with all that this implies for protecting the integrity of the person’s personality.

In effect, man is being asked to give up of his own will the most precious thing he has been given: the freedom of his own will.

The true God fully respects man’s free will. When the Lord Jesus Christ came to the man who had lain paralysed for 38 years beside the pool of Bethesda, He asked him: “Wilt thou be made whole?” (John 5:6). The Lord preached: “If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.” (Matthew 16:24).
He wants those who follow Him to do so of their own will and with full knowledge of the love of the truth. When some of His disciples were offended and went back, the Lord did not change His words, but asked His apostles too: “Will ye also go away?” (John 6:67)

“Then Simon Peter answered him, Lord, to whom shall we go? thou hast the words of eternal life. And we believe and are sure that thou art that Christ, the Son of the living God.” (John 6:68–69).

Shortly before the catching up of the church by her Bridegroom, the Lord Jesus Christ, blessed indeed are those who, by the power of the Holy Spirit, line up with the apostle Peter’s answer. Let us follow them, with all our will.

Published in the team's column in the newspaper “Christianity”, February 2018.
