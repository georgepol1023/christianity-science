A child’s first steps in life

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

April 2026 · The “Christianity & Science” team

1. The foundation is the Bible

The Word of God reminds us that bringing up a child begins with its very first steps. In the book of Proverbs of Solomon we read: “Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.” (Proverbs 22:6). The Bible regards the infant as a precious soul, shaped every day by its surroundings.

The apostle Paul, too, urges in the Epistle to the Ephesians: “bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord” (Ephesians 6:4). Love, gentleness, peace in the home, prayer, hymn-singing, the Word of Scripture – all of these are spiritual influences that are written deep into a child’s heart and mind from the first two or three years of life.

Today science confirms what the Word of God has taught for centuries: the surroundings of the early years largely determine the later course of a person’s life.

2. Scientific research and a stable family environment

One of the most important theories in developmental psychology is John Bowlby’s attachment theory. According to his studies, when an infant grows up with stability, tenderness and a prompt response to its needs, it develops “secure attachment”. It feels confident, safe, and that its parents and those close to it are emotionally available. Long-term research has shown that children with secure attachment show better control of their emotions, greater self-confidence and healthier social relationships in adult life.

The research of Edward Tronick (the “still face” experiment, 1975) showed that even a brief emotional withdrawal by a parent causes intense stress in an infant. A child is extremely sensitive to facial expressions, to tone of voice and to the emotional atmosphere. We can imagine, then, how much it is affected when it lives amid constant tension, anger or conflict.

Modern neuroscience, too – through Vincent Felitti’s research on “Adverse Childhood Experiences” (ACEs) – has shown that chronic family stress in the early years is linked to an increased risk of physical and mental health problems later on. By contrast, an environment of love and stability protects the brain and the body.

Science therefore reaches a clear conclusion: the early years are not simply a “preparation”; they are the foundation of a whole life.

3. Examples from the Bible

The Bible offers shining examples of children who grew up in a healthy spiritual environment.

Young Samuel, dedicated to God by his mother Hannah, grew up “before the LORD”. From childhood he learned to hear the voice of God, and later he became a prophet and judge of Israel.

Timothy was taught “the holy scriptures” “from a child” by his mother and his grandmother. His upbringing made him a fellow worker of the apostle Paul and a shepherd of the Church.

Even Jesus Christ Himself “increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour” in an environment of godliness, obedience and spiritual life. The home in Nazareth was a place of faith, of prayer and of keeping the law of God.

These examples show that the spiritual atmosphere of childhood is not of secondary importance: it shapes characters, missions and callings.

4. A word to parents

Parents hold in their hands not only the physical care of a child but also the shaping of its heart and mind. Peace in the home, respect between husband and wife, praying together, the Word of God, hugs and tenderness are not “details” – they are spiritual and psychological foundations.

When the Lord was about to give Samson to Manoah and his wife, the parents-to-be made one of the most beautiful prayers of entreaty:

“O my Lord, let the man of God which thou didst send come again unto us, and teach us what we shall do unto the child that shall be born.” (Judges 13:8)

Let us remember the Word of God: “bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord”. The love we sow in the early years will bear fruit throughout their lives. And as Scripture assures us, when a child is trained “in the way he should go”, that way will stay with him to the end.
