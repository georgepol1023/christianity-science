The water cycle

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

September 2017 · The “Christianity & Science” team

The most widespread inorganic chemical compound on the surface of the Earth is water. All known forms of life contain water and need it to survive. It is found in three states – liquid, solid (ice) and gas (water vapour) – and covers about 70% of the Earth’s surface. Yet only 2.5% of this amount is so-called “fresh water”, which, under the right conditions, is fit to drink.

For life to be sustained, besides the existence of water, a basic condition is its continual movement through the water cycle, or hydrological cycle, which describes the presence and circulation of water on Earth, including inside the Earth and in the atmosphere. The water cycle begins at the sea, where, driven by the heat of the sun, quantities of water pass into the gas phase as water vapour. The atmosphere is also supplied with water by evaporation from lakes, rivers and the soil, and by the transpiration of plants. The water vapour is then carried by rising air currents to the upper layers of the atmosphere. There, because of lower pressures and temperatures, part of the vapour condenses and clouds form. The droplets of water that make up the clouds collide and join together to form larger drops, and at some point they fall to the Earth’s surface as various kinds of precipitation (rain, snow, hail and so on). The water cycle is completed by the run-off phase, in which the water from precipitation returns to the oceans, either directly as rain falling on them or by flowing through various watercourses (rivers, for example) on the Earth’s surface or underground. Large quantities of water end up in lakes, which together with the rivers are among the “stores” of fresh water. At the same time, water that passes underground may either find its way back to the surface and form springs, or end up deeper in underground basins that store large quantities of water for long periods.


The water cycle may seem a relatively simple phenomenon in the light of modern knowledge. But in ancient times the movement of water on earth puzzled philosophers and naturalists. For example, Thales of Miletus (6th century BC) expresses the general belief of his time that rivers owe their existence to the fact that the inside of the earth must be full of cavities containing huge quantities of water. About two centuries later Aristotle put forward an early view of a “cyclical” movement of water, which evaporates through the heat of the sun and, when it cools, falls back to earth. But it remained unthinkable that rivers (which flow unceasingly) could exist solely because of rain (after all, it does not rain every day).


In the book of Ecclesiastes (10th century BC) the Bible says:


“All the rivers run into the sea; yet the sea is not full; unto the place from whence the rivers come, thither they return again.” (Ecclesiastes 1:7)


and in the even older book of Job (before the 15th century BC) it says:


“For he maketh small the drops of water: they pour down rain according to the vapour thereof: which the clouds do drop and distil upon man abundantly.” (Job 36:27–28).


So there is clearly a reference to a “cyclical” movement of water, which evaporates, is stored in the clouds and returns to earth “abundantly” – that is, enough to supply the continual flow of the rivers. This also explains the statement in Ecclesiastes that the rivers “return”, that is, they are replenished by a “cyclical” movement of water.


Only in the 17th century AD did Pierre Perrault solve a scientific puzzle of centuries, by measuring the rainfall and the flow of the river Seine in France and finding that the amount of precipitation is six times greater than what flows in the river – that is, more than enough to supply the river and the nearby watercourses.


So once again the Bible proves to be true and given by inspiration of God. But it also speaks to us of the living water, which is the Holy Spirit and the Word of God, and is the only water that can completely quench the thirst – not of this material body, but of the human soul:


“If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water. (But this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive…)” (John 7:37–39)
Every person who repents of his sins and looks in faith to the sacrifice of Jesus Christ on the cross and to His resurrection comes to the source of this spiritual water:


“I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end. I will give unto him that is athirst of the fountain of the water of life freely.” (Revelation 21:6)
