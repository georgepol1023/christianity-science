Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

The effects of alcohol, and the freedom God gives!

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

May 2024 · The “Christianity & Science” team

Alcohol (ethyl alcohol, or ethanol) is the basic substance in the wine we all know and in all the various alcoholic drinks (beer, for example). Scientific research shows that in adults moderate drinking can contribute to the good health of the heart and circulation, and can give the body a feeling of relaxation and well-being. In ancient times, too, people used wine as a medicine for various illnesses, to disinfect wounds, to make drinking water safe, and so on.

In the Psalms we read that one of the gifts of God’s creation to man is “wine that maketh glad the heart of man” (Psalm 104:15).

The good Samaritan, to care for the injured man he met, “bound up his wounds, pouring in oil and wine” (Luke 10:34).

The apostle Paul advised Timothy to “use a little wine for thy stomach’s sake and thine often infirmities” (1 Timothy 5:23), and Jesus Christ Himself blessed and turned water into wine at the wedding in Cana of Galilee (John chapter 2).

But when drinking goes beyond certain limits, instead of doing good it has harmful effects on the body, and drinking large amounts brings a person into a state of drunkenness, leading in the end to addiction and dependence.

What happens, then, when alcohol enters the body?

Alcohol first reaches the stomach, where a small amount is absorbed, while the rest passes unchanged into the blood and spreads throughout the body. From there the liver takes over breaking it down, so that it can be passed out in the urine. The liver first tries to turn the alcohol into acetaldehyde, and then into carbon dioxide and water. But acetaldehyde is a highly toxic substance and can cause inflammation which, over a long time, can lead to cirrhosis of the liver.

In the brain the acetaldehyde produced acts on the neurotransmitters in a similar way to drugs and to nicotine. That is, a person feels satisfaction from the breaking down of alcohol into acetaldehyde, and that is where dependence begins. But the process that takes place in the brain as alcohol is broken down suppresses the working of areas of the prefrontal lobe, which is responsible for higher mental functions, decision-making and holding back inappropriate behaviour. As a result, reasoning is disturbed and inhibitions are lifted.

The Word of God describes very vividly the behaviour of a person who gets drunk and is dependent on alcohol:

“Who hath woe? who hath sorrow? who hath contentions? who hath babbling? who hath wounds without cause? who hath redness of eyes?… Thine eyes shall behold strange women, and thine heart shall utter perverse things. Yea, thou shalt be as he that lieth down in the midst of the sea, or as he that lieth upon the top of a mast. They have stricken me, shalt thou say, and I was not sick; they have beaten me, and I felt it not: when shall I awake? I will seek it yet again.” (Proverbs 23:29–35).

Freedom from dependence

In these words the Bible gives a very accurate picture of a person who has become an alcoholic, that is, dependent on alcohol.

Getting free is considered very difficult and slow, and it has two sides: physical and psychological recovery. Teams of doctors, psychologists, social workers and others try both to help the alcoholic come off alcohol physically and to help him face the problems of his life and daily routine that drive him to seek a way out in drink. So we understand what a complex problem it is, and how hard it is for these human methods to succeed.

On the other hand, there is a power that can set a person free in a moment from such destructive passions. That is the power of God. The apostle Paul writes:

“But I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind, and bringing me into captivity to the law of sin which is in my members. O wretched man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death? I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord.” (Romans 7:23–25).

For God’s freeing intervention to come into a person’s life, he must come into contact with God. And that contact comes about when a person believes that Jesus Christ is the Son of God – God who became flesh and was crucified for the sins of every person, but rose again, and now lives, and visits and sets free anyone who calls on Him.

Then a person lives in the fullness of God’s presence, so that he needs no substance to feel joy, nor does he need to get drunk to fill his soul; instead the Holy Spirit makes him overflow with heavenly happiness:

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit” (Ephesians 5:18).
