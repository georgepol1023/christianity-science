The Flood (Part 2)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

November 2023 · The “Christianity & Science” team

“… By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house; by the which he condemned the world, and became heir of the righteousness which is by faith.” (Hebrews 11:7)

In this feature we will briefly set out the facts about the building of the ark.

First we should make clear that Noah did not build a vessel shaped like a ship (with a keel, bow, stern and so on), because he was not setting out to build a craft meant for travelling. He built an ark (in Greek, kivotos: a chest or box) – that is, an emergency rescue vessel. So the various pictures that show the ark as a ship do not match reality.

It was built of gopher wood, which some think was cedar; it is more likely, though, that it was cypress, a wood that does not rot or get worm, and was the material ships were built of in ancient times. God also told Noah to coat the ark inside and out with pitch, so that water could not get in.

DIMENSIONS AND CAPACITY

According to the description in the 6th chapter of Genesis, the ark was a truly enormous structure with the following dimensions:

  • Length: 300 cubits = 135 metres
  • Breadth: 50 cubits = 22.5 metres
  • Height: 30 cubits = 13.5 metres

(the cubit is the length of a man’s forearm from the tip of the fingers to the elbow, reckoned at about 45–46 centimetres)

It was, then, a structure longer than a football pitch and taller than a three-storey building.

The proportions of its dimensions – length 6 times the breadth and 10 times the height – gave it astonishing stability at sea, and it was practically impossible for big waves or strong winds to capsize it. It is even calculated that it was more stable than modern ships, which are built sacrificing a small degree of stability to reach higher speeds.

The ark’s capacity was roughly 40,000 cubic metres, which corresponds to about 570 large railway freight wagons. The ark also had three decks, and its total floor area was at least 9,100 square metres.

“Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he.”

With this verse the account of the 6th chapter of Genesis ends, stressing the importance of Noah’s complete obedience to the words of God, to the detailed instructions He gave him. He did not propose changes to the design, putting forward his own opinion or any other excuse. After all, he was neither a shipbuilder nor an engineer, to know how such a structure should be built to face an event that had never happened in the world before. He received the voice of God in simple faith, and he could hear it clearly because he and his family kept themselves holy and pure in a world of sin and corruption all around him.

The message of the Word of God is clear, as all the signs point to the coming of the Lord, an event that has also never happened before: it is urgently necessary for the church of Christ, as another ark, to be built exactly according to the instructions of Scripture, without any alteration, addition or omission, sealed with “pitch” against the “waters” of the world, so that it can be a safe vessel of salvation for its members.
