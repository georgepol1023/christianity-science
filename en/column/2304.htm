Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

School bullying (Part 2)

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

July 2024 · The “Christianity & Science” team

“Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us.” (Romans 8:37)

In our previous feature we briefly set out the main aspects of school bullying: the systematic use of physical, verbal or psychological violence against people who are in a weaker position because of some difference in their physical condition, appearance, origin, character, performance and so on. There is particular concern recently because, although bullying is not a new phenomenon, it is on the rise, and at ever younger school ages.

Dealing with such situations first requires us to look back at the causes behind them, which we can only touch on in a newspaper column. The title of a recent article in Kathimerini (16 October 2023) is telling: “The gang has rules that the family and the school are unable to set”. In a few words it aptly states the need for rules within healthy relationships in the two fundamental institutions of society, the family and the school. Many other studies, too, point to the need to strengthen relationships between parents and children, and to the value of a stable family environment: the sense of security, love and acceptance in which a child grows up minimises the risk that he will seek recognition of his worth, and confirmation of his standing, through violent behaviour. All this is equally helpful for the children who are victims of bullying, so that they find a safe refuge, their traumatic experiences are dealt with, and they are not driven to desperate acts.

We cannot fail to point out that such findings lead straight to the framework that the Word of God was the first to set, as a great many passages in the Old and New Testaments speak of a young person’s need to receive guidance within healthy discipline in the family. In this article, however, we want to focus on how bullying is healed, on the side of both the bully and the victim, through the Word of God.

At the end of the previous article we already pointed out what is constantly proclaimed in all the articles of this newspaper and on our radio station: that Jesus Christ can transform a person and make him a “new creature”:

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.” (2 Corinthians 5:17)

So if you are a “bully”, desperately looking for the thing that will give you healthy recognition and worth, the time has come to repent of the pain you have caused others. And then something wonderful will happen: the Prince of Peace, Jesus Christ, will visit you and fill every emptiness in your soul with His Word and His Spirit.

On the other hand, if you are a “victim”, looking for the “weapon” to face the situations that hurt you, learn from the attitude of Joseph (Genesis chapters 37 and 39). Joseph suffered one of the worst kinds of bullying, since the bullies were his own brothers. Because he was different from them, following God’s will in his life, they not only despised and mocked him but threatened him with physical harm, and in the end sold him as a slave. Later, when he had prospered and got back on his feet in a foreign land, he received a new blow from his master’s wife, because he did not give in to her advances, and he ended up in prison.

How did Joseph endure? What kept him from despairing, or from taking his own life, in the face of these injustices and dead ends?

“And the LORD was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man” (Genesis 39:2)

“But the LORD was with Joseph, and shewed him mercy” (Genesis 39:21)

This is exactly what upheld Joseph: God’s presence showed itself in practice in his life, giving him favour among the people he was with. But how did Joseph come to be treated in this way by God?

Very simply: the first time, because he obeyed his father’s wish to go and find his brothers in the distant pastures and take them supplies, in spite of the way they treated him. And the second time, faced with the immoral advances of a woman, he chose to keep his integrity!

So if you are a victim of bullying, remember Joseph: do good to those who hurt you when you see their need, and keep your integrity in the face of the temptations of sin. Then you will see the grace of God defending you, and nothing will be able to daunt you!
