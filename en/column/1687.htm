Our column in the newspaper “Christianity”

Creation shows the power of the Creator

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

May 2017 · The “Christianity & Science” team

Looking simply at the nature around us, and at the whole universe, especially with the help of modern scientific knowledge, an honest person does not hesitate to recognise that there is a Creator of all this.

“For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse” (Romans 1:20)

What is more, all the structures and phenomena from the microcosm to the macrocosm, which are governed by mathematical laws and contribute to the existence of life, show that this Creator has unique qualities and a good character.

In this feature we will look at phenomena that show the power of God – knowledge that will help us realise, as ordinary people but especially as faithful Christians, how great this God is who loved us and invites us to know Him.

A common phenomenon that has inspired awe from ancient times until today is the storm. To give one example, an average storm pours onto the Earth’s surface a quantity of water roughly equal to what flows over Niagara Falls in 6 minutes – that is, 660,000 cubic metres of water! The same average storm also releases, through its lightning, energy of about 10,000,000 kilowatt-hours! Even more astonishing is that about 16 million such storms happen every year across our planet!

“Who hath divided a watercourse for the overflowing of waters, or a way for the lightning of thunder” (Job 38:25)

And if such phenomena, which take place on our own planet, make us feel small and powerless before them, how much more the phenomena and the sizes in the vast universe.

The energy produced by the Sun is released at the terrifying rate of 5,000,000 tonnes of matter a second! In 1 second the Sun gives out more energy than humanity has produced from the beginning of its history until today! And the Earth receives only one billionth of that energy!

As for the scale of sizes, more than 1,000,000 Earths would fit inside the Sun. And yet our Sun is a medium-sized star. Many stars in our galaxy outshine it. For example, Arcturus is 100 times brighter than the Sun and has a radius 20 times greater. But even it looks small compared with the supergiant Betelgeuse, which has a radius 600 times greater than our Sun’s and is 16,000 times brighter. And yet not even Betelgeuse is the largest star in our galaxy. Several red supergiants in the Milky Way are larger still, some with a radius 1,500 times greater than our Sun’s. And all this only in our own galaxy, which contains about 100 billion stars and is one of the roughly 100 billion galaxies thought to exist in the universe.

“Lift up your eyes on high, and behold who hath created these things, that bringeth out their host by number: he calleth them all by names by the greatness of his might, for that he is strong in power; not one faileth.” (Isaiah 40:26)

All these unimaginable sizes, the huge amounts of energy and many other features we could mention, reasonably lead to the question: how wise and how powerful is the One who tames these forces so that the universe works in such a wonderful way? And at the same time, how small and powerless is man before all this?

God, then, is truly the Almighty! But at the same time He is the One who laid aside His power and glory and humbled Himself, taking human form, to be near His creature. He died as a man on the cross to pay for our sins, and rose again to give eternal life to whoever repents and turns to Him:

“If thou return to the Almighty, thou shalt be built up, thou shalt put away iniquity far from thy tabernacles. […] Yea, the Almighty shall be thy defence […] For then shalt thou have thy delight in the Almighty, and shalt lift up thy face unto God. Thou shalt make thy prayer unto him, and he shall hear thee…” (Job 22:23–27)
