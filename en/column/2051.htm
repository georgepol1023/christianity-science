Does atheism have answers? (Part 6) The problem of morality

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

April 2021 · The “Christianity & Science” team

By definition, atheism is a philosophy without hope. It offers no answers to any of the basic questions of life. It cannot explain either the creation of the universe or the appearance of life. That is why the Word of God links atheism with folly in many places: “The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God.” Psalm 14:1

But there is another basic point on which atheism fails completely: explaining the moral dimension of man. It is commonly accepted that there is a worldwide, universal sense of what is good and what is evil. We would all react if we saw a child being brutally abused by someone older, and we would want justice to be done. Or we would feel pain for an elderly couple thrown out onto the street by their relatives so as not to be a financial drain.

But what meaning do ideas like justice, compassion, philotimo and care for others have in a universe governed by the theory of evolution? If we are simply the product of random processes, then the only law we should see around us would be the survival of the strongest. The big fish eats the small one, and the weakest must be eliminated so as not to hold back the further evolution of the species. How could we call the Holocaust wrong from the standpoint of the philosophy of atheism? The elite of that time decided to speed up the evolution of the species by getting rid of all those people who, in their view, were a brake on the progress of the Aryan race. And yet worldwide surveys show that everyone today condemns it. On what moral law do they do so? And if there are moral laws, should I not reasonably ask whether there must also be a corresponding Lawgiver who is eternal, timeless and unchanging?

In the animal kingdom there is no sense of good and evil. A lion is not called evil because it has killed more antelopes than another. It is simply driven by the instinct to survive and to preserve its genes by protecting its pride. But with human beings it is not the same. Man has the breath of God within him. He has a conscience, which convicts him when he tells lies, makes him feel guilty when he cheats his neighbour, and stirs a desire for justice when he sees injustice. The apostle Paul says in the Epistle to the Romans: “which shew the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience also bearing witness, and their thoughts the mean while accusing or else excusing one another” Romans 2:15.

But when a person moves away from the love of God and sears the voice of his conscience, he loses these sensitivities. “Man that is in honour, and understandeth not, is like the beasts that perish.” Psalm 49:20

So the problem is not a lack of evidence for God. It is not the absence of proof that leads a person to embrace the philosophy of atheism. It is simply that he does not want any higher authority in his life to tell him what is right and what is wrong.

“And this is the condemnation, that light is come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil.” John 3:19

God, however, did not become a cold lawgiver who sternly watches human pain and anguish. He became part of that pain, sending His beloved Son to go up onto the cross. And if “he spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?” Romans 8:32

Where does your heart stand today before this greatness of love?
