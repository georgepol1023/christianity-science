The deception of “probably”

Translated from the Greek original in the newspaper “Christianity”. Read it in Greek

July 2022 · The “Christianity & Science” team

At a time when information is abundant and news comes thick and fast, the various media need to draw the attention of readers or listeners to their own posts and publications, getting particular messages across. One of the ways they do this is by giving articles headlines that either promise much more than they actually describe, or present as true something that is at best uncertain.

In internet jargon this is called “clickbait”: bait meant to win the reader’s attention by using misleading headlines and captions.

In recent news we read:

  1. The discovery of the century? Palaeontologists in the USA find a fragment of the asteroid that wiped out the dinosaurs. 1

But a little further down the article we read: “of the asteroid that probably fell to Earth” and “Palaeontologists in the USA may have been led to an incredible, world-changing discovery.”

  1. Here is the nearest “new Earth” 2

And yet just below, describing an artist’s impression of the planet, they tell us: “This is what the exoplanet Kepler-1649c may look like, according to NASA…”

  1. Homo bodoensis: the new human ancestor who lived in Africa 500,000 years ago. 3

And further on in the article we read: “Homo bodoensis, which is probably a direct ancestor of modern humans…”

So we see a language of “probably” in the body of the articles, in contrast with the grandiose headlines that want to make an impression and get messages across to readers – messages that are, more often than not, against the truths of the Bible. The apostle Paul warns us about this spirit: “And this I say, lest any man should beguile you with enticing words.” (Colossians 2:4) and “O Timothy, keep that which is committed to thy trust, avoiding profane and vain babblings, and oppositions of science falsely so called…” (1 Timothy 6:20)

In complete contrast, the Word of the Lord, which cannot lie, does not speak in probabilities but gives certainties. In the book of Proverbs (22:20–21) it tells us:

“Have not I written to thee excellent things in counsels and knowledge, that I might make thee know the certainty of the words of truth; that thou mightest answer the words of truth to them that send unto thee?” The believer does not follow “cunningly devised fables” but is an eyewitness of the Lord’s majesty (2 Peter 1:16).

Today Christ does not speak in probabilities about your salvation. He does not say: if you follow Me you increase your chances of being saved; you may give your heart to Me and still be lost in the end… But: “That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.” (Romans 10:9)

So we invite you out of the realm of “probably” into the certainty of salvation!
